Shantou University's Research Office announced the university's internal recommendation arrangements for the Ministry of Science and Technology's 2013 Innovation Talent Promotion Program on June 8, 2013. The school notice addressed units and faculty members and focused on three recommendation categories: young and middle-aged science-and-technology innovation leaders, innovation teams in key fields, and innovation-talent cultivation demonstration bases.

Launched in 2011, the Innovation Talent Promotion Program was designed to connect research projects, innovation bases, and talent programs in order to develop high-level science and technology professionals, innovation teams, and technology entrepreneurs. The implementation plan included with the notice set 2020 targets of establishing 100 scientist studios, supporting 3,000 young and middle-aged science-and-technology innovation leaders, supporting 1,000 science-and-technology entrepreneurs each year, building 500 innovation teams in key fields, and establishing 300 innovation-talent cultivation demonstration bases.

### University Recommendation Categories

| Category | Main publicly stated conditions |
| --- | --- |
| Young and middle-aged science-and-technology innovation leaders | Applicants should generally hold a doctoral degree or an associate-senior or higher title and be under 45. Priority was given to leaders of ongoing major research projects at the national, departmental, or local level. Candidates were also expected to have high-level innovation results, strong development potential, leadership ability, and team-organization skills. |
| Innovation teams in key fields | Teams were to be selected primarily from those undertaking major national research projects, national key projects, or major construction projects. The team leader had to meet the basic conditions for a young and middle-aged science-and-technology innovation leader, except for the age requirement, and be no older than 50. A core team was to have at least 5 and no more than 15 members. |
| Innovation-talent cultivation demonstration bases | Recommendations were to focus on national-level science parks, higher-education institutions, and research institutions that were active in reform and had achieved notable results in innovating systems, mechanisms, and policy environments for talent cultivation and use. |

For an overseas-recruited candidate in the young and middle-aged category, the university notice stated that people already selected for the Thousand Talents Program were outside the scope of this recommendation round. Other overseas returnees had to have worked in China for at least two years and, for the following three years, work in China for at least nine months each year. Units supporting recommended teams were also expected to submit team-building and talent-development plans and commit to providing necessary working conditions.

### Additional Conditions in the Ministry Attachment

The Ministry of Science and Technology recommendation document included with the notice also listed a science-and-technology innovation and entrepreneurship talent category and supplied four sample recommendation forms. The forms covered young and middle-aged science-and-technology innovation leaders, science-and-technology innovation and entrepreneurship talents, innovation teams in key fields, and innovation-talent cultivation demonstration bases. Because the university notice itself identified three internal categories, the entrepreneurship conditions below are presented as additional information from the attachment.

A science-and-technology innovation and entrepreneurship candidate was to be a major founder of an enterprise, hold at least 20% of its shares, have at least a bachelor's degree, and demonstrate strong innovation, entrepreneurship, market-development, and business-management abilities. The enterprise had to be registered and lawfully operated in mainland China, have been established for no more than eight years, possess core technology or independent intellectual property, and offer products or services with strong market potential and competitiveness. Major founders of enterprises undertaking projects under the Innovation Fund for Technology-Based Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises, as well as leaders of science-and-technology correspondent projects, could receive priority.

The ministry attachment further specified that young and middle-aged candidates should generally be no older than 45; it used a birth date after January 1, 1968, as an example, and allowed some relaxation of the education requirement for enterprise science-and-technology talent. Key-field team leaders were generally to be no older than 50, with 5 to 15 core members, and teams could collaborate across units. A demonstration-base applicant could be a science park, a higher-education institution and its internal teaching or research organization, a research institution, or an enterprise research and development organization. It was expected to have substantial science and technology resources, innovation capacity, talent-development mechanisms, and a system for opening and sharing resources. The attachment's general requirements also called for recommendations to favor frontline, enterprise, and young science-and-technology talent; among local recommendations, enterprise science-and-technology talent and candidates under 35 were to account for at least one-third.

### Recommendation Channels

The ministry attachment stated that young and middle-aged science-and-technology innovation leaders, innovation teams in key fields, and innovation-talent cultivation demonstration bases were mainly to be recommended by relevant departments, local science-and-technology administrations, and certain federations, associations, and academic societies. Science-and-technology innovation and entrepreneurship talents were mainly to be recommended by local science-and-technology administrations and certain federations and associations. Innovation-talent cultivation demonstration bases selected in 2012 could recommend young and middle-aged science-and-technology innovation leaders and science-and-technology innovation and entrepreneurship talents again within the quota. Recommendations for scientist studios were to be arranged separately. The specific quotas were set out in the attached allocation tables.

The overseas-recruited-candidate requirement differs between the two source levels: the Ministry document said that candidates had to have worked in China for at least two years and guarantee at least nine months of work in China each year for the following five years. Both versions are retained here rather than being silently reconciled.

### Process and Deadlines

Faculty members interested in applying had to email the electronic version of the recommendation book to `yxhu@stu.edu.cn` by **5 p.m. on June 19**. The Research Office would organize expert review based on the number of applications and recommend selected candidates. Only candidates recommended by the university could proceed to formal online submission.

The Ministry attachment stated that the program used the National Science and Technology Program Application Center (<http://program.most.gov.cn>) for unified online submission and recommendation. Recommendation units were to organize an expert preliminary review and make a formal recommendation after an internal announcement at the host unit produced no objection. Host and recommending units had to review the materials and confirm each submission in the system according to their authority. Each candidate could apply for only one task and could be recommended through only one channel.

Formal materials had to be reviewed and stamped by the host and recommending units, prepared in three copies, and accompanied by a video-material disc prepared according to the attachment. They were to be sent to the Ministry of Science and Technology in the form of an official document from the recommending unit and mailed or delivered to the Science and Technology Talent Center by July 15, 2013. This was the ministry-level deadline for formal recommendation materials, distinct from the university's June 19 deadline for internal electronic materials.

### Form and Material Guidance

The form-filling guidance required applicants to select a technical field from nine options: agriculture, energy, information, resources, environment, population and health, materials, advanced manufacturing, and other. Basic disciplines could be placed under “other.” The discipline field was to be completed to the third level under the national standard GB/T 13745-2009. The forms also requested a self-evaluation, an explanation of the relationship between the host unit's development needs and the candidate, support and guarantee measures from the host unit, and the recommending unit's opinion.

The ministry attachment requested evidence for host projects, papers, results, and citations. Entrepreneurship candidates were also asked to prepare evidence of core technology or intellectual property, as well as key parts of enterprise annual-inspection materials, audit reports, financial statements, and articles of association. Demonstration bases were to provide their most representative and valuable materials. Because recommendation forms and supporting documents may contain personal and institutional application data, this article does not reproduce them; the original OA notice remains the source for the templates and detailed requirements.
