The 156th Engineering Research Forum of Shantou University's School of Engineering will be held at **10:00 a.m. on June 4** in ACC Meeting Room 5. Professor Qingfu Zhang of the University of Essex, UK, will present “Multiobjective Evolutionary Computation.” Graduate students in the Department of Electronic Engineering and related faculty members are invited to attend, as are other faculty members and students interested in the topic.

## Topics of Discussion

The presentation will introduce optimization problems with multiple conflicting objectives, the Pareto-optimal solution set in multiobjective optimization and the use of evolutionary algorithms. Professor Zhang will focus on two multiobjective evolutionary algorithms, MOEA/D and RM-MEDA. MOEA/D decomposes a multiobjective problem into a number of single-objective or simple multiobjective subproblems and solves them collaboratively. RM-MEDA uses the regularity of Pareto-optimal solutions in the search space to model their distribution and generate new solutions. The talk will also outline possible research issues in multiobjective evolutionary computation.

## About the Speaker

Qingfu Zhang is a professor in the School of Computer Science and Electronic Engineering at the University of Essex and a Changjiang Visiting Chair Professor at Xidian University, China. From 1994 to 2000, he worked with the National Laboratory of Parallel Processing and Computing at the National University of Defense Technology, Hong Kong Polytechnic University, the German National Research Centre for Information Technology, now the Fraunhofer-Gesellschaft, and the University of Manchester Institute of Science and Technology.

His research interests include evolutionary computation, optimization, neural networks, data analysis and their applications. He holds two patents and has published numerous research papers. He is an associate editor of IEEE journals in evolutionary computation and systems, man and cybernetics, and serves on the editorial boards of other international journals. MOEA/D, a multiobjective optimization algorithm developed by his group, won the Unconstrained Multiobjective Optimization Algorithm Competition at the 2009 Congress on Evolutionary Computation and received the 2010 IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation Outstanding Paper Award.
