It is reported that the Academic Affairs Office has announced the schedule for Spring 2014 undergraduate course registration and textbook ordering, with registration to be conducted in five phases.

| Phase | Time |
|-------|------|
| Initial Registration | Dec. 20, 2013 09:00 – Dec. 22 23:00 |
| Re-selection | Dec. 27 12:00 – Dec. 29 23:00 |
| Add/Drop | Jan. 7, 2014 10:00 – Jan. 10 17:00 |
| Online Application for Additional Seats | Jan. 11 09:00 – Jan. 15 17:00 |
| Adjustment Period | Mar. 3 09:00 – Mar. 7 17:00 (first week of Spring semester) |

During the online application phase, students may apply for general education courses and some foundational courses offered outside their major via "Course Registration" — "Browse Alternative Courses." Applications for English Language Center, Social Sciences Department, and major-specific courses must be submitted as paper forms to the respective teaching secretaries. No registration changes will be accepted after 17:00 on March 7, and textbook orders cannot be modified during the adjustment period.

Textbook ordering is open from Dec. 20, 2013 09:00 to Dec. 29, 2013 23:00. After the deadline, the Academic Affairs Office will generate orders and forward them to the bookstore; no ordering service will be available during the add/drop or adjustment periods, and subsequent course additions or removals will not affect finalized textbook orders. Students should review their textbook lists carefully before the deadline. For courses with multiple alternative textbooks, ordering defaults to all options — students who need only some titles can remove unwanted items via "Course Registration" — "Order Textbooks" — "Current Ordered Textbooks List." Listed prices are estimates; a price of 0.00 indicates "to be determined" and may change due to reprinting or new editions.

The Academic Affairs Office also advises students to read the "STU Undergraduate Course Registration Rules (Dec. 2012)" and "Registration Process and Handling Rules (ver2.0)," consult their advisors, safeguard personal accounts, and ensure that paper-based registration change forms are signed by the course instructor personally. Students who have fulfilled their program's required English level may, resources permitting, continue to higher-level English courses, with excess credits counting toward free electives or Category III (Language, Literature and Arts) general education requirements. Professional courses from other majors that are passed and fall outside the student's own program may substitute for corresponding general education categories (by the first three digits of the course code), except for physical education and compulsory Social Sciences Department courses.
