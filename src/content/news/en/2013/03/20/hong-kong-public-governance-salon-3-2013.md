STU Law School’s third **Hong Kong Public Governance Experience** salon, titled “The Fantastic Drift of Hong Kong’s Advantages,” will be held from **4:00 to 6:00 p.m. on Sunday, March 24** in the Science Lecture Hall. The event is also part of the continuing-education course series *Public Governance Commentary* and is required for students enrolled in that course.

The guest is an emeritus professor of history at Rutgers, the State University of New Jersey. Ms. Chen Wong Sui will serve as guest moderator, and Associate Professor Yan Zhigang will moderate the event. From historical and public perspectives, the salon will examine Hong Kong’s changes over the past three decades, the relationship between government policy and social conditions, and lessons from development experiences in Hong Kong, mainland China, the United States, and Japan.

Topics will include the elements and conditions behind Hong Kong’s advantages, the effects of different political systems, the impact of development policies on people’s lives, whether modernization follows identifiable patterns, and how to preserve values and culture that may otherwise disappear during development.
