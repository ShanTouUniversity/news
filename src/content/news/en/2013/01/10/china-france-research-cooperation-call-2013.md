It is reported that, under the memorandum of understanding between China's Ministry of Science and Technology (MOST) and the French National Research Agency (ANR), the two sides have jointly launched the call for the 2013 China-France Intergovernmental Science and Technology Cooperation Projects to promote innovation collaboration among industry, academia and research institutions in shared priority areas. The Research Office of Shantou University has forwarded the application guidelines.

The program aims to co-fund high-level joint research projects to foster sustainable, high-quality Sino-French scientific cooperation. Universities, research institutes and enterprises from both countries are encouraged to apply jointly, with priority given to projects integrating industry, academia and research. Applicants should have an existing collaboration basis, and projects should be completed or achieve interim results within three years. The two sides will jointly fund no more than three projects in principle, based on the quality of proposals.

The priority areas for this call are resource recycling technologies — including recycling, treatment and utilization technologies for waste such as metals, plastics, rubber and high value-added products, water recycling technologies, and sludge treatment — and environmental remediation technologies — including soil remediation, biodiversity restoration for coastlines, rivers and groundwater, and POPs pollution analysis and monitoring for marine and basin environments. Research leveraging biotechnology, nanotechnology and energy-saving technologies is particularly encouraged.

Application requires parallel submissions: Chinese and French partners must submit separately to MOST and ANR respectively, with identical English project titles, partner institutions and applicants; unilateral submissions are invalid. The Chinese side uses an offline procedure, requiring a bilingual information form and direct completion of the designated proposal template without reformatting, followed by submission of two printed copies plus electronic files to the university's designated mailbox. The French side applies online via www.agence-nationale-recherche.fr.
