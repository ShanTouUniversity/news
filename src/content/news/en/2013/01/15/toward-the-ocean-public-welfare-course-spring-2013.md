The Shantou University School of Science opened registration for the spring offering of “Toward the Ocean,” a public-service course formally titled “Marine Territorial Awareness and Marine-Ecosystem Conservation Outreach.” The course will run from March through May 2013, offer two classes for 60 undergraduates across the university, and carry one credit for 48 hours of study and service after successful assessment.

The course will include polar topics connected with STU’s Antarctic expedition project. It is designed to build understanding of the ocean and marine conservation while giving students experience communicating marine knowledge to the public. Sun Zewei is the course coordinator; the course has seven instructors and two student assistants.

### Course plan

Lectures and discussions will take place in weeks 3–12 on Friday afternoons during periods 6–7 or 8–9 in E Building Room 303, with some lectures taught jointly. Fieldwork will take place on weekends at locations announced separately. The practical component totals four days and 32 hours, including:

- A survey of the Suai Bay mangrove ecosystem;
- Social-practice work at middle schools or seaside fishing villages;
- Public marine-knowledge education and social surveys;
- Group service-learning and social-research reports.

Course content covers basic marine knowledge, the current state of marine-ecosystem conservation, service-learning, project design, and social-survey methods, as well as mangrove and coral-reef ecosystems, polar environments, and biodiversity conservation. Students will also study questionnaire design, survey planning, and survey-report writing.

### Registration

The course is open to undergraduates across the university. Applicants should follow the rules for public-service courses, be willing to study marine topics and participate in service work, and be prepared to work hard and cooperate with others. Students active in public service, interested in marine conservation, or previously enrolled in marine-related courses will receive priority.

Download the *Toward the Ocean Public-Service Course Registration Form* and email the completed spreadsheet to Yang at `mlyang@stu.edu.cn` by February 27, 2013. To protect student personal information, this article does not reproduce the form or its fields for names, contact details, dormitories, and similar information.

Course grades will combine process evaluations by instructors and teaching assistants (70%) with evaluations of team performance by service partners and the public-service teaching team (30%). Within the first component, classroom performance accounts for 20%, service practice for 50%, and course summary and sharing for 30%.

For field activities, participants must follow instructions, travel in groups, avoid entering the water or engaging in dangerous activities, respect local customs, and pay attention to food hygiene and personal safety.
