The Shantou University Changjiang School of Journalism and Communication opened registration for its Public-Welfare Photography course. The course is open to 40 undergraduates from across the university and is scheduled to run from March to May 2013. Students who complete the course and pass the assessment will receive one credit. Students who own a digital single-lens reflex camera will receive priority, but the notice does not list one as a requirement.

The course, offered jointly by the Changjiang School of Journalism and Communication and the University Youth League Committee, combines photography fundamentals, social-research methods, public-welfare photography, team training, project planning, and field service. It aims to develop photography, communication, collaboration, and community-project implementation skills.

### Course schedule

| Stage | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Wednesdays in Weeks 3–9 | Periods 6 and 7: lectures on service learning, photography fundamentals, field communication, and social research |
| Saturday of Week 5 | On-campus photography practice and topic research |
| Saturday and Sunday of Week 8 | Field photography for the public-welfare project |
| Saturday of Week 9 | Public-welfare photography service at a rural primary school or Cunxin Shantang factory |
| Wednesday of Week 10 | Periods 6–9: review, experience sharing, and exhibition |

The course includes 48 class hours: 12 hours of training, 32 hours of field service, and 4 hours of review and assessment. Practice locations include the campus, areas around Shantou, and partner service organizations. Students will work in groups on public-welfare photography projects and, based on service recipients’ needs, produce albums, hold an exhibition, or submit work to the media.

### Registration

Interested undergraduates should download and complete the *Public-Welfare Photography Course Registration Form*, include their grade, major, and name in the filename as required, and email the spreadsheet to `11zcguo@stu.edu.cn`. Registration closes at the end of the workday on February 27, 2013. The selected-student list will be posted on the university office information network.

Course grades will combine evaluations by course and teaching assistants with evaluations by the community-service host and beneficiaries, weighted at 70% and 30%, respectively.
