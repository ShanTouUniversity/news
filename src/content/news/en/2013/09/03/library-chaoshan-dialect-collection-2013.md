It is reported that Shantou University Library has released the sixth issue of its special-collections feature, focusing on works about the Chaoshan dialect. The library holds more than 40 titles on the subject, and 12 representative books were selected for this issue.

Also known as Teochew, or Chaozhou speech, the Chaoshan dialect — commonly called "Hoklo" — is spoken in the counties and districts of Shantou, Chaozhou and Jieyang and in most of Shanwei, and has spread with Chaozhou communities to overseas settlements. A sub-dialect of the Minnan language group, it is known for its classical elegance, rich vocabulary and distinctive phonology, and is hailed as a "living fossil of ancient Chinese," giving it considerable value for linguistic research.

The dialect serves as the vehicle for Chao opera, Chaozhou songbooks, folk songs, proverbs and lantern riddles, and is also a bond linking more than 30 million people of Chaozhou origin at home and abroad. With social development and the promotion of Mandarin, the dialect has been fading from the language use of younger generations, making its preservation and protection increasingly urgent.
