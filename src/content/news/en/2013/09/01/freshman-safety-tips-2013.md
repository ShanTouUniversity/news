The period when new students arrive on campus is a time of heightened risk for various crimes. It is reported that the security office of Shantou University has recently compiled everyday safety guidance for the incoming class of 2013, focusing on fraud and theft prevention and reminding students and their parents to stay alert during registration and campus life afterwards.

## Fraud Prevention: Beware of Impersonation

The guidance lists several common types of fraud seen around campus: false information about prize wins or goods offered at unusually low prices; requests for help designed to win sympathy; impersonation of police officers asking for bank account details; calls to parents by people posing as university staff who falsely claim that the student has been in a car accident and needs money urgently; and impersonation of judicial officials or acquaintances.

In response, the security office advises new students not to be tempted by small gains and to stay wary of unfamiliar calls and messages. They should not disclose personal or bank card information to strangers, should not remit or transfer money to strangers or to so-called "safe accounts," and should remind their family members to take precautions. Suspicious situations should be reported to the relevant university department or to the police promptly.

## Theft Prevention: Lock Doors and Safeguard Belongings

On theft, the guidance warns of people who enter rooms under the pretext of looking for someone or selling goods, of thieves who seize opportunities in public places, of property stolen through unsecured windows with the help of tools, of forced entry, and of cases in which a fellow student takes someone else's belongings on impulse.

The precautions include locking the door whenever leaving a dorm room and closing windows when going out; keeping personal belongings in sight in public places; storing valuables in lockable cabinets; not leaving valuables near windows at night; and carrying little cash while depositing money in the bank promptly.

The guidance also covers everyday safety topics such as fire prevention and accident prevention.
