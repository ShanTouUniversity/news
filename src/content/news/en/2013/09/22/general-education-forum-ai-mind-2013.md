It is reported that the fifth talk in Shantou University's General Education Forum lecture series will be held at **7:30 p.m. on Monday, September 23**, in the lecture theatre of the STU Library, under the theme "A Dialogue: Can Computers Surpass Humans?" Co-hosted by the Higher Education Research Institute and the Christianity Research Center of the College of Liberal Arts, the event brings together Professor Liu Xiaoli of Renmin University of China and Professor Zhou Changle of Xiamen University for a dialogue between artificial intelligence and the philosophy of mind.

According to the organizers, Liu Xiaoli is a professor and doctoral supervisor at the School of Philosophy of Renmin University of China and director of the university's Institute of Modern Logic and Philosophy of Science and Technology. Her research focuses on the philosophy of science and cognitive philosophy, and she serves as secretary-general of the Modern Logic Professional Committee of the Chinese Logic Society, among other academic posts.

Zhou Changle is a professor and doctoral supervisor at Xiamen University's School of Information Science and Technology, where he previously served as dean. He is now director of the Fujian Provincial Key Laboratory of Brain-like Intelligent Systems and also director of Xiamen University's Art Cognition and Computing Laboratory. He is a council member of the Chinese Association for Artificial Intelligence and president of its Fujian branch, and has long been engaged in artificial intelligence and interdisciplinary research, publishing six monographs and more than 200 papers.

The dialogue will cover how computers compose poetry and music, whether they can perform all human work, the three rises and three falls over the more than 50-year history of artificial intelligence, and how to think about the mind from a philosophical perspective, as well as the relationship between Gödel's incompleteness theorems and artificial intelligence.
