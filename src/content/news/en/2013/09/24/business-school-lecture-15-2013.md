The 15th academic lecture of Shantou University Business School in 2013 will be held from **7:30 to 9:30 p.m. on Thursday, September 26** in **ACC Meeting Room 5**. Professor Xu Deqi of the National University of Singapore has been invited to speak on **“The Importance of Statistics in Research.”** Associate Professor Lin Jiali will chair the lecture.

| Item | Information |
| --- | --- |
| Lecture | Business School Academic Lecture No. 15 of 2013 |
| Topic | The Importance of Statistics in Research |
| Speaker | Xu Deqi, National University of Singapore |
| Chair | Associate Professor Lin Jiali |
| Time | 7:30–9:30 p.m., Thursday, September 26, 2013 |
| Venue | ACC Meeting Room 5 |

The lecture will focus on the role of statistics in academic research. The speaker holds that statistics is an important tool for data analysis. Many studies in the past relied mainly on qualitative methods, whose conclusions are more easily influenced by subjective factors; today, to publish in well-known international journals, quantitative approaches such as statistical analysis are better able to support research arguments and give a paper a higher chance of being accepted. He stresses that quantitative research does not necessarily require sophisticated techniques — simple methods can serve the purpose just as well.

The lecture will also discuss several published papers to show how researchers can select the necessary techniques and interpret the results with the help of computer tools. The speaker will caution that special care is needed when obtaining data, as the way data are acquired matters greatly to a study’s conclusions. Faculty and students across the university are welcome to attend.

Xu Deqi was born in Hong Kong to a family from Chaozhou and received his doctorate from the Department of Statistics at the University of Toronto in 1983. From 1990 to 1993 he taught at the National University of Singapore Business School’s graduate school and served as its deputy director, teaching bachelor and master of business administration courses and providing executive training to local managers. From 1995 to 1996 he served as head of the Department of Decision Sciences and as director of the Chinese MBA programme. He has been a consultant and visiting professor at the University of British Columbia, the University of Toronto, California State University and the Chinese University of Hong Kong, and in recent years has been a visiting professor at Jimei University, South China University of Technology, SIM University, Maejo University and Rajamangala University of Technology. He has published numerous papers in international journals and has served on the editorial boards of several journals.
