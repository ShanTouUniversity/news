It is reported that *Night Singing Brother*, a new stage production performed by Hong Kong professional a cappella theatre group Yat Po Singers, will be staged in the Auditorium on the evening of September 27, opening Colorful Arts Season 2013 Autumn. To help audiences understand and enjoy the performance, the organizers — the Art Education Center of the Cheung Kong School of Art and Design — released the creative background of the production and its scene breakdown in advance.

## Creative Background

According to the organizers, Yat Po Singers has developed a distinctive form of performance: it uses music but is not only singing, incorporates dance but is not limited to movement, and draws on theatre but is not simply acting. Through voice and body, the troupe presents concepts, stories and characters, as well as the encounters, lessons, joys, sorrows and laments of life, and shares them with the audience.

The creative team — Wu Zhuoxian, Zhao Bocheng, Wu Yulie and Wu Jiamei — came to know one another through their creative work, and their collaboration began with *Shi Jian*, the group's first production. That work was highly inspiring for the team, and after several years of restaging, reflection and refinement, this multi-faceted form of performance was raised to a new level.

The sheng is a classical and romantic instrument, and in Wu Zhuoxian's hands it also feels "trendy". How can a fashionable take on classical Chinese music converse with modern people and the past? Does the breath blown into the sheng also give it life? This is where *Night Singing Brother* began to take shape.

The name Du Yuesheng carries an air of mystery. A prominent figure who moved between the underworld and the establishment, he was originally a source of inspiration for the creative team. Yet after repeated discussions, the team realised that what it wanted to explore was not the legend itself, but the state of mind of a powerful figure like Du Yuesheng — someone who could command great influence and possessed money, status and power — as he faced death, and whether such a person would still have thoughts about life after dying. The work thus reflects on the responsibility that creators, arts practitioners and teachers bear for society and for their own lives, and on their relationship with audiences, students and the next generation: how to pass things on, how to keep them going, to whom and what should be passed on, whether anyone still treasures the values they wish to hand down, whether the world needs us, and whether we will leave with regret.

As for why the title is *Night Singing Brother* rather than the familiar Chinese phrase meaning "feasting and singing every night", the team asks: Is there much "owing" in life? Is it the brother who owes the sheng, or the sheng that owes the brother? Must everything owed eventually be repaid?

Once the music was written and rehearsals began, the seven young singers each brought their strengths to the fore: the steady and seasoned Huang Dahui anchors the team, while the petite Li Lijin plays the bulky sheng, making a striking contrast. Image, colour, body, age, conflict and harmony all became elements of the work, which together tell of the past and the present and reflect the face of, and phenomena in, present-day Hong Kong.

The organizers noted that the program introduction was narrated by Wu Yulie and compiled by Wu Jiamei.

## Admissions Reminder

The organizers also reminded students and faculty without tickets that they may queue in the designated area outside the main entrance of the Auditorium from 19:00 on the evening of the performance. If seats for issued tickets are still empty at 19:30 before the performance begins, the organizers will regard the ticket holders as having given up their rights, and staff will lead the students queuing on site to those seats. Seating is limited, and admission stops once the venue is full. Ticket holders are also asked to take their seats 10 minutes before the performance. The organizers thanked students and faculty for their support of the Colorful Arts Season.
