It is reported that the Student Affairs Office of Shantou University has issued Disciplinary Bulletin No. 2 of 2013, reporting two cases of examination misconduct that occurred in June 2013 and the decisions taken by the university. Seven students were disciplined in total.

## What Happened

According to the bulletin, during the **Psychology examination on June 19, 2013**, a 2010 student surnamed Wu majoring in Mechanical Design, Manufacturing and Automation in the College of Engineering used a mobile phone to search for answers and was caught on the spot by an invigilator.

During the **Random Signal Analysis examination on June 25, 2013**, six 2010 students brought materials related to the examination into the venue: Deng, Huang, Zhu and Wu from Communication Engineering, and Meng and Sun from Electronic Information Engineering.

## Basis and Decisions

Under Article 16 of the Regulations on the Management of Students in Regular Institutions of Higher Education (Ministry of Education Order No. 21), Article 18 of the STU Regulations on Disciplinary Action against Students (Trial) and Article 50 of the STU Interim Regulations on Credit-Based Undergraduate Student Status Management, and after deliberation by the university, the student surnamed Wu received a serious warning, while the six students Deng, Huang, Zhu, Wu, Meng and Sun received warnings. All seven also had a zero recorded for the course in which they cheated.

## Impact on Degrees

The Detailed Rules for the Award of Bachelor's Degrees to Regular Undergraduates at Shantou University (STU Degree Document No. 06 of 2005) provides that a student disciplined for cheating in an assessment during their studies cannot be awarded a bachelor's degree. However, a student who was previously disciplined for examination cheating and performs well afterwards, receiving a university second-class scholarship or higher, or graduating with a total GPA of 3.5 or above, may apply in person. If the department and college confirm that the student's conduct has improved markedly and that all other degree requirements are met, and the case is recommended by the college's degree assessment subcommittee, reviewed by the Academic Affairs Office, approved by the president in charge and submitted by the Academic Affairs Office to the university's degree assessment committee for discussion and approval, a bachelor's degree may be awarded.

The university said it hopes the bulletin will serve as a warning to all students so that they help maintain normal teaching and campus order and promote sound campus culture and academic conduct.
