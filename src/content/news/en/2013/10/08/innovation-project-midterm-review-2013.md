It is reported that, in accordance with the requirements of the Notice on the Mid-Term Review of 2012 Undergraduate Innovation Experiment Projects, the Office of Academic Affairs conducted a mid-term review of projects approved in 2012 in September 2013 and has now announced the results.

## Review at a Glance

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Time | September 2013 |
| Projects reviewed | 76 |
| Projects that passed | 70 |
| Projects requiring corrections | 6 (see Attachment 1) |
| Follow-up support | Projects that passed will continue to receive the second installment of project funds |

## What Was Reviewed

The Office of Academic Affairs examined project materials, including the mid-term review form and the project progress report. The review focused on research progress, interim results already achieved, major problems and the measures taken to address them, plans for the next stage, and the use of funds.

## Review Results

A total of 76 projects took part in the mid-term review, of which 70 passed and six require corrections. The review found that the projects generally completed the Mid-Term Review Form and the Progress Log carefully, that the vast majority were making good progress toward their tasks with reasonable plans for the next stage, and that team members were actively involved, achieving the intended training objectives and producing interim results. Some projects have drafted papers and reports, a few have completed their papers and are actively submitting them for publication, and others have produced preliminary physical models, software designs, videos or websites. However, some projects were found to be progressing slowly, facing difficulties with research methods and approaches, or submitting records that were too brief.

## Two Well-Documented Projects Shared as References

The Office of Academic Affairs shared the mid-term review materials of two well-documented projects — "Physical Model Study of Soil Reinforcement with New GFRP Soil Nails" and "A Comparative Study on Bagged Collection of Rural Domestic Waste in Selected Areas of Guangdong Province" — for other project teams to use as references. These two projects recorded their experiments and fieldwork in detail, including records of each stage and meeting minutes, accumulated extensive research data and images, and produced research reports. Their team members had clear divisions of labor, actively communicated with their supervisors to solve problems encountered in the research, and used funds reasonably.

## Recommendations

On the basis of the review, the Office of Academic Affairs offered several recommendations. Projects that are progressing slowly should adjust their research schedules, make good plans for the next stage and speed up their work. Projects with questions about research approaches and methods are advised to consult their supervisors more often and improve their experimental methods. Projects with insufficient supporting materials should build up drafts, experimental data analyses, photographs and other materials produced during the research process, and actively produce survey reports, experiment reports and related research papers.

The university will continue to provide the second installment of project funds to projects that passed the mid-term review, while projects that require corrections must complete them as required.
