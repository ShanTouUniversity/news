It is reported that the English Language Center (ELC) of Shantou University has released its Fall 2013 student activity lineup, offering eight programs that cover crafts, English debate, film appreciation, gender issues, current affairs, food culture, business English and singing. All activities are open to students and faculty members.

## Activity Details

### 1. Making Memories at STU

The activity combines hands-on crafting with English conversation. Students bring a few photos each week, decorate new pages for their photo books, and talk in English about their own experiences and a range of other topics. It takes place at 19:00 every Tuesday at the EAC (second floor, Building 9).

### 2. English Debate Society (EDS)

The society aims to develop critical thinking and strategic skills. It is open not only to debaters but also to students who prefer to watch and listen carefully to arguments, because the ability to analyze issues and make judgments matters just as much. According to the organizers, debate is the best way to improve language ability and the ultimate test of fluency, accuracy and effective use of language. Debates follow the British Parliamentary (B.P.) format. The society meets from 15:00 to 17:00 every Saturday at CILL, with Anny and Collin as advisers.

### 3. Movie Mania

The club uses films as a tool for practicing English, with discussions, scene re-enactments and other activities after screenings. It gathers from 12:00 to 13:30 every Monday at the EAC. Films are screened at 11:00 and 19:00 on Wednesdays at the EAC, and at 10:00 on Tuesdays and 14:00 on Fridays at CILL. Students can also watch films at any time on the computers at CILL. Contact: John Harper (johnh@stu.edu.cn).

### 4. The Gender Issues Group

The group discusses gender, sexual orientation, gender roles and other issues on campus and in society. Past topics have included dating at university, same-sex marriage, AIDS, domestic violence, sexual harassment and prostitution. It aims to create a comfortable space where students can share their views and listen to others in English, and students of all levels are welcome. It meets from 19:00 to 20:00 every Wednesday at the EAC, and topic suggestions can be sent to sarap@stu.edu.cn.

### 5. Current Events Discussion Group

Members discuss local, national and international news and current affairs, sharing facts, opinions or both, which often leads to interesting debates. English is encouraged throughout the session, and students help one another. Computers are available on site for looking up news articles, pictures or cartoons. The lineup did not specify meeting times or a venue for this group.

### 6. Eat. Cook. Learn.

The sessions focus on global food, cooking and table manners. Students can write their own bilingual recipes, share their favorite foods and try new ones, and no cooking skills are required. Led by Ana Rossetti, a multicultural food enthusiast, the activity includes conversation, writing and tasting. It runs from 19:00 to 21:00 every Thursday at the EAC (second floor, Building 9). For details, email anar@stu.edu.cn.

### 7. Business English Practice

The program is designed to help students make a smooth transition from university to the workplace. Students can raise the vocabulary, phrases and business topics they need, look up and confirm their meanings and usage, and then discuss how they are used in business. It runs from 13:30 to 15:00 every Saturday at CILL and welcomes students of all years and majors.

### 8. ELC Sing Along

The activity teaches English through singing, with live band accompaniment (Dave on piano, Don on guitar and Maxi on lead vocals). Songs can be slowed down to help students learn English songs. It takes place at 20:15 every Monday at the EAC.

According to the ELC, all of the above activities are open to students and faculty members, and interested students are welcome to join at the times listed or contact the relevant coordinators for more information.
