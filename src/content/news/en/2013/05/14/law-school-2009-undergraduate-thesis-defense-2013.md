Shantou University’s School of Law announced the undergraduate thesis defense arrangements for the 2009 cohort. A total of **137 students** applied to defend their theses. The defenses were scheduled for May 18, 2013, with six groups working in Rooms G301, G302, G303, G304, G306 and G307.

The defenses were scheduled from 8 a.m. to 11:30 a.m. and from 2 p.m. to 5 p.m. Michael Palmer chaired the defense committee, whose members were Chen Ruilin, Nie Shuo, Xiong Jincai, Temur Gholitsot, Li Wanli and Li Guanghui. Ni Xiangdong served as secretary. Committee members and other faculty members led the six groups. Student names and individual group assignments are not reproduced here.

## Procedure and Requirements

Each student was to introduce the purpose, structure, main arguments and innovations of the thesis within 10 minutes. The lead examiner would then ask two or three questions, followed by 15 minutes of preparation and the defense response. Other faculty members could ask additional questions. A thesis supervisor could not serve as the lead examiner for that thesis. Each group could recommend outstanding theses at a rate of approximately 15 percent, subject to final approval by the defense committee.

Students were required to defend with a PowerPoint presentation and submit their theses and assessment materials as required. The overall score bands were 90–100 for excellent, 80–89 for good, 70–79 for average, 60–69 for pass and 59 or below for fail. A thesis found to involve serious plagiarism would automatically receive a failing grade.

All graduating students were required to attend the defense; students who did not attend could not apply for a degree. Leave was not permitted during the defense period, and lateness, early departure or failure to attend the full defense could be treated as an absence or affect the defense-performance score.
