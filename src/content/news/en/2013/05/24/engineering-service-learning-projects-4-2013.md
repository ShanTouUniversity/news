The Youth League Committee of Shantou University's Engineering School has opened its fourth service-learning project cycle to Engineering students. The public-practice projects are intended to connect service with learning, give students a platform to design and carry out community-oriented activities, and develop communication, project-planning, social-practice, responsibility and empathy skills.

## Project Information

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Organizer | Engineering School Youth League Committee, Shantou University |
| Eligible applicants | Shantou University Engineering School students |
| Project type | Public-practice projects |
| Overall activity period stated in the notice | May–December 2013 |
| Project implementation period | June 2013–April 2014 |
| Application deadline | 11:00 p.m., May 27, 2013 |
| Next stage | The defense time and venue will be announced through the OA system after applications are received |

The projects encourage students to develop, design and operate public-interest activities in teams, learn through service and apply academic knowledge in social practice while reflecting on the results. The cycle also supports the Engineering School's EIP-CDIO educational reform approach and service-learning activities outside class time.

## Defense Arrangements

A subsequent Engineering School notice sets the defense for **May 29, 2013 (Wednesday), 3:10–5:00 p.m.** in **Room D304 of the Teaching Building**. Each project must give a **four-minute PPT presentation**, and at least two members of each project must attend. Participants are expected to dress appropriately. A project that does not attend the project-review defense as required will be treated as having withdrawn from the application.

The attached project summary lists five proposed project entries, with service arrangements running from June 2013 through April 2014 and locations including the STU campus and Tuopu. Because the summary also contains project-leader names and funding amounts, those personal and financial details are not reproduced here.

## Results

A subsequent result notice from the Engineering School reported that the defense for the fourth service-learning cycle was held on **May 29, 2013**. Based on the review, project feasibility and expected practical effect, the school approved the following four projects for funding:

- **“Mingjing Qingmu” (“Clear Vision”—Eyeglass Cleaning and Care)**
- **“Book Exchange—Cultural Transmission and Exchange”**
- **“Jianxi Guan’ai” (“Care Through Haircuts”)**
- **“Smoke Dispersed”**

The result attachment gives the third project as **“Jianxi Guan’ai, Haircuts Convey Care,”** while the body of the result notice calls it **“Jianxi Guan’ai.”** This difference in the project name is retained. The attachment also lists **“Medical Plan,”** which was not approved; the review comment said that students lacked the professional medical knowledge needed for the proposed activity. Project leaders, defense scores and personal information from the result attachment are not reproduced.

Approved project teams were asked to organize their activities in line with service-learning principles and complete the work according to plan.
