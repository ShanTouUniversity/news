The Shantou University Youth Volunteer Association planned to hold its **19th Board** transition process in **early June 2013** and invited students from across the university to apply as candidates. Guided by the Communist Youth League Committee of Shantou University, the association uses a service-learning model to organize social public-welfare service with the goal of making everyone a volunteer.

## Positions and Eligibility

The 19th Board would have **five members**: one president and four vice presidents. The president would set the association’s development direction, coordinate and make decisions on its affairs, and communicate regularly with the University Youth League Committee. Vice presidents would assist the president and oversee the association’s standing bodies and three project teams. Board members had to be able to serve continuously for one academic year.

Applicants were expected to have strong academic and personal records, be committed to volunteer service, have experience coordinating activities and social activities, understand the association’s current development, show an innovative and enterprising spirit, and have a sound understanding of service learning.

## Application and Election

Applicants had to submit a preliminary candidate-registration form and a work plan of at least 1,000 Chinese characters addressing the position they sought. All materials had to be packaged as a RAR file and emailed to the association at `volunteersofstu@163.com` by **10 a.m. on May 26**. The registration form requested names, student numbers, contact details and personal experience, so it is not reproduced here. A note in the form still referred to the “18th Board,” which conflicts with the 19th-board transition described in the notice; this article follows the main notice.

The selection process allocated 20% to a work assessment, 40% to an interview and 40% to a speech and defense. The interview consisted of a one-minute self-introduction, a five-minute summary of the work plan and ten minutes of questions from the interview panel. Formal candidates would give public speeches and answer questions at the transition assembly. The transition committee and attending volunteer representatives would take part in the election, with the new Board selected on the basis of the combined scores. The result would then be submitted to the University Youth League Committee for review and publicity; appointments would follow if no objection was raised.

## Board Responsibilities

The Board would make decisions through collective voting, with one vote for each of the five members and a majority required to approve or reject a proposal. Proposals concerning the organization’s overall development had to be submitted in writing at least three days before a regular meeting. All Board members would be responsible for Board decisions and attend regular and emergency meetings; anyone unable to attend for an important reason had to request leave in advance and explain the reason.
