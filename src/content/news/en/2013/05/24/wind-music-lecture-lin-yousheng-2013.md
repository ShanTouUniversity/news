The Shantou University Cheung Kong School of Art and Design and Art Education Center will host a lecture on **“The Appeal of Wind Music”** at **7:30 p.m. on Monday, May 27, 2013**, in the **Science Lecture Hall**. Lin Yousheng, a first-class conductor with the Shanghai Opera House, part-time professor at the Shanghai Conservatory of Music and guest professor at Shantou University, will speak. Wang Shouzhi, dean of the Cheung Kong School of Art and Design, will moderate. Faculty and students interested in wind music are welcome.

## Speaker

Lin Yousheng graduated from the Composition and Conducting Department of the Shanghai Conservatory of Music in 1983, where he studied under Professor Huang Xiaotong. He is a first-class conductor with the Shanghai Opera House and has also served as a part-time professor at the Shanghai Conservatory of Music and East China University of Political Science and Law and as a guest professor at Shantou University. He has conducted orchestras including the Saarland State Opera Orchestra and Nordhausen Symphony Orchestra in Germany, the Tokyo Philharmonic Orchestra, the China Symphony Orchestra, the Beijing Symphony Orchestra, the Shanghai Symphony Orchestra, the Taipei Symphony Orchestra, the Shenzhen Symphony Orchestra and the Macao Youth Wind Orchestra.

As an opera conductor, Lin conducted the Chinese premieres of *Turandot*, *Così fan tutte* and *La belle Hélène*. He has also conducted *La bohème*, *Eugene Onegin*, *Die Fledermaus*, *Tosca*, *Carmen*, *Cavalleria rusticana*, *Pagliacci*, *Jiang Jie*, *The Hegemon-King of Chu* and *The Wilderness*, as well as the ballets *Giselle*, *Swan Lake* and *The Great Qin Dynasty*. He has visited Germany, Switzerland, Spain, Poland, Austria, Thailand, Japan, Denmark, Sweden and Finland, as well as Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macao.

He has received awards at the Shanghai Arts Festival and the Shanghai Spring International Music Festival on multiple occasions. In 2000, he received the Best Conductor Award at the ninth International Wind Music Festival in Rybnik, Poland, and was named one of Shanghai’s Top Ten Worker Artists and an Outstanding Arts Educator in Shanghai. He received a State Council special government allowance in 1992. In recent years, he has taught symphonic wind-band ensemble at the Shanghai Conservatory of Music, delivered wind-music outreach lectures and conducting workshops at universities nationwide under Ministry of Education assignments, and served repeatedly as a judge for competitions organized by the Ministry of Education, the Wind Music Association of the Chinese Musicians’ Association and Taiwan wind-music competitions.

Lin has also recorded six symphonic wind-band CDs and led wind bands at international festivals and competitions. The bands received awards at international music festivals in Poland, Austria and Switzerland, among other locations.
