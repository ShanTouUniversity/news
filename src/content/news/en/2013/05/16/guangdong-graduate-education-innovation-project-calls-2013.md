Following a notice from the Guangdong Provincial Department of Education, Shantou University’s Graduate School opened applications for the 2013 Guangdong Graduate Degree and Education Innovation Plan. The call covered graduate academic forums and summer schools, graduate degree and education reform research, graduate model courses, and talent-development projects at Guangdong joint graduate-training demonstration bases.

## Application Categories

| Category | STU quota and main requirements |
| --- | --- |
| Graduate academic forums and summer schools | STU could apply for two academic forum projects and one summer school project. Applications were limited to philosophy, economics, law, education, literature, history, management and arts |
| Graduate degree and education reform research | STU could apply for six projects, including no more than two key projects. Projects had to improve graduate education quality, address practical issues facing Guangdong or universities, and offer original, targeted and actionable research and recommendations |
| Graduate model courses | STU could apply for four projects. Courses had to be graduate courses in science or engineering, and each application required a classroom teaching video of at least 30 minutes, with RMVB suggested |
| Joint graduate-training demonstration-base projects | Applications were open to the first two batches of established Guangdong joint graduate-training demonstration bases. A university supervisor and graduate students would apply together; each supervisor could submit one project, normally each base one project, while a base involving multiple universities could submit two |

For a key reform-research project, the principal investigator had to be an active employee with an associate-senior or higher professional title who had led a project at the provincial or ministerial level or above. Principal investigators for general and self-funded projects had to be active employees with an intermediate or higher professional title, prior participation in a provincial or ministerial project, and the ability to organize implementation. Applicants could not have an unfinished project of the same type. Key projects were normally to be completed within two years of approval; general and self-funded projects within one year.

A joint-training-base talent project would normally last no more than one year, with a possible one-year extension in special circumstances. Its topic had to arise from a real problem at the partner organization, with graduate students carrying out the core research collaboratively under a supervisor’s guidance. The program was intended to strengthen the partner’s innovation capacity and the students’ research and problem-solving experience.

## Submission Schedule

Units were asked to organize applications and preliminary selection in light of their disciplinary strengths and graduate-training characteristics. Application or proposal forms, one paper copy each, were due to the Graduate School by **May 31**. The Graduate School would evaluate submissions and determine a recommended list. Final recommended materials had to arrive by **June 5**: ten paper copies and one electronic copy of each application or proposal, plus one paper and one electronic copy of each summary form. Late submissions would not be accepted.

Inquiries could be directed to campus extension `4745` or `xwb@stu.edu.cn`. Application forms, summary sheets and supporting materials contain applicant and project-team information and are not reproduced here.
