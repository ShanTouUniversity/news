### Assessment standards

Design-report results will use a combination of **five-grade ratings, percentage scores and written comments**. The main areas of assessment are:

- Completion of the design-report assignment;
- The ability to apply learned knowledge to analyze and solve practical problems;
- The significance and practical value of the design-report results;
- Innovative ideas and defense performance.

| Grade | Main standard | Quality threshold |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Excellent | The report presents views with practical potential, sufficient evidence, accurate and effective methods and reasonable results; the student explains concepts clearly and answers questions accurately | Above 90% |
| Good | The report broadly meets its stated requirements, evidence is generally sufficient, methods are basically accurate and effective, results are reasonable and questions are answered with relative accuracy | Above 80% |
| Average | The report basically meets the assignment and report requirements, contains no fundamental errors, has a generally reasonable structure and allows the student to answer questions basically | Above 70% |
| Pass | The report only barely meets the basic requirements, has multiple minor errors or an isolated fundamental error, and the student can only barely answer basic questions | Above 60% |
| Fail | The report does not meet the assignment and report requirements, contains multiple fundamental errors, or the student cannot understand or answer the main questions | Below 60% |

### Defense procedure

Before the defense, each panel should review the students' design reports and prepare its main questions. During the defense, a student's presentation of the report's views and content should be no more than five minutes, questions from teachers should be no more than five minutes, and the student's answers should take about five minutes. Each defense should normally be kept within 15 minutes. Panels must keep records and assess students' command of basic theory, knowledge and skills, as well as their ability to analyze and solve problems. Questions unsuitable for debate during the session may be discussed afterward.
