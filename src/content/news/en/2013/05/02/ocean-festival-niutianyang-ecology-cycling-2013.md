STU's School of Science will organize the “Toward the Ocean, Practice Environmental Protection, Enjoy Cycling” field study of the Niutianyang estuarine wetland from **9:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. on May 4**. Participants will meet at the **university motto monument** and travel by bicycle. The activity is an outdoor class for *Exploring Marine Biology* and part of the school's first Marine Festival.

### Activity details

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Theme | Field study of the current ecological conditions of the Niutianyang estuarine wetland |
| Format | Bicycle field study |
| Time | 9:00 a.m.–2:00 p.m., May 4 (Saturday) |
| Meeting point | University motto monument |
| Faculty instructor | Sun Zewei, Institute of Marine Biology |
| Participants | All students enrolled in *Exploring Marine Biology*; students enrolled in *Marine Ecology* and the *Toward the Ocean* public-welfare course may also choose to participate |

### Safety and environmental requirements

Participants should bring a bicycle in good condition, drinking water, lunch or snacks and protective equipment. Cyclists should stay in formation, control their speed, follow the group instructions, avoid overtaking the lead rider without permission and remain ahead of the rider closing the group. Participants should prioritize safety, avoid racing, keep the site clean and avoid making loud noises in the wetland so that birds are not disturbed. Rain or other extreme weather will automatically cancel the activity; a replacement date will be announced separately.

The notice provides no public institutional email or other general contact channel. The instructor's telephone number and the names and internal contact details of the student lead and closing riders are not reproduced here.

### Background on Niutianyang wetland

The notice describes Niutianyang as being in western Shantou, within Shantou's inner harbor and west of the Rongjiang River. It was once a beach that flooded at high tide and emerged at low tide, covering approximately 10,000 mu. In the early 1960s, land reclamation was carried out there. The notice records a post-reclamation harvest of 1,190 jin of grain per mu in the year of planting and also recounts the deaths of 553 young soldiers and students who defended the embankment during a severe typhoon. The area later shifted back to aquaculture, with more than 20,000 mu of ponds.

According to the notice, Niutianyang now includes a scenic embankment extending for several miles and a wetland nature-reserve landscape. It is an important habitat and stopover for migratory birds, while also facing pressure from urban expansion. The field study is intended to draw attention to the ecological conditions of this coastal wetland near STU.
