The STU School of Science will hold a Science Lecture Series talk from **4:30 to 5:30 p.m. on Friday, May 10, 2013**, in **Room D403**. Professor Zhang Jiping of Peking University will introduce the basic ideas and methods of group theory in a talk titled “Symmetry and Classification.”

A group is a mathematical concept used to describe the symmetry of objects, while group theory is the mathematical study of symmetry. The talk will discuss group theory's applications across mathematics, science and technology, including solving algebraic and differential equations, developing cryptography, understanding important laws of physics and studying the basic structures of molecules in chemistry. Classifying groups helps researchers understand the structures of different objects through their symmetries.

The talk is intended for undergraduate and graduate students in science and engineering, as well as faculty members and researchers interested in the subject.

Zhang Jiping is a mathematician specializing in finite groups and their representations. He served for many years as dean of the School of Mathematical Sciences at Peking University and is currently executive director of the Beijing International Center for Mathematical Research.

According to the public profile in the notice, Zhang's honors include the First China Youth Science and Technology Award, the National Science Fund for Distinguished Young Scholars, selection for the national “Hundreds, Thousands and Tens of Thousands of Talent Project,” a first prize from the former State Education Commission for scientific and technological progress, a third prize in the National Natural Science Awards, the Qiushi Outstanding Young Scholar Award, the China Young Scientist Award, designation as a nationally recognized young and middle-aged expert with outstanding contributions, appointment as a Changjiang Scholar Distinguished Professor under the Ministry of Education program, the Chern Mathematics Award, the National Outstanding Science and Technology Worker title and the May 1 Labor Medal.
