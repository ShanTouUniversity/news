The STU School of Science will hold a Science Salon talk from **9:30 to 10:30 a.m. on Tuesday, May 14, 2013**, in the **Lecture Hall of the Center for Biomedical and Advanced Materials Research**. Shuai Zhigang, a Changjiang Distinguished Professor in the Department of Chemistry at Tsinghua University, will speak on “Theoretical Simulation of Electronic Processes in Organic Functional Materials.”

The talk will focus on theoretical simulation of electronic processes in organic functional materials. Shuai's long-term theoretical-chemistry research includes electronic correlation, quantum-chemical methods, charge transport in organic semiconductors, luminescence processes, excited-state structure and nonlinear response.

### Speaker profile

Shuai Zhigang was born in Jiangxi in August 1962. He received a bachelor's degree from the Department of Physics at Sun Yat-sen University in July 1983 and a PhD in theoretical physics from Fudan University in July 1989. From March 1990 to December 2001, he was a postdoctoral researcher and researcher at the New Materials Chemistry Laboratory of the University of Mons-Hainaut in Belgium. From January 2002 to April 2008, he was a Hundred Talents Programme researcher and doctoral supervisor at the Institute of Chemistry of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. Since May 2008, he has been a Changjiang Distinguished Professor in Tsinghua University's Department of Chemistry.

His honors include the National Science Fund for Distinguished Young Scholars and selection as a national candidate in the New Century Talents Project. He was elected a member of the International Academy of Quantum Molecular Science in 2008, became a fellow of the Royal Society of Chemistry in 2009, was elected a foreign member of Academia Europaea in 2011, received the Chinese Chemical Society–AkzoNobel Chemistry Award in 2012 and was elected a foreign member of the Royal Belgian Academy in 2013.

Shuai also serves on the Chinese Chemical Society executive council and related professional committees, sits on the editorial boards of several chemistry journals, and is an adjunct professor at Peking University, Nanjing University, Northeast Normal University and Hunan University.

According to the attached profile, he has published 260 SCI papers, received more than 6,700 citations and has an h-index of 44. His representative theoretical-chemistry work includes proposing an electronic-correlation effect that explains singlet-exciton formation in polymer electroluminescence; establishing a density-matrix renormalization-group method in quantum chemistry; developing a path-integral theory of non-radiative transitions coupled to multiple vibrational modes; proposing a tunneling-enhanced hopping model for charge transport in organic semiconductors; and developing excited-state-structure and nonlinear-response theories and computational methods for correlated-electron systems.
