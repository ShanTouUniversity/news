STU's School of Science will offer a summer Hong Kong collaboration class for the *Toward the Ocean* service-learning course. The school will recruit **20 undergraduates** from across the university to study and conduct marine-service activities with 20 exchange students from The Hong Kong Polytechnic University. Formally titled “Marine Territorial Awareness and Marine-Ecosystem Conservation Outreach,” the course syllabus classifies the offering as a general-education course of 48 hours carrying one credit after successful assessment.

The opening paragraph of the notice says the special class will run from **July 2 to 9**. The course syllabus and detailed schedule instead state **July 1 to 9**. The source does not explain the difference, so both dates are preserved here; students should follow subsequent arrangements from the course team.

### Course content and fieldwork

The course combines lectures, group discussion, interactive sharing and social practice. Students take part in project design and public-service work with support from instructors. Sun Zewei is the course coordinator, and five STU instructors are assigned to the course.

The course covers:

- Basic marine knowledge, China's marine territory and marine-rights issues;
- China's coastal ecosystems, marine pollution, marine disasters and conservation strategies;
- Mangroves, coral reefs, sharks, marine mammals and other marine biological resources;
- Service-learning, project design, questionnaire design and social-survey report writing;
- Marine-knowledge education and public outreach for middle-school students and the public, together with social surveys;
- Group service reports, social-research reports and project presentations.

Fieldwork locations include **Yun'ao Middle School in Nan'ao County, Nan'ao fishing villages and urban Shantou**. Partner organizations include Shantou marine and environmental authorities, nearby fishing villages, primary and secondary schools and public activity venues. The practical component is planned as four days and 32 hours; the syllabus also lists lectures, project-design presentations and review, fieldwork, final assessment and experience sharing.

### Recruitment and registration

The class is open to undergraduates across the university and plans to select 20 students, with interviews if necessary. Applicants should follow the rules for service-learning courses, be willing to study marine topics and participate in public service, work hard and cooperate with others. Priority will be given to students active in public service, willing to devote time to marine conservation or previously enrolled in marine-related courses.

### Assessment and safety

Course grades combine evaluations by instructors and teaching assistants of students' learning process (70 percent) with evaluations of team performance by service partners and beneficiaries (30 percent). Within the instructor component, classroom performance accounts for 20 percent, service practice 50 percent and course summary and sharing 30 percent. Students must also complete project presentations, individual reflections and social-research and fieldwork reports.

During fieldwork, participants must follow instructions and travel in groups, and must not swim in the sea or take part in dangerous activities. During home visits and surveys, they should respect local customs, avoid embarrassing questions, accept no gifts, and pay attention to food hygiene, heat, sun exposure and personal belongings.

The *Toward the Ocean* course has been offered since the autumn semester of the 2012–2013 academic year. It has received coverage from Shantou Television, *Xin Kuai Bao*, *Shantou Metropolis Daily*, *Shantou Special Zone Evening News*, Sina Guangdong and 21CN News, and was approved as a project under the Ministry of Education's Hong Kong–Mainland University Faculty and Student Exchange Program.
