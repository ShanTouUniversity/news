STU will organize faculty participation in the first National University Micro-Lecture Teaching Competition, hosted by the National Teacher Education Network Training Center of the Ministry of Education. The competition aims to support faculty professional development and teaching ability, promote the integration of information technology with disciplinary teaching, and provide a platform for sharing teaching experience and showcasing faculty teaching.

The campus notice asks interested faculty members to upload their entries through the National University Micro-Lecture Teaching Competition website ([weike.enetedu.com](http://weike.enetedu.com)) **by June 1, 2013**. The competition plan attached to the notice gives the preliminary-round period as **December 20, 2012 to May 20, 2013** and states that individual participants may submit their work through the competition platform. Because the two materials provide different preliminary-round deadlines, participants should follow subsequent university notices and the latest schedule published on the official competition website.

### Entry requirements

A micro-lecture is a short, complete teaching activity recorded on video and centered on a specific knowledge point or teaching segment. Faculty members may select a university course, record a **10–20-minute** micro-lecture video and submit supporting materials such as a teaching-design document and multimedia courseware.

The teaching video should have clear and stable images, appropriate composition and clear sound, and should accurately reflect the teaching setting and the instructor's teaching style. Its opening should display the title, author and institution, and major teaching stages should be identified with captions. The main multimedia courseware accompanying the video must be submitted separately in **PPT format**. Practice exercises, teaching evaluations and multimedia resources may also be submitted in the formats required by the website. The teaching-design document should describe the instructor's teaching ideas, course-design approach and distinctive features, including the teaching background, objectives, methods and summary. It must be submitted in **Word** format and identify the relevant discipline, major, course and target audience at the beginning.

### Competition schedule

Teachers from all disciplines at universities nationwide may participate. The competition plan divides entries into humanities and social sciences and science and engineering categories. The humanities and social sciences category covers philosophy, economics, law, education, literature, history, management and art. The science and engineering category covers science, engineering, agriculture and medicine. Separate awards will be evaluated for faculty members from higher vocational institutions.

The competition has three stages:

| Stage | Schedule and arrangement |
| --- | --- |
| Preliminary round | The competition plan lists December 20, 2012–May 20, 2013 for university selection, production and uploading; the campus notice separately asks interested faculty to upload by June 1, 2013 |
| Semifinal round | May 21–July 20, 2013; provincial organizers conduct evaluations and recommend entries for the national final, generally no more than 5% of the province's total participating faculty |
| Final round | July 21–August 2013; the National Teacher Education Network Training Center determines finalists based on semifinal recommendations and online evaluation, conducts an on-site teaching competition and holds an awards ceremony |

Faculty members may participate through university recommendation or individual registration. University-recommended entries proceed directly to the semifinal round, subject to a limit of no more than three recommended teachers per first-level discipline represented in the university's teaching. Individual participants submit through the competition platform and advance after expert and online evaluation. Each participant may submit only one entry.

### Evaluation and awards

The evaluation rules assign 100 points across four areas:

| Evaluation area | Points | Main criteria |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Entry standards | 10 | Completeness of materials and technical standards for video and courseware |
| Teaching design | 35 | Value of the topic, teaching design and organization, methods and tools |
| Teaching effectiveness | 35 | Achievement of objectives, teaching features and the instructor's teaching style |
| Online evaluation | 20 | Clicks, votes, user reviews, interaction, saves, shares and discussion activity |

The competition offers first, second and third prizes for individual participants and may establish additional special awards. Outstanding organization awards will also be presented to participating institutions and provincial competition organizers.

Entries and supporting materials must be original and may not plagiarize or infringe others' copyrights or contain inappropriate information. Participants retain the copyright to their work but must authorize the National Teacher Education Network Training Center and its affiliated institution to disseminate it online. All entries will be made freely available to the public. Competition organizers will not charge participating faculty members or universities any fees.
