Shantou University’s Academic Affairs Office announced course-selection and textbook-ordering arrangements for the summer and fall 2013 semesters. Course selection for the two semesters would take place in the same cycle. Students could select no more than **six credits** in the summer semester and **25 credits** in the fall semester, excluding public-welfare courses.

## Course-Selection Periods

| Stage | Time | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First selection | May 24, 9 a.m.–May 26, 5 p.m. | If the selected class was not adjusted, the first-selection result would become final and the data would not be cleared |
| Second selection | May 31, 12:30 p.m.–June 2, 5 p.m. | Places were assigned on a first-come, first-served basis within class capacity |
| Add/drop | June 11, 12:30 p.m.–June 16, 5 p.m. | Students could add or drop courses during the prescribed window |
| Online applications | June 17, 9 a.m.–June 21, 5 p.m. | Applications could be submitted for general-education courses and some foundational courses open to students outside the offering major |
| Fall adjustment period | September 15, 9 a.m.–September 20, 5 p.m. | Scheduled for the first week of the fall semester; no course changes would be accepted afterward |

The attached *Course-Selection Process and Handling Rules (ver. 2.0)* stated that the summer semester had only the first four stages and no fall-style adjustment period. Priority numbers applied during the first-selection stage. Second selection and add/drop were generally handled on a first-come, first-served basis, while online applications were processed with reference to factors including year level and credits already earned. Classes that did not reach their minimum enrollment could be canceled, and over-enrolled classes could be adjusted or randomly reduced.

Students were asked to select courses carefully according to their study plans and circumstances and to consult their academic advisers. Some major courses might not be suitable for students from every major or year. A course selected but not assessed, or failed, would still be recorded in the student’s academic file. Students unable to add or drop a course because of special circumstances, or applying for a dual degree, a retake conflict or an overload, had to submit a course-change application in the system, print it, obtain the required instructor and other signatures, and submit it through their academic administrator. Students were warned against unauthorized use of another person’s account and substitute or self-signed signatures.

The notice said that the Academic Affairs Office would issue the official course-opening plan after the fall adjustment period. The Chinese body used the phrase “spring course-opening plan” at this point, which conflicts with the fall-semester context; the discrepancy is retained here rather than silently corrected.

The core elective **Humanistic Care in the Fables of *Zhuangzi* and the Stories of the Bible**, class 64469, also changed its eligibility. It was originally open to students in the 2010 and 2011 cohorts and was expanded to include the 2012 cohort. Students in the 2012 cohort could select it during the same second-round and add/drop periods; the class meets on Wednesdays during periods 3–4.

## Textbook Ordering

Textbook ordering would be open from **9 a.m. on May 24 through 5 p.m. on June 2**, alongside the first and second course-selection stages. The textbook system was a separate subsystem. Students had to log in to the credit-based system, confirm whether to order the designated books for selected classes, and review and maintain their orders under the current textbook-order list.

After the ordering deadline, the Academic Affairs Office would generate the order and submit it to the bookstore. No additions, returns or changes to textbook orders would be available during the add/drop or fall adjustment periods. Students had to confirm their lists by 5 p.m. on June 2 and pay for and collect the books in the following semester according to the confirmed orders. Listed prices were estimates; “0.00” meant that a price was pending. Since the summer 2012 semester, books other than the “Two Courses” textbooks had been ordered at **76% of list price**. No university unit, organization or individual other than the Academic Affairs Office was permitted to order student textbooks.
