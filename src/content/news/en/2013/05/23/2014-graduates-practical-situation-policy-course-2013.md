Shantou University’s Department of Social Sciences planned to offer the university-wide required **Practical Situation and Policy Education** course to main-campus undergraduate students graduating in 2014 during the **fall 2013 semester**. The course carries **16 class hours and one credit** and would be completed through a current-affairs commentary paper rather than scheduled classroom sessions.

## Course Arrangements

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Students | Main-campus undergraduates graduating in 2014, mainly the 2010 cohort and some graduates from other cohorts |
| Term | Fall 2013 |
| Course type | University-wide required course |
| Workload and credit | 16 class hours; 1 credit |
| Selection | Enter the credit-based system and select a topic assigned by the chosen instructor under “Guiding Courses” |
| Paper | About 3,000–5,000 Chinese characters on major domestic and international current affairs of the previous three years, submitted in the prescribed paper format |
| Deadline | December 31, 2013 |

During the course-selection period, 2014 graduates had to enter the credit-based system and then select a topic assigned by the relevant instructor under “Guiding Courses.” Students would complete the paper according to the topic and the instructor’s guidance and submit a printed copy to the instructor of their selected class. A passing grade was required to receive the credit.

The attachments included a *Current-Affairs Report and Commentary* paper template and the Ministry of Education’s higher-education Situation and Policy Education teaching points covering **the second half of 2010 through the first half of 2013**. The template’s personal-information fields are not reproduced here.

Because the course was offered to graduating students only once each fall, eligible students had to select it during the fall 2013 course-selection or supplementary-selection period. Students graduating early had to submit a separate paper application. The notice said that the same arrangement would also serve as a reference for undergraduate cohorts graduating after 2014.
