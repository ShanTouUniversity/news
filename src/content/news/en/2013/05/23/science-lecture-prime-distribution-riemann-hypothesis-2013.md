The Shantou University School of Science will host a lecture in its Science Lecture series at **7 p.m. on May 27, 2013**, in **E Staircase 201**. Professor Jin Xiaoqing, head of the Department of Mathematics at the University of Macau, will speak on **“Prime Distribution and the Riemann Hypothesis.”** The lecture is open to science and engineering undergraduates, graduate students, interested faculty members and researchers.

## Lecture Details

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Speaker | Professor Jin Xiaoqing, head of the Department of Mathematics at the University of Macau |
| Topic | Prime Distribution and the Riemann Hypothesis |
| Time | 7 p.m., May 27, 2013 |
| Venue | E Staircase 201 |
| Audience | Science and engineering undergraduates, graduate students, faculty members and researchers |

The lecture will introduce the basic theories of prime numbers and the Riemann zeta function and discuss figures and research associated with the Riemann hypothesis. The notice describes the hypothesis as the proposition that all meaningful solutions to an equation concerning prime numbers lie on a straight line. It was proposed by German mathematician Bernhard Riemann and remains a longstanding unsolved problem in mathematics.

Professor Jin’s research focuses mainly on large-scale scientific and engineering computation, including preconditioned conjugate-gradient methods for large Toeplitz systems and their applications to numerical solutions of partial and ordinary differential equations and linear least-squares problems. According to the notice, he had published more than 60 academic papers, received more than 200 citations from international peers, and been invited to international academic conferences on multiple occasions. He also gave a 45-minute invited presentation at the 2007 International Congress of Chinese Mathematicians in Hangzhou. He had published seven monographs, including *An Introduction to Iterative Toeplitz Solvers*, published by the Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics (SIAM), and *Developments and Applications of Block Toeplitz Iterative Solvers*, jointly published by Kluwer Academic Publishers and the Science Press.
