The fourth Ai Zhi Workshop hosted by the Department of Social Sciences will take place at **7 p.m. on Monday, June 3, 2013**, in the **Library Lecture Hall**. The theme is **“How Should We Understand Modern Neo-Confucianism?”** The speaker is Gan Chunsong, deputy dean, professor and doctoral supervisor at Renmin University of China&apos;s School of Philosophy. Faculty members and students are welcome to attend.

## Discussion Topics

The lecture will examine the historical development and contemporary significance of modern Neo-Confucianism. The notice states that Confucian thought was once regarded as an obstacle to China&apos;s social progress, but it continues to influence Chinese society through cultural identity, social psychology and other dimensions. A modern Neo-Confucian current emerged after the New Culture Movement, forming both an intellectual community that included figures such as Liang Shuming, Xiong Shili, Mou Zongsan and Tu Weiming in China and abroad, and a school of thought concerned with how Confucianism might engage with the development of modern China.

The lecture will also consider the significance and prospects of this current within the broader landscape of Chinese thought.
