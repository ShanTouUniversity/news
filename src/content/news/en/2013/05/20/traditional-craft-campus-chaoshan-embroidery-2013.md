Shantou University’s Enactus team launched a “Traditional Crafts on Campus” series, beginning with a public lecture and a second Chaoshan embroidery culture camp for students. The activities focused on learning, preserving and developing Chaoshan embroidery.

## Opening Lecture

The first lecture, “The Art of Needle and Thread: The Revival of Chinese Chaozhou Embroidery,” would be held from **2:30 to 4:30 p.m. on Wednesday, May 22**, in **E204 stepped lecture room**. The speaker was Hong Yujing.

Hong is a master of Chaoshan embroidery at the Shantou Dingjin Embroidery Heritage Center and a folk inheritor of the craft. She also served as a deputy to the People’s Congress of Jinping District, Shantou, and vice president of the Shantou Arts and Crafts Association. Through a live demonstration and hands-on instruction, she would introduce students to basic embroidery techniques, the craft’s historical development and its current preservation situation.

Chaoshan embroidery is a long-established handicraft from Chaozhou. The lecture would discuss how schools, public-welfare groups, businesses, government bodies and embroidery masters had participated in efforts to promote the craft’s preservation and development, encouraging more people to learn about and study the tradition.

## Chaoshan Embroidery Culture Camp

The second Chaoshan embroidery culture camp was organized by STU Enactus’s Chaoshan Embroidery Revival Project and would be held at the Shantou Dingjin Embroidery Heritage Center in two sessions:

- First session: **July 1–10, 2013**
- Second session: **September 5–15, 2013**

The application deadline was **May 31**. The application form and submission procedure are personal registration materials; applicants should follow the original OA notice. The form, student contacts and personal contact details are not reproduced here.
