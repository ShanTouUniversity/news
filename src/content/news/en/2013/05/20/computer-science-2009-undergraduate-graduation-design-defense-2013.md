## Requirements and Submission

Students taking part in the defense had to complete and print STU’s Undergraduate Thesis (Design) Defense Record and bring it to the session. The defense consisted of an **eight-minute maximum** PowerPoint presentation of the graduation design followed by approximately **five minutes** of questions from the defense committee. Students were also asked to arrive early at the designated laboratories to install and check their presentation materials.

After the defense, students were required to revise their work in accordance with the feedback, formally bind it under university requirements, and submit paper and electronic copies to their supervisors by **May 28**. Supervisors would collect the materials and submit them to the department. Students were required to remain at the university until their final grades had been confirmed so that errors in their work could be corrected promptly.

## Assessment

Graduation-design results would use five grades with written comments:

- **Excellent:** The task-book requirements were completed outstandingly, with original insights, a correct and well-structured plan, strong independent-work ability and accurate answers during the defense.
- **Good:** The task was completed comprehensively, with feasible views, a basically correct and well-structured plan and generally correct answers.
- **Average:** The task was basically completed and the views were generally correct, although non-critical errors remained; the plan was broadly reasonable and the student could generally answer questions.
- **Pass:** The task requirements were completed only marginally, with numerous unscientific errors or isolated non-critical fundamental errors and weak independent-work ability, but the student could answer basic questions.
- **Fail:** The basic task-book requirements were not completed, or were completed only with extensive dependence on the supervisor; substantial fundamental errors remained, concepts were unclear or major questions could not be answered.
