The 155th Engineering Research Forum at Shantou University’s College of Engineering will be held at **10 a.m. on May 22** in ACC Room 3. The topic was “Human-Centered Robotics: An Approach to Developing Co-Robots,” with Weihua Sheng of Oklahoma State University as the speaker.

## Talk Overview

As robots move closer to people’s living and working environments, developing co-robots that can live or work with humans has become an important research question. Sheng’s talk would present a human-centered approach to co-robot development and examine how wearable sensors, wireless communications and computing technologies support human-centered computing.

The presentation would focus on mining high-level human-context knowledge from the low-level, noisy data generated by wearable sensors. It would discuss using state-of-the-art wearable sensors, computers and machine-learning algorithms to understand human activities, gestures and intentions, helping to create more natural human-robot interaction and future co-robots, particularly human-service robots.

## Speaker

The notice’s main text described Weihua Sheng as a professor at Oklahoma State University, while the attached English biography identified him as an associate professor in the university’s School of Electrical and Computer Engineering and director of its Laboratory for Advanced Sensing, Computation and Control (ASCC Lab), at `http://ascc.okstate.edu`. This report preserves the discrepancy between the two source descriptions.

Sheng received a Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Michigan State University in May 2002 and M.S. and B.S. degrees in Electrical Engineering from Zhejiang University in 1997 and 1994. He taught in Kettering University’s Electrical and Computer Engineering Department from 2002 to 2006 before joining Oklahoma State University. He has authored more than 120 papers in major journals and international conferences, seven of which received best-paper or best-student-paper awards. His research interests include mobile robotics, wearable computing, human-robot interaction and intelligent transportation systems. His work has received support from the U.S. National Science Foundation, Department of Defense, Oklahoma Transportation Center and Department of Transportation. He is a senior member of IEEE and an associate editor of *IEEE Transactions on Automation Science and Engineering*.
