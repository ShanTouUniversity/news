Shantou University has issued the **Regulations on Undergraduate Student Status Management** (Shanda Fa〔2013〕No. 67). The regulations, which were discussed and approved by the University Academic Committee on January 10, 2013, apply to full-time undergraduates and take effect on **September 15, 2013**. The Interim Regulations on Undergraduate Student Status Management under the Credit System, issued in 2005, are repealed at the same time.

## Main Provisions

### Admission and Registration

New students must report on the required date with their admission notice and other documents specified by the university. Students who cannot report on time must request leave from the Admissions Office before the reporting date; leave normally may not exceed two weeks. Failure to request leave or failure to report after the approved period is treated as giving up admission. The university conducts an admission-qualification review within three months after enrollment. Students who pass the review are registered and obtain student status; student status obtained through fraud or malpractice is cancelled once verified.

A new student who is medically unfit to study on campus may retain admission eligibility for one year, but does not hold student status during that period. To resume admission, the student must apply to the Admissions Office before the next year&apos;s new-student reporting date with a certificate showing that a re-examination at a designated hospital has been passed, and must complete the admission procedures again.

The university organizes each academic year into summer, fall and spring semesters. Students must register with their department or school&apos;s academic office according to the academic calendar to obtain eligibility to study in that semester; summer and fall require only one registration each. A student who is absent from registration for one week without approved leave, or who exceeds an approved leave period by one week without force majeure, is handled as withdrawn. Students must pay fees and register as required. Students who fail to pay on time are suspended from taking courses, while students with financial difficulties may apply for loans or other assistance through the relevant procedures.

### Study Duration, Courses and Assessment

The university uses a credit system. Except for specially admitted undergraduates, the standard undergraduate program lasts four years, with a minimum study period of three years and a maximum of six years. Periods of retained student status or leave count toward the study period, except for military service. Students who complete all courses and other teaching activities in the curriculum, pass the required assessments, earn the required credits, and meet the conduct and physical-education requirements may graduate and apply for a degree in accordance with applicable rules.

Students select courses under the guidance of their academic advisers. In principle, the total number of credits selected in each spring or fall semester must be at least 10 and no more than 25; summer-semester enrollment may not exceed 6 credits. A course grade of at least 60 is required to earn course credits. Students who fail a required course must retake it. Students who fail an elective may retake it or choose another course that meets curriculum requirements. The university provides academic supervision based on accumulated failed credits, including credit controls, probation requirements and withdrawal where applicable.

A student who cannot attend an examination for a valid reason must apply in advance for a deferred examination. Approval is required from the course instructor, department head and school dean, followed by approval from the Academic Affairs Office; the student then takes the deferred examination arranged by the Academic Affairs Office. Unexcused absence from an examination, cheating, and plagiarism in assignments, course papers or graduation theses are recorded and handled under the disciplinary provisions.

### Changing Majors and Transferring

A student may generally change majors only once during enrollment. Applications are limited to the first and second spring semesters and the second fall semester after admission. Each major must announce available places and requirements by the fifth week of the relevant semester. Application materials are generally submitted to the receiving department by the eighth week, sent to the Academic Affairs Office for review by the tenth week, and followed by a university decision before the twelfth week. An approved change takes effect at the beginning of the following semester.

The regulations also list circumstances in which transfer is prohibited, including limits related to time since admission, admission batch, special admission requirements and cases requiring withdrawal. They specify credit recognition and fee arrangements for transfer students. Students who transfer schools or majors pay fees according to the requirements of the receiving major.

### Leave, Retained Status and Withdrawal

Students may or must take leave for illness, when they cannot participate in at least one-third of the teaching hours in a semester, or for other special reasons. Leave is taken in units of one semester or one academic year and may not exceed two academic years in total. Students must apply for resumption or further leave after the leave period. Students enlisted in the Chinese People&apos;s Liberation Army or the Chinese People&apos;s Armed Police Force may retain student status until one year after discharge; military service does not count toward the study period.

Withdrawal may be handled when a student reaches the academic-failure threshold, fails to complete the program within six years of admission, does not apply for resumption or further leave after leave ends, is absent from required teaching activities for two consecutive weeks without leave, fails to register for more than one week without a valid reason, is medically unable to continue studying, or requests withdrawal. Withdrawal proceeds through review by the department or school and deliberation by the Academic Affairs Office. Students who disagree with cancellation of admission, withdrawal or a disciplinary decision may appeal under the student appeal procedures. The regulations provide five working days to appeal to the university Student Appeal Committee after delivery of a proposed withdrawal notice. After the university makes a final withdrawal decision, the student may submit a written appeal to the Guangdong Provincial Department of Education within 15 working days of receiving the decision.

### Discipline and Graduation Certificates

The five disciplinary sanctions are warning, serious warning, demerit, probation and expulsion. Serious disruption of teaching or educational order, serious plagiarism of research results, taking an examination for another person, organizing cheating, repeated examination cheating after a prior sanction, and other serious cheating may lead to expulsion.

Students who complete all courses in the curriculum within the prescribed study period, earn the required credits, and meet conduct and physical-education requirements receive a graduation certificate after application and review. Students who complete the courses but earn at least 90 percent of the credits required by their major may apply for a completion certificate. Graduation or completion applications generally must be submitted within the first five weeks of the spring or fall semester in which the student expects to complete the required credits. Students expected to meet bachelor&apos;s-degree requirements apply for the degree at the same time.

Academic and degree certificates are issued at the end of the spring semester. Students approved for graduation in the fall semester first complete departure procedures and return at the end of the following spring semester to collect their certificates. A student who completes the program without all required credits may apply to make up credits within one year after leaving with a completion certificate; after meeting graduation requirements, the completion certificate may be exchanged for a graduation certificate. A student who has studied for at least one academic year before withdrawal receives a certificate of incomplete study. Students expelled from the university receive only proof of study, and students without student status receive no graduation, completion or incomplete-study certificate.

The full regulations contain 54 articles and are available in the original OA attachment. The Academic Affairs Office is responsible for interpretation.
