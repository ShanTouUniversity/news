Shantou University’s Changjiang School of Journalism and Communication has opened registration for its **summer 2013 Public-Welfare Photography** course. The notice states that the course is open to **40 undergraduates** university-wide in one class, and students who complete the course and pass the assessment will receive **one credit**. The attached syllabus describes the student group as “all university students,” which differs slightly from the notice’s reference to undergraduates; the university’s arrangement is authoritative.

The course combines photography with public-welfare practice, encouraging students to document fishing-village culture and customs, urban intangible cultural heritage, and marine or wetland environments and endangered animals. It covers photography fundamentals, social-research methods, public-welfare photography, team training, project design, field photography and reflection.

## Course Schedule

| Dates | Content | Hours |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| July 1–3 | Theoretical training and project design | 12 |
| July 4–7 | Service practice, primarily photography work in Nan’ao | 32 |
| July 8 | Review, exhibition and experience sharing | 4 |
| **Total** | Total course hours | **48** |

Practice locations include the STU campus and Nan’ao. Students will work in groups on public-welfare photography projects and, according to service recipients’ needs, produce visual materials, hold an exhibition or submit work to the media.

## Registration

- **Applicants**: The notice is addressed to undergraduates university-wide; students who enjoy photography and are committed to public service are encouraged to apply.
- **Places**: 40
- **Deadline**: Before noon on June 2, 2013
- **Requirement**: Students with a digital single-lens reflex camera receive priority; members of the 2012 cohort should ensure that course activities do not conflict with freshman military training.
- **Selection results**: The selected-student list will be posted on the university office information network.

Assessment combines evaluations by course and teaching assistants with evaluations by the public-welfare service host and beneficiaries, weighted at 70% and 30%, respectively. Registration instructions, the official submission channel and the course syllabus should follow the original OA attachments.

## Follow-up Selection

On **June 4**, the college announced the list of students who met the registration requirements. Because a small number of places remained available, interested students could select the course through the credit-system platform; the specific selection period was to be announced by the Academic Affairs Office. The student list contains personal information and is not reproduced here.
