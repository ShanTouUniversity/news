The 264th STU Law School lecture will be held from **3 to 4 p.m. on Tuesday, May 14, 2013**, in **Room 213 of the Law School**. Wong Hoi Yu Winnie, a barrister of the Hong Kong Bar Association and a lawyer of the Law Society of Upper Canada, will deliver the English-language lecture **“Property rights of indigenous males in New Territories.”** Faculty members and students from across the university are welcome.

The lecture will examine the legal background of land rights held by indigenous residents of the New Territories and their relationship with Hong Kong's modern legal system. Wong's doctoral thesis examines the development of Hong Kong's legal system since 1843, including the interaction of English law with Chinese traditional laws and customs and the continued application of traditional rules to land inheritance among indigenous residents of the New Territories.

### Speaker profile

Wong Hoi Yu Winnie was called to the Hong Kong Bar in 1996 and admitted to the Law Society of Upper Canada in 2003. She practiced litigation in Hong Kong, handling civil matters involving commercial contracts, real-estate sale and purchase agreements, intellectual property, trusts, probate, administrative law, family law and child custody. She also represented defendants in criminal cases and represented the government in prosecutions. Since October 2003, she has practiced independently in Toronto, providing legal advice on family law, immigration, wills, estates and commercial contracts.

Her qualifications include a Bachelor of Science from the University of Toronto, a Bachelor of Education from the University of Windsor, the Hong Kong Common Professional Examination Certificate in Laws, a Postgraduate Certificate in Laws and a Master of Laws from the University of Hong Kong, a Certificate of Qualification from the Federation of Law Societies of Canada, and the Bar Admission Course of the Law Society of Upper Canada. She received a PhD in law from Peking University in June 2011.

Her doctoral thesis examines the formation and development of Hong Kong's legal system since 1843, including the influence of English law and Chinese traditional laws and customs in marriage, inheritance and adoption, as well as the retention of traditional laws and customs governing land inheritance among indigenous residents of the New Territories. Wong has also been invited to introduce Hong Kong's legal system and specific legal issues at universities in China, including Peking University, Renmin University of China, Minzu University, Business University, Agricultural University and Huazhong University of Science and Technology.
