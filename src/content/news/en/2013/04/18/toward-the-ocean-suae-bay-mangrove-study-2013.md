The **Toward the Ocean** public-service course at Shantou University will take enrolled students to the Suai Bay mangroves on **Saturday, April 20, 2013**, for a survey of the current condition of the Shantou Suai Bay mangrove ecosystem. Participants will meet at the STU motto monument at **8:50 a.m.** and travel together by bus to the study area.

### Field-study guidance

Participants should bring drinking water, lunch, a hat, sun protection and rain gear. Sportswear and sports shoes are recommended. Everyone should protect the environment by not littering, entering protected mangrove areas without permission or picking branches and leaves. Because mangroves provide habitat for migratory birds, participants should keep quiet and avoid disturbing them.

The activity will be automatically canceled in rain or other extreme weather, with rescheduling announced separately.

### About Suai Bay&apos;s mangroves

A mangrove ecosystem consists of mangrove plants growing on tropical coastal mudflats and their surrounding environment. It provides ecological functions including coastal protection and habitat for animals living on tidal flats. The notice describes the Suai Bay Mangrove Reserve as lying in the Suai Bay wetland south of Nanbin Road in Shantou. It contains what the notice identifies as one of the larger, northernmost and relatively intact natural secondary stands of Kandelia mangroves found in Guangdong, and is also one of China&apos;s three major migratory-bird areas.

The notice says that land reclamation, coastal aquaculture, logging and environmental pollution have reduced mangrove areas, while urbanization along Shantou&apos;s coast has placed additional pressure on the ecosystem. The course hopes that field study will encourage greater public attention to the condition of mangrove habitats.
