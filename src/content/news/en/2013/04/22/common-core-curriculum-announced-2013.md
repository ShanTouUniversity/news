Shantou University has released its *Common Core Curriculum*. The scheme was discussed and approved by the University Academic Committee on **April 16, 2013**, and took effect upon publication. It defines the common core as a program for educating citizens of modern society: students are expected to understand social responsibility, engage with Chinese and world culture, develop scientific thinking and use reliable knowledge and sound values to analyze and address real-world problems.

### Course requirements

Each student must complete at least **12 common-core courses totaling no fewer than 27 credits**:

- At least 3 courses must be selected from Citizenship and Society;
- At least 5 courses must be selected from Culture and Values;
- At least 2 courses must be selected from Science and Scientific Methods.

The four political courses marked “★” are compulsory, including two courses in the first field and two in the second. Integrated-thinking courses in the third field, art-education courses in the second field and public-service courses in the second field, marked “▲”, are also compulsory. Core courses offered by a student&apos;s own department do not count toward common-core credits.

### Three curriculum fields

#### Citizenship and Society

This field contains 2 course groups. It emphasizes patriotic and socialist education, civic awareness, knowledge of China&apos;s conditions, and modern concepts of democracy and justice, while encouraging rational discussion rather than one-way indoctrination.

1. **Theories of Socialism with Chinese Characteristics**: Students study China&apos;s historical, social and political context and the values and policies of reform and development. Main courses include “★Introduction to Mao Zedong Thought and the Theoretical System of Socialism with Chinese Characteristics” and “★Outline of Modern Chinese History.”
2. **Political Philosophy**: Students study basic social and political concepts and major theories and apply them to practical questions.

#### Culture and Values

This field contains 5 course groups and aims to help students understand human culture and values, develop a multicultural outlook and reflect on complex ethical questions.

1. **Cultural Inheritance and Innovation**: This area includes Chinese culture and world culture. Topics include the development of Chinese culture, ancient Chinese intellectual traditions, Confucianism and Daoism, China&apos;s modern cultural transformation, Christianity and Western society, ancient Greece and Rome, the Enlightenment, modern Western thought, Islamic civilization and multiculturalism. “★Basic Principles of Marxism” is compulsory.
2. **Aesthetics and Interpretation**: Through aesthetic analysis and textual interpretation of literature, drama, film, visual art and music, students develop the sensitivity needed to understand human life and experience. “▲Art Education” is compulsory.
3. **Values and the Self**: This area includes value reasoning and human nature and life. Value-reasoning courses discuss beliefs, values and contested ethical questions. “★Ideological and Moral Cultivation and the Fundamentals of Law” and “▲Public-Service Courses” are compulsory. The human-nature-and-life area covers psychological analysis, the mind, love, death, happiness, the philosophy of life, divinity and human nature.

#### Science and Scientific Methods

This field contains 6 course groups. It aims to cultivate a scientific spirit and help students understand scientific inquiry, the history and philosophy of science, and the use of scientific thinking to address practical problems.

1. **Scientific Methods**: This area includes the history and philosophy of science, social-science methods, and critical thinking and quantitative reasoning. Topics include the history of science and technology, basic ideas in the philosophy of science, scientific analysis of social phenomena, ideal models and value neutrality in social-science research, and methods of critical thinking and quantitative reasoning. “▲Integrated Thinking” is compulsory.
2. **Scientific Fields**: This area includes economy and society, the physical world and the life world. Economy and society covers core concepts, theories and research methods in economics, management, political science and sociology. The physical-world area covers core concepts, principles and methods in fields such as physics and chemistry. The life-world area covers core concepts, principles and methods in life science and the connections between living systems and other systems.

### Implementation

The university organizes courses into groups under the three fields. Instructors develop courses according to each group&apos;s objectives and content, and courses open after review. The scheme encourages research- and inquiry-based courses and discourages introductory courses that remain at the level of factual instruction. Humanities courses may use guided reading of classic works or organize learning around a theme or question; science courses may use topic-based research.

Teaching-method reform is a central part of the common-core program. Recommended formats include lectures combined with discussion, reading combined with discussion, student group and independent learning, experiments and inquiry-based learning. The aim is to help students use knowledge to examine complex questions about life, society and the material world from multiple perspectives.

### Distinguished-speaker lecture courses

The university will offer distinguished-speaker lecture courses through its General Education Forum, inviting well-known scholars from outside the university. Students must attend **three lectures during four years** and, after each lecture, complete related reading, discussion and assignments under the guidance of an on-campus instructor. Each lecture course requires at least **18 in-class hours** and is worth 1 credit toward the common-core requirement.
