Shantou University Academic Affairs Office has published **Teaching Bulletin No. 2 of 2013**, the 15th issue, covering university developments, teaching information, education-reform projects and student competition results. The 22-page issue contains sections on major news, academic-affairs developments, teaching information, student achievements and education developments.

### Major news

On April 10, 2013, the university invited experts from MyCOS Data Co., Ltd. to interpret the *Annual Report on Shantou University Graduates&apos; Educational Quality and Social Demand*. Based on a survey of STU&apos;s 2011 undergraduate graduates, the report compared relevant STU data with national averages for 211 universities and provided a third-party reference for identifying strengths and weaknesses in talent development. The bulletin states that several university indicators performed well overall, while continued improvement was needed in areas identified by the report.

The university also recommended 11 teaching achievements for review in the Seventh Guangdong Higher Education Teaching Achievement Awards. The achievements covered CDIO-based training for outstanding engineers, internationally oriented medical education, public-service courses, medical professional development, applied journalism education, clinical-skills teaching, high-quality teaching platforms, business-management education, introductory design courses, public-art teaching and peer-assisted student learning.

The Ministry of Education announced the 2012 filing and approval results for undergraduate programs, and the bulletin relayed that 258 programs at more than 60 universities could not enroll students. It also reported newly approved programs, filed programs and nationally controlled programs. STU&apos;s 2012 applications for Optoelectronic Information Science and Engineering and Social Work were filed by the Ministry of Education.

### Academic-affairs developments

**Inspection and acceptance of the 11th Five-Year Plan quality projects.** Seven projects at the main campus passed the first round of inspection or acceptance, while one project was suspended after its responsible person left. The projects that passed included the EIP-CDIO Engineering Education Innovation Experimental Zone, the CDIO engineering-education reform teaching team for mechatronics, the experimental zone for innovative art-and-design education at a comprehensive university, the innovative integrated-thinking business-management training experimental zone, a collective undergraduate innovation experiment, the Excellent Course *Introduction to Civil Engineering Design* and the national EIP-CDIO Engineering Education Innovation Experimental Zone. The *Numerical Analysis* excellent-course project was suspended.

**Inspection and acceptance of university teaching-reform projects.** Academic Affairs reviewed 49 projects from 2009 and 2011, including delayed 2009 projects and 2011 projects. Of the seven 2011 projects seeking early completion, three completed their research and were closed, while four passed an interim review and continued. The two delayed 2009 projects completed their planned work and were closed. Thirty-eight interim-review projects passed and were scheduled to receive their second installment of funding, while two projects were suspended. The projects covered English-major cultural teaching, paperless examinations, service-learning courses, practical legal education, journalism graduation-design reform, deep learning in university classrooms, logical-thinking training, CDIO engineering education, general-education courses, public computing, art and design, practical legal teaching and the sports-club model.

**2013 Guangdong quality-engineering applications.** The university completed its recommendations, covering three pilot comprehensive major reforms, one high-quality video open course, five upgraded high-quality resource-sharing courses, two newly developed high-quality resource-sharing courses, two experimental-teaching demonstration centers, five off-campus undergraduate practice bases and 14 higher-education teaching-reform projects. Recommended areas included outstanding journalism and communication talent training, visual communication design, nursing, civil-engineering materials, medical and biological science courses, university-enterprise practice bases and multiple course-reform projects.

**Undergraduate innovation and entrepreneurship training.** Academic Affairs and Student Affairs jointly organized the 2013 project call. Projects were divided into innovation training, entrepreneurship training and entrepreneurial practice. The main campus selected **53 projects**, and on April 15 recommended all 53 as provincial projects. The projects were expected to complete final review by the end of 2014, with Academic Affairs continuing to provide implementation management and support. The project list contains student names and project-leader information; this article reports only the totals, categories and overall schedule.

### Teaching information

The fall 2012 classroom teaching-quality assessment began in February 2013. It covered **507 instructors, 824 course offerings and 1,093 classes**, with **5,139 students** participating. The average score for general theoretical-course indicators was **90.75**, while the average for general laboratory-course indicators was **94.81**. The laboratory-course average increased by **1.31 points** from the previous semester. The largest improvement, 1.68 points, was recorded for instructors&apos; proficiency with laboratory instruments or software tools; the indicator concerning careful, prompt and constructive laboratory-report feedback rose by 1.63 points.

In the spring semester of the 2012–2013 academic year, the university offered **1,089 classes**, with **45,320 seats** and **40,093 course registrations**. General-education courses accounted for 204 classes, 7,854 seats and 6,929 registrations. Across the university, 34 new major courses and 20 new general-education courses were offered.

The second phase of classroom renovation was completed and accepted in early March 2013. Staircase classrooms E203 and E204 were converted into two U-shaped multimedia classrooms with 103 seats each. E203 received a remote high-definition video-education system for recording lessons and interactive distance teaching. Multimedia equipment, air conditioning, monitoring, access-control systems and desks and chairs were also replaced in classrooms on the second and third floors of Building E, and the walls were repaired and repainted. The university planned to renovate the entire fourth floor during the 2013 summer holiday.

### Student achievements

STU&apos;s Mathematics Department entered the 2013 Mathematical Contest in Modeling for the first time with **24 undergraduates in eight teams**. The teams won **one Meritorious Winner award and three Honorable Mention awards**.

At the Second Guangdong Undergraduate Engineering Training Comprehensive Ability Competition, which also served as the Guangdong selection event for the national competition, STU sent **seven teams**. The university won first prizes in the 8-shaped and S-shaped events, four second prizes and one third prize, and again advanced to the national competition.

### Education developments

The issue also featured a teaching-reform article on Beijing University of Civil Engineering and Architecture&apos;s use of real engineering cases in its *Civil Engineering Construction* course. The approach built a resource library with more than 60 projects, including the National Grand Theatre and the CCTV headquarters, supported by construction videos, animations, textbooks, a course website and three-dimensional simulation demonstrations. Classroom teaching, social practice, production internships and graduation projects were connected into a progressive training process. The article also reported that the university had sent more than 20 graduates in five cohorts overseas for internships in Angola, Rwanda, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and Mauritius since 2009.

The bulletin closes with a call for submissions on undergraduate teaching developments, teaching achievements, teaching experience, teaching information and domestic or international teaching theories and methods. Submissions could be sent to `o_jwc@stu.edu.cn`; the listed telephone numbers were `82902406` and `86502406`.
