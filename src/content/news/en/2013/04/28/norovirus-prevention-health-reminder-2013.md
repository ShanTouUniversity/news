After norovirus-related infectious diarrhea was reported at some universities in Guangzhou and at schools in Jieyang, STU's Resource Management Office issued a health reminder asking faculty, staff and students to strengthen food, environmental and personal hygiene practices as part of campus public-health prevention.

The notice describes norovirus infectious diarrhea as an illness with sudden onset and relatively rapid spread. Symptoms listed include nausea, vomiting, fever, abdominal pain and diarrhea. The original notice says that infected people generally recover within one to two days; anyone developing digestive symptoms should seek diagnosis and treatment at the campus hospital. It lists gastrointestinal, contact and airborne transmission as possible routes and identifies contaminated food and environments as important sources of contamination.

### Daily prevention measures

- Avoid undercooked food, especially undercooked seafood and salads; do not eat spoiled, unhygienic, raw, cold or raw-cured food;
- Keep indoor spaces ventilated and clean, and remove breeding grounds for flies and cockroaches;
- Wash hands frequently, maintain healthy routines, eat a balanced diet and get adequate sleep and rest;
- Drink plenty of water, avoid unclean fruits and vegetables, and do not drink untreated water;
- Avoid crowded places where possible; when traveling, prepare clothing, food and drinking water, and people with underlying conditions should bring their regular medicines;
- Seek medical care promptly if fever, abdominal pain, diarrhea or other discomfort develops.

### Historical background in the notice

The notice says that since 2012, the “Sydney2012” norovirus variant had caused substantial outbreaks in Europe and the United States, and that the variant had been detected in Guangzhou, Foshan, Zhongshan and Dongguan in Guangdong in 2012 and 2013. It therefore reported that the variant was circulating in the Pearl River Delta and projected that the intensity of norovirus gastroenteritis in Guangdong in 2013 might exceed that of 2012. At the same time, the notice said there was then no evidence that the variant increased the proportion of severe diarrhea cases.

These epidemiological background details and projections are historical information from the 2013 notice and do not represent a current assessment. Health concerns should be addressed according to advice from the campus hospital or a qualified medical provider.
