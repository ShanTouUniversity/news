Shantou University Research Affairs has issued **issue 1 of the 2013 Research and Innovation Newsletter**, the 27th issue overall. The issue reviews project applications, research achievements and awards, academic exchanges, local services and university-industry cooperation during the first quarter of 2013.

### Research project applications

By the end of March, STU had submitted **213 National Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC) applications**: 129 general programs, 66 young-scientist programs, five key programs, one major research plan, two Distinguished Young Scholars programs, three Excellent Young Scientists programs, three joint-fund programs and four other applications.

| NSFC division | Applications |
| --- | ---: |
| Mathematical and Physical Sciences | 10 |
| Chemical Sciences | 10 |
| Life Sciences | 26 |
| Earth Sciences | 6 |
| Engineering and Materials Sciences | 17 |
| Information Sciences | 15 |
| Management Sciences | 6 |
| Medical Sciences | 123 |

The university had also submitted **100 longitudinal humanities and social-sciences projects**, including applications to the National Social Science Fund, the Ministry of Education humanities and social-sciences program, the General Administration of Sport humanities and social-sciences program, the Ministry of Justice legal-research program, the Ministry of Civil Affairs social-work and volunteer-service program, and Guangdong Provincial Party Committee Publicity Department calls.

The newsletter states that 28 applications were submitted to the National Social Science Fund, with a listed breakdown of 18 general, 11 young-scientist and one arts project. Those subcategories total 30, so the internal discrepancy in the source is retained here rather than silently corrected. The Ministry of Education program accounted for 60 applications: 31 planning-fund projects, 27 young-scientist-fund projects and two special projects.

Five 2012 humanities and social-sciences projects recently received funding. Fifteen projects completed their research and passed completion review, while seven more entered the completion process.

### Achievements and honors

The issue reports one third prize in the Ministry of Education&apos;s Sixth Outstanding Research Achievements Awards for Higher Education (Humanities and Social Sciences), and three Guangdong Philosophy and Social Sciences Outstanding Achievements Awards for 2010–2011, including one first prize and two third prizes then under publicity. STU faculty published 18 papers indexed by SSCI in 2012, including 10 with STU faculty as corresponding authors, and one paper indexed by A&HCI with an STU corresponding author.

Other achievements and honors included:

- Peng Xiaoyan of the School of Liberal Arts was selected for the Ministry of Education&apos;s 2012 New Century Excellent Talents Support Program;
- Wang Yi of the Business School and collaborators published “IT Capability and Organizational Performance: The Roles of Business Process Agility and Environmental Factors” online in the SSCI-indexed *European Journal of Information Systems*;
- Chen Weizhou of the Key Laboratory of Marine Biology received the title of National Outstanding Science and Technology Worker;
- Kui Fei received a national honor for outstanding science popularization in the social sciences;
- a work by Xie Lin received second prize in the Eighth China Federation of Literary and Art Circles Literary Criticism Award, while a work by Zeng Jianping received third prize in a Guangdong photography-theory seminar collection;
- Melitta Schachner, director of the STU Medical College Neuroscience Center, was elected a member of the German Academy of Sciences;
- Wu Yong, director of the Design Department at the Cheung Kong School of Art and Design, became a member of the Alliance Graphique Internationale (AGI); and
- Chen Zhiming was appointed an adviser to the second Academic Committee of the Chaoshan Historical and Cultural Research Center, while Wang Shouzhi was appointed to the Development and Planning Group of the Expert Committee for the 10th China (Wuhan) International Garden Expo.

### Local service and science cooperation

The issue also reports several partnerships:

- On January 15, STU and the Shantou Haojiang District People&apos;s Government signed a science-and-technology cooperation agreement. STU also began product-development cooperation with Guangdong Jinyuan Technology Co., Ltd. and Shantou Huatianfu Industrial Development Co., Ltd. on high-performance heat-resistant thermoplastic elastomers and printed-circuit-board recording materials;
- on February 5, Law School faculty attended a Spring Festival symposium for legal advisers to the Chaozhou Municipal People&apos;s Government and offered suggestions on innovative management, traffic management and administration according to law;
- on March 1, the Shantou Federation of Trade Unions and the STU Legal Aid Research Center launched a cooperation project offering legal consultations, visitor reception and case representation within the center&apos;s capacity;
- on March 13, Business School experts advised Nan&apos;ao County on a “single-ticket” tourism system and integrated service-management plan; and
- on March 22, STU and the Shantou Cosmetics Industry Association signed an agreement to promote technological transformation and innovation in the cosmetics industry.

Other reported activities included a visit from the South China Sea Institute of Oceanology, the annual Mathematics Department and Institute report meeting, a chemistry-discipline strategy workshop, the successful review of STU&apos;s 2012 science and social-sciences statistical reports, a research-awards meeting, a research-project application seminar and a visit by a Chaozhou municipal delegation.
