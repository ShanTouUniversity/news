### About the speaker

Zhang Yongyu received a master&apos;s degree in biochemistry and molecular biology from the STU School of Science in 2005 and a doctorate in marine microbial ecology from Xiamen University in 2009. He studied and conducted collaborative research at the University of Maryland from October 2006 to June 2007 and has worked at the Chinese Academy of Sciences Institute of Urban Environment since July 2009.

His research covers interactions between marine bacteria and viruses, environmental genomics and proteomics, microbial ecology and environmental toxicology. He has led and participated in projects supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China, the Ministry of Science and Technology&apos;s international cooperation program, the 973 Program and the China Ocean Mineral Resources Research and Development Association, and has published nearly 20 papers in Chinese and international journals.

His current projects include an NSFC Young Scientists project on marine Roseobacter and virus interactions, a subproject of a national major research program on carbon fractionation and storage by algalytic bacteria in the marine microbial carbon pump, and an open project on the role of marine viruses in gene transfer and co-evolution among microorganisms.

His representative work addresses host responses of marine Roseobacter to phage infection, interactions between marine microorganisms and phages, and the isolation and characterization of marine Roseobacter phages.
