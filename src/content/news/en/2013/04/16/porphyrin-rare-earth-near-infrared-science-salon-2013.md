### Report topics

The report will address the photophysical properties of rare-earth porphyrin compounds and their potential use as fluorescent probes and biomedical-related materials. The abstract covers:

- donor-acceptor rare-earth ytterbium (III) porphyrin compounds with near-infrared emission, including energy transfer, linear and nonlinear photophysical properties and two-photon absorption;
- aqueous rare-earth porphyrin fluorescent probes for detecting mercury (II) ions, including fluorescence and lifetime responses in visible and near-infrared bands;
- water-soluble, cell-targeted rare-earth porphyrin compounds and their possible use in near-infrared cell imaging and photodynamic therapy; and
- the roles of different rare-earth compounds in cancer-cell recognition, near-infrared imaging, reactive-oxygen-species generation and magnetic-resonance-imaging-related research.

The abstract reports that some compounds respond to specific cell structures or membranes. It gives a 2.5% near-infrared luminescence quantum yield and an 18.1-microsecond fluorescence lifetime for Yb-Rh in aqueous solution. It also discusses Yb-N for near-infrared cancer-cell imaging, Gd-N for singlet-oxygen generation and the possible use of Gd-N as a magnetic-resonance contrast agent. These are research findings and directions described in the lecture abstract, not medical advice.
