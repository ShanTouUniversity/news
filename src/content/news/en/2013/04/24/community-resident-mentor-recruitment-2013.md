Shantou University's Community Resident Mentor Association opened a university-wide recruitment drive on April 24, 2013, for mentors serving Residence Halls A, B, C, D, E, F and G. Founded in September 2012 under the guidance of the Student Affairs Office's residential-counselor office, the association works to strengthen communication among residents, gather their views and organize community activities that support self-management and self-education.

Applications close at **midnight on May 3**, and the appointment period runs from **September 1, 2013, to June 30, 2014**. The selection process consists of voluntary application, document review, a democratic election within the relevant residential community, mentor training and formal appointment. The position is a university work-study role, with pay provided according to work-study standards.

### Eligibility and application

Applicants should communicate well, show a strong sense of rapport and be willing to serve residential communities. The notice also calls for responsibility, teamwork, communication skills, resilience and a genuine interest in personal development. Applicants must have had no failing courses during the previous academic year.

Applicants must download and complete the *STU Community Resident Mentor Association Application Form*, send the electronic version through the channel for the relevant residence area and submit a hard copy to the current resident mentors in that area. Because the area-specific addresses in the notice correspond to student contacts, those personal addresses are not published here. Questions may be sent to the Student Affairs Office's residential-counselor office at `o_fdy@stu.edu.cn`.

### Responsibilities

Community resident mentors are expected to:

- visit the assigned residential community at least once each week and learn about residents' academic, personal and daily-life needs, identifying issues that require attention;
- organize at least one community group activity each semester, encourage communication and help develop residential-community culture;
- conduct safety education and discipline guidance, reporting issues to counselors when they cannot be handled directly;
- assist with checks of cleanliness inside residences and in shared areas;
- help residents protect their rights and develop self-management practices;
- report emergencies promptly to counselors and assist with investigation and response; and
- complete other tasks assigned by the Student Affairs Office's residential-counselor office.

The application form asks for personal identity and contact details, academic information, awards and disciplinary history, extracurricular activities, student-leadership experience and a proposed work plan. These personal application details are not published here.
