Changjiang School of Arts and Design will host the 13th Kan Kan Culture event from **7:30 to 9:30 p.m. on April 26** at the Lecture Hall of the STU New Library. Titled “The Universality of Chinese Music Seen from Guinness World Records to New York Carnegie Hall: Half-Century Testimony of Maestro Yan Huichang,” the dialogue is open to the university community.

### Guests

Yan Huichang is an artistic director and chief conductor. In 1987, he became one of the first conductors to receive the national First-Class Conductor designation under China's professional rating system. He joined the Hong Kong Chinese Orchestra in 1997 and later received distinctions including Singapore's Cultural Award, the Bronze Bauhinia Star from the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, the Special Jury Award for Conducting at the Chinese Golden Record Awards and a Taiwan Chinese Literary and Art Award. He has taught or led master classes at institutions including the Central Conservatory of Music, Shanghai Conservatory of Music and the Hong Kong Academy for Performing Arts. He has also promoted Chinese-music education and professional-orchestra training, including an internship program developed by the Hong Kong Academy for Performing Arts and the Hong Kong Chinese Orchestra, an orchestra academy and an international Chinese-orchestra conducting competition.

Born in Macao and raised in Hong Kong, Zhou Guangzhen holds a PhD in music history from the University of Hong Kong and studied Chinese cultural history and ethnomusicology at the University of Hawaiʻi graduate school. He has worked as a senior writer for the *South China Morning Post*, a music columnist for *Asiaweek*, a classical-music presenter for Radio Television Hong Kong's Radio 4 and a critic for *Musical America*. He wrote *A History of the Central Philharmonic Orchestra, 1956–1996* and received a commendation from Hong Kong's Home Affairs Bureau in 2010 for his contribution to culture and the arts.

Wang Shouzhi is dean and professor of Changjiang School of Arts and Design at Shantou University. His research covers the history and theory of architecture, industrial products, graphic design, fashion, automobiles, urban planning, illustration and modern and contemporary art. He previously taught modern design theory and history as a tenured professor at ArtCenter College of Design in Pasadena and has taught or served as a visiting professor at institutions in art, design and architecture. His publications include multiple works on design history and cultural topics.

The organizer welcomes students, faculty and staff to attend the dialogue.
