The Ministry of Education has opened applications for its 2013 philosophy and social-sciences development-report projects. The program supports long-term tracking research by university scholars on major real-world issues and aims to produce distinctive, influential reports that provide policy advice for national and regional economic and social development.

The program has two categories: **development projects** and **cultivation projects**. An applicant may apply for only one category. Development projects receive **RMB 300,000 per year**, while cultivation projects receive **RMB 50,000 per year**. A development project has a three-year construction cycle; a cultivation project has a two- to three-year cultivation period.

### Scope and construction tasks

The program supports major issues in national economic, political, cultural, social and ecological development; regional development strategies and nationally designated economic zones, demonstration zones and pilot areas; global, regional and country studies with major implications for China&apos;s diplomatic strategy; and major reform and development tasks identified in the *National Medium- and Long-Term Plan for Education Reform and Development (2010–2020)*.

Project tasks include:

- preparing and publishing annual development reports based on original survey data and characterized by foresight, innovation, authority and research security;
- building stable, well-structured research teams through collaboration with government agencies, university and external research institutions and major industries;
- creating dynamic thematic databases based primarily on original data, with timely updates, standardized formats and controlled open sharing; and
- conducting applied policy research on urgent national and social issues and submitting forward-looking consulting reports and policy recommendations.

### Eligibility and review

For a development project, the proposed report title should substantially match the project guide, have continuity and have been published publicly for at least two consecutive years. The applicant must be a full-time, established university teacher, and the research and writing team should be stable. The report should have a clear theme, reliable data, rigorous methods, innovation and scientific and forward-looking value, and comply with research-integrity and academic-norm requirements.

A cultivation project may propose a report title or research field beyond the project guide, but the applicant must provide a rigorous justification. The team should have a sound structure, the capacity for long-term collaboration, prior accumulation and development potential, sufficient research funding and construction conditions, and strong institutional support in policy, funding and personnel.

Translations, reference books, papers and paper collections, textbooks, software, audio-visual products and similar forms of output are ineligible. Proposals involving academic misconduct or intellectual-property disputes are ineligible. Development-project applicants must also agree to unified publication organized by the Ministry of Education.

The Ministry will organize peer and practitioner experts to review proposals. Review criteria include political orientation, clear demand orientation, team structure, advanced research methods, thematic database development, and the applied value and social impact of results. Projects recommended by experts will be publicly announced and approved by the Ministry before formal launch. The Ministry will sign a project agreement with the project leader and the applicant&apos;s university covering the topic, research content, funding, publication schedule and dissemination.

### Management and output requirements

The Ministry and the applicant&apos;s university will jointly manage each project. The Ministry is responsible for overall planning, application review, inspection, evaluation and publication; the university must include the project in its key research plan, provide necessary conditions and conduct follow-up checks. Funding must be included in the university&apos;s consolidated budget, used for the approved purpose and subject to financial and audit supervision.

A project team should generally write and publish one high-quality annual development report each year. Reports should include an abstract, preface or afterword, contents, main text, appendices and references, and should generally be about 500,000 Chinese characters including charts. International-issues reports should include an English abstract and contents page. Development projects enter the Ministry&apos;s development-report series and are edited and published by a publisher selected by the Ministry; cultivation projects arrange publication independently and may not use the series&apos; unified branding or cover design. The publisher should issue the book within three months after receiving a complete, clear and final manuscript.

Each team should submit one or two current consulting reports of about 3,000 Chinese characters to the Ministry each year and release results through forums, presentations, seminars or other publication events. Confidential data and internal material unsuitable for public release must not be disseminated publicly.

Projects undergo annual inspections. After three years, development projects receive a comprehensive evaluation focused on report writing and publication, application and social impact, thematic databases, teamwork and collaborative innovation. Cultivation projects that pass their final assessment may become development projects. Funding may be reduced or suspended, a project may be placed in a cultivation period or required to rectify problems, or a project may be cancelled when a team cannot complete its tasks, substantially departs from its research direction, commits academic misconduct or falsifies data, or fails evaluation.

### STU application arrangements

Applicants must complete the *Application and Review Form for the Ministry of Education Philosophy and Social Sciences Development-Report Project* and send the electronic version to `yczhuo@stu.edu.cn` for formal screening by **May 24**. Qualified final documents must be delivered to the Academic Research Office by **May 31**, with electronic copies sent to the same address.

Required hard-copy materials are:

- **Seven copies** of the application and review form, with supporting evidence bound as attachments and printed double-sided on A4 paper; and
- **Four sets** of development reports from the previous two years for development-project applications.

Contacts are Huang Yumei and Zhuo Yanchun at `86502563`. The application form contains personal and internal fields, including the project leader&apos;s identity and contact details, team information and budget; those details are not reproduced here.
