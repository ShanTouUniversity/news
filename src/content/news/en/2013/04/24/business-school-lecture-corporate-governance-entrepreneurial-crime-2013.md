STU Business School will hold its fifth academic lecture of 2013 from **10:00 to 11:30 a.m. on Friday, April 26** in the Business School Academic Seminar Room on the second floor of Wenxi Building. Dr. Song Lihong of Sun Yat-sen University will speak on “Corporate Governance and Entrepreneurial Crime in the Context of a Transitional Economy.” Professor Zeng Qiang will chair the session, which is open to the university community.

### Research focus

Song Lihong's research examines corruption and crime among entrepreneurs during China's economic transition. The presentation will use data from **312 Chinese listed companies** to conduct an empirical analysis of the relationship between corporate governance and entrepreneurial crime.

According to the notice, the study found that shareholder returns and corporate risk helped restrain the occurrence of entrepreneurial crime to some extent; managerial self-confidence was associated with a higher incidence of crime; and independent directors did not show a significant preventive or supervisory effect on entrepreneurial crime. The study suggests that these findings may inform research on entrepreneurial development in management and entrepreneurship, as well as related policy development. These statements describe the findings reported for this empirical study and are not extended here into a general causal claim.

### Speaker

Song Lihong is a doctoral researcher at the School of Business at Sun Yat-sen University. She received a bachelor's degree in management from Northeastern University in 2008 and a master's degree in management from Sun Yat-sen University in 2010. She previously worked as a short-term research assistant at the Asia-Pacific Institute of Business at The Chinese University of Hong Kong. Her research interests include entrepreneurship and family businesses and corporate social responsibility. She has published seven related papers in Chinese core journals including the *Journal of Management*, *Journal of Jilin University* and *China Industrial Economics*. Two English-language papers were under review at the *Strategic Management Journal* and *Management and Organization Review* when the notice was issued.

She has also contributed to two book chapters on entrepreneurship and family businesses, presented nine papers at international and domestic academic conferences, participated in four National Natural Science Foundation of China projects, led one university doctoral research project and participated in multiple government or enterprise projects.
