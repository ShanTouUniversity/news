STU Academic Affairs Office has set the teaching arrangements for the 2013 summer and fall semesters. Each teaching unit should prepare course plans, including public-service courses, and complete textbook selection at the same time.

### Course planning

The summer semester is a formal term in STU’s three-term system. Its total teaching hours should generally be about 15–20 percent of those in the spring or fall semester. Units may schedule continuous practical teaching, public compulsory courses, general-education or major courses with unmet demand, and make-up offerings of compulsory courses. A student’s summer workload should generally not exceed **six credits**. Each public-service course is 48 hours and carries one credit.

In the fall semester, students may select no more than **25 credits** excluding public-service courses. Plans should enable most students to complete required courses within four years and address the needs of the 2013 entering class. Public-service courses held on weekends or holidays are also 48 hours and one credit. Intensive practical and internship courses should generally be scheduled in summer. The fall semester has 16 teaching weeks and the summer semester four.

Teaching units must submit summer and fall course plans in the credit-system platform by **May 4, the Saturday of Week 10**. Summer and fall public-service course plans are due to the Teaching Section by **May 17, the Friday of Week 12**. Written applications or filings for general-education courses, new courses, bilingual or English-taught courses and adjunct instructors are due to Academic Affairs Office by **April 26, the Friday of Week 9**.

### Faculty and textbooks

Units must follow the undergraduate course-setting and offering rules. Teaching personnel outside the university establishment or personnel-employment system are managed as adjunct instructors and may teach only after submitting an application approved by Academic Affairs Office. Bilingual or English-taught courses and new courses also require the relevant applications.

In principle, every course should have a textbook, ordered centrally by Academic Affairs Office. Instructors should select textbooks, obtain approval from the unit director or dean, and have academic staff register the information. Textbook information must be entered in the credit-system platform and linked accurately to courses by **May 10**. It cannot be changed after course selection and textbook ordering open to students. Units should also estimate textbook quantities for the 2013 entering class based on the preliminary enrollment figures.

After selection, units should submit printed textbook-registration, instructor-textbook-demand and new-student-textbook-demand forms to the Teaching Section and email electronic copies to `lxl0726@stu.edu.cn`. Use the latest formats in the attachments.
