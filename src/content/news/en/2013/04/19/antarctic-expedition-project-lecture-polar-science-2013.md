A lecture in Shantou University&apos;s 2014 Antarctic Expedition Project series will be held from **8:00 to 9:30 p.m. on Monday, April 22, 2013**, in Lecture Room 203 of the E Teaching Building. Dr. Yin-Xin Zeng, a researcher in the Biology and Ecology Laboratory of the Chinese Polar Research Center and the State Key Laboratory of Polar Science of the State Oceanic Administration, will present **“My Polar Scientific Expeditions.”**

### Fieldwork

Zeng has participated in or led the following polar and marine expeditions:

- China&apos;s first Arctic expedition, July–September 1999;
- a U.S. expedition in the northern Bering Sea, May–June 2007;
- summer fieldwork at the Yellow River Station in the Arctic, July–August 2008;
- South Korea&apos;s first Arctic expedition, July–August 2010;
- summer fieldwork at the Yellow River Station in the Arctic, July 2011;
- summer fieldwork at Great Wall Station in Antarctica, December 2011–January 2012.

### Speaker

Yin-Xin Zeng is a researcher in the Biology and Ecology Laboratory of the Chinese Polar Research Center and the State Key Laboratory of Polar Science of the State Oceanic Administration. He is also a master&apos;s supervisor at Jimei University. His research interests include polar and marine microorganisms, microbial molecular ecology and environmental microbiology, with research directions including microbial community structure and diversity, microbial ecological functions and enzymology.

Zeng studied microbiology at Sichuan University from 1990 to 1994 and received his bachelor&apos;s degree there. He earned a master&apos;s degree in microbiology from Xiamen University from 1994 to 1997 and a doctorate in microbiology from Xiamen University from 2007 to 2010. He was a visiting scholar at the University of Liège and held successive positions as an intern researcher, assistant researcher and associate researcher at the Chinese Polar Research Center before becoming a researcher in December 2007.

His research covers the diversity and ecological functions of polar marine microorganisms, polar cold-adapted bacteria and their enzymatic characteristics. He has led or participated in multiple National Natural Science Foundation and polar-science projects and has published research in international and Chinese journals.

Faculty and students interested in the topic are welcome to attend.
