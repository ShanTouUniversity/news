Following a decision by the university Academic Committee and its teaching-reform program, Shantou University Academic Affairs is inviting course proposals from teaching units across the university. From the 2013 cohort onward, the university will use new core courses as the main route for general education and plans to build a more complete core-curriculum system over two to three years.

### Three fields and 13 course groups

Proposed courses must fit one of the following groups and match its goals and content. Examples listed for a group are not fixed course titles: faculty may choose titles based on their course content or develop courses beyond the examples, provided that the proposals meet the group requirements.

| Field | Course groups |
| --- | --- |
| Citizen and Society | 1-1 Theory of Socialism with Chinese Characteristics; 1-2 Political Philosophy |
| Culture and Values | 2-1 Chinese Culture; 2-2 World Culture; 2-3 Aesthetics and Interpretation; 2-4 Value Reasoning; 2-5 Humanity and Life |
| Science and Scientific Methods | 3-1 History and Philosophy of Science; 3-2 Social Science Methods; 3-3 Quantitative Reasoning; 3-4 Economy and Society; 3-5 Physical World; 3-6 Life World |

The guide identifies the following designated courses: *Introduction to Mao Zedong Thought and the Theoretical System of Socialism with Chinese Characteristics* and *Outline of Chinese Modern History* in the Theory of Socialism with Chinese Characteristics group; *Basic Principles of Marxism* in the World Culture group; and *Foundations of Morality and Law* in the Value Reasoning group.

Existing general-education courses that fit the core-course objectives may be revised and submitted for inclusion in the core-course directory. From the 2013 cohort onward, existing general-education courses not included in the directory may be registered only as free electives and may not offset or reduce core-course credit requirements. The university also encourages new courses developed under the framework. Courses included in the core-course directory and receiving university support should be offered every semester.

### Teaching and syllabus guidance

The guide encourages research- and inquiry-based courses. Teaching should go beyond the transfer of knowledge and help students use theory to consider complex questions about life, society and the material world. Humanities courses may use guided reading of major works or organize teaching around a theme or question; science courses may use a focused-research format.

The guide discourages relying entirely on lectures and encourages combinations of lectures and discussion, reading and discussion, student group learning, independent study, experiments and inquiry-based learning. A syllabus should cover the course title, course group, credits, class hours, target students, objectives, teaching and assessment methods, textbooks, reference books and other learning resources. It should also specify course content, major viewpoints, independent-study content, discussion topics and the main assignments.

### Submission schedule

Faculty proposing a core course must submit the core-course application form and syllabus. One paper copy should be sent to the Teaching Section of Academic Affairs, with electronic materials emailed to `zzhang@stu.edu.cn`.

To allow core courses to open in the **fall 2013 semester**, materials should be submitted by **Friday, May 10**. If submission by that date is not possible, the materials may be submitted by **Monday, May 20**. The Core-Course Expert Group will review proposals, provide revision comments and return them to syllabus authors.

Academic Affairs provided the application form and syllabus templates with the notice; the forms are not reproduced here.
