STU has issued Document No. 54 of 2013 on graduate employment work. The university aims to improve employment quality and graduate satisfaction while maintaining the employment rate, with additional efforts in grassroots employment, self-employment, support for graduates facing employment difficulties and career guidance services.

The document reports that STU's 2012 initial employment rate was **97.34%** and its overall employment rate was **99.37%**. It cites the employment outlook for 2013, estimating 442,000 university graduates in Guangdong, with more than 650,000 job seekers in the province after including graduates from outside the province and graduates whose employment had previously been deferred.

### Employment priorities

STU has asked schools, colleges and relevant units to make graduate employment a top leadership responsibility, align their approach, involve all personnel, expand employment fields and improve the employment-service system.

#### Career guidance and services

The university will strengthen employment education and help graduates understand national and Guangdong employment policies and develop appropriate employment perspectives. The planned guidance includes:

- encouraging graduates to work or start businesses at the grassroots level in towns and below in economically less-developed areas of Guangdong, teach in rural schools, or take part in programs involving rural education, agriculture, health care and poverty alleviation;
- encouraging graduates to respond to national needs by working in Tibet, Xinjiang and other locations where they are needed; and
- publicizing policies for graduates who enlist for compulsory military service.

STU will continue to organize campus-wide job fairs, employer and supply-demand meetings and employer presentations. It will expand cooperation with human-resources and social-security authorities, industry associations and chambers of commerce to develop a more effective graduate employment market.

The university will improve its digital employment platform. In addition to the employment information website, email and graduate employment text-message service, it will strengthen online job-search guidance, provide online video interviews and explore the use of Weibo, WeChat and other channels to collect, publish and update employment information more quickly. Targeted assistance will be provided to graduates facing employment difficulties. STU will also strengthen job-search safety education, simplify employment-dispatch procedures and continue follow-up surveys of graduates and employers.

#### Career-guidance staff and courses

STU will develop the courses *Career Counseling and Training* and *Employment Guidance* to strengthen career-planning awareness and job-search skills. Career-planning education will be introduced earlier, especially for first-year students, and run throughout university education.

The career-guidance team will be developed as a relatively stable, professional team combining full-time and part-time staff. The university will arrange professional training for guidance personnel and invite alumni, scholars, experts and successful industry professionals to serve as career advisers.

#### Practice and entrepreneurship

Schools, colleges and relevant departments will continue developing employment internship bases and expanding university-enterprise cooperation. Internships and practical training are intended to build students' practical skills and workplace experience while helping employers and students understand one another.

STU will draw on alumni, industry associations and professional organizations to organize events such as the *Career Launch Distinguished Speakers Forum*, employment workshops and the “Business Leaders on Campus” series. These activities are intended to facilitate direct exchange between employers and students and broaden employment channels.

For innovation and entrepreneurship, STU will organize students to join innovation and entrepreneurship competitions, simulated business ventures and innovation-and-entrepreneurship training projects. Its entrepreneurship parks and practice bases will support entrepreneurial practice, internships and project incubation and help develop entrepreneurial teams and entities.

### Employment administration and incentives

STU will assist graduates facing employment difficulties, issue graduate employment incentives, identify and remove employment-related safety risks and simplify dispatch procedures. For 2013 undergraduate graduates at the main campus who take an active approach to employment, the university will continue the following incentive policies:

- students who start businesses and obtain a business license by November 30, 2013 may receive an award under the applicable rules;
- students who sign employment agreements early will receive an award according to the date the agreement is implemented, with the relevant date determined by the time it is stamped at the Student Affairs Office Graduate Employment Guidance Center; and
- students taking part in the Western Plan, the non-commissioned officer plan, the rural teaching plan or similar programs will receive the corresponding award under university and government policies.

The university will continue its two-year deferred-employment policy. For graduates who apply for deferred employment, files will be managed centrally by the Provincial Higher-Education Graduate Employment Guidance Center, while household-registration relations will remain in their original locations for two years. Graduates who find an eligible employer during the deferral period may complete employment-dispatch procedures. Those who remain without an employer after two years will be included in the employment plan for the year in which their deferral expires and will continue to receive employment recommendations from the graduate-employment authority of their place of origin.

The university Party Committee Organization and United Front Department will manage Party-organization relations for graduate Party members during deferred employment under the relevant Guangdong education authorities' rules. The document also calls for protecting the seriousness, authority and reputation associated with the Graduate Employment Agreement; students who have signed an agreement must fulfill its terms.

### 2013 schedule

| Period | Main arrangements |
| --- | --- |
| January–June | Provide routine career guidance; offer job-search subsidies for financially disadvantaged 2013 undergraduate graduates; hold a special job fair for alumni enterprises; contact and receive employers; provide job information; conduct career-guidance and career-development activities; train career-guidance teachers; collect, compile and report employment data. |
| April–May | Sign employment-work target responsibility agreements; hold career-guidance lectures; follow students without a confirmed employment intention; compile and submit the 2013 graduate employment plan to the provincial graduate employment guidance center; create and submit the 2014 graduate source-information database for review; have each unit help every graduate secure employment. |
| June | Distribute employment awards for graduates who meet the policy requirements; process registration certificates for the 2013 graduating class; conduct graduation education, commencement and alumni activities; prepare for graduates to leave, archive files and report employment figures before departure; continue monitoring and assisting graduates who need help. |
| July–September | Handle outstanding graduate issues; organize and send files; continue tracking and reporting the employment of graduates without jobs; summarize and publish initial employment rates by major; conduct a sample follow-up survey of the 2012 graduating class. |
| August–December | Process registration certificates for graduates whose files are held in custody; process certificates at the end of each month for graduates eligible to adjust dispatch arrangements, while normally making no adjustment for those who have signed an employment agreement; receive and organize files for the 2013 intake; introduce the majors and employment directions of the 2014 graduating class to employers and local personnel authorities; continue employer presentations and career-development activities. |
| October–December | Hold a special recruitment event for STU and the Cheung Kong Holdings Group and a supply-demand meeting for the 2014 graduating class; guide and organize 2014 graduates to attend talent supply-demand meetings; assist with 2014 graduate qualification review. |
| November | Collect and report to the provincial graduate employment guidance center information on graduates who secured jobs after June; launch an assistant-position employment plan if needed. |
| December | Compile graduate employment statistics as required by the provincial Education Department. |

The document asks schools, colleges and relevant units to give employment work a prominent position, keep monitoring graduate employment and complete employment planning, qualification review and data reporting according to provincial requirements.
