The seventh “Mindful Hands DIY” activity, organized by the STU Communist Youth League Committee and the STU Science and Technology Association, has opened registration. With the theme **“TRY TO DIY, MAKE A BETTER LIFE,”** the activity is open to all STU students and combines environmental awareness with hands-on practice and creative innovation.

### Schedule

The activity runs from **April 27 through May 25**, with the final held in the Science Report Hall.

| Stage | Date | Venue or note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Activity consultation points | April 27, May 3 and May 11 | Below the Second Dining Hall |
| Environmental-awareness campaign | May 4, Saturday | One part of the activity program |
| Preliminary round | May 18, Saturday | Details subject to subsequent organizer notices |
| Public voting for works | May 25 in the main notice; May 18 in the registration-form attachment | The organizer documents give different dates |
| Final | May 25, Saturday | Science Report Hall |

Registration closes at **midnight on May 6**. Both individual and team entries are accepted, with teams limited to five members. The registration form is available through the OA system; submission channels and file-naming requirements should follow the original notice.

### Competition categories

1. **Aesthetic DIY**: Use aesthetic judgment and creative thinking to move beyond conventional materials and presentation, producing distinctive works such as original 3D paper folding, self-designed clothing, photography, video, animation or decorative pieces. Materials should not be wasteful or polluting.
2. **Practical DIY**: Design and make works based on everyday needs, or modify existing objects to improve their functions and convenience. Entries should be useful and safe, and their materials should not be wasteful or polluting.
3. **Technology-creative DIY**: Modify everyday objects or make small inventions that apply knowledge in practice. Examples include modifying a cup to heat water, adding solar cells to an appliance, or using sensors and circuits to make a new electrical product. Entries must be safe, have a degree of technological content and need not be ordinary handicrafts.

### Judging and awards

At the final, participants will display their works and explain their creative concepts, methods and meaning to judges and viewers. The overall score consists of **70% judges' evaluation, 20% public voting and 10% Weibo voting**.

The judges' assessment is out of 100 and contributes 70% of the final score. For all categories, environmental awareness and creativity or innovation each account for 30%. The remaining 30% assesses aesthetics in the aesthetic category, practicality in the practical category and scientific or technological content in the technology category. On-site presentation accounts for 10%. Public voting is scored out of 100 and converted according to ranking. Weibo voting is ranked by vote count, with the highest vote-getter receiving 10 points and subsequent positions receiving progressively lower scores. Ties in the overall score are decided by a judges' vote.

The awards are:

| Award | Places | Prize |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| First prize | 1 | RMB 500 and a certificate |
| Second prize | 2 | RMB 300 and a certificate for each winner |
| Third prize | 3 | RMB 200 and a certificate for each winner |
| Most Popular Award | 1 | RMB 100 and a certificate |

Participants must prepare a presentation for the on-site display covering team members, materials and production reflections. Technology-creative entries must also explain the principles used in the work.
