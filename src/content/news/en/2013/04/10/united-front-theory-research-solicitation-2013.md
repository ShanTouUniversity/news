The 13th Guangdong University United-Front Theory Symposium is scheduled to be held at **Wuyi University in mid-to-late November 2013**. Hosted by the United Front Work Department of the Wuyi University Party Committee, the symposium will solicit research on theoretical and practical issues in united-front work in Guangdong and higher education, with a focus on implementing the guiding principles of the 18th National Party Congress and the second plenary session of the 11th Guangdong Provincial Party Committee.

### Suggested topics

The call covers the following areas:

- The distinctive development of Guangdong united-front work since reform and opening up, and the role of united-front work in the overall work of the Party under new circumstances
- The united front as a methodology of Party leadership and governance, and the feasibility of regulations on united-front work and the selection and appointment of non-Party officials
- Building mainstream united-front voices in public opinion, using the internet and emerging media, and work with online opinion leaders
- Innovative measures for implementing the relevant central and provincial documents, and the political representativeness and social influence of representative non-Party figures
- Core values of democratic parties and the development of information channels and platforms for their participation in deliberation and administration
- United-front methods for self-employed professionals, comparative studies of urban ethnic work, and the positive role of religion in building socialism with Chinese characteristics
- The standardization, institutionalization and rule-of-law development of religious administration, and united-front work among hub-type social organizations
- The ideological situation, value guidance, transformation and upgrading of non-public-sector entrepreneurs, and the cultivation and guidance of the “second generation” of private entrepreneurs
- Changes in Hong Kong–mainland relations under “one country, two systems,” Guangdong–Hong Kong integration, the distribution and characteristics of Cantonese-origin Hakka and Teochew groups in Taiwan, and united-front work among returned overseas students developing businesses in Guangdong
- Coordination between united-front departments and the Chinese People&apos;s Political Consultative Conference, grassroots united-front work, and institutional mechanisms for united-front work in higher education
- Democratic parties and non-Party intellectuals in universities, as well as other issues in university united-front work

### Paper requirements and submission

Papers should follow a correct research direction and set of principles, build on practical experience, and pursue theoretical innovation. Regular papers should be approximately **5,000 Chinese characters**; research reports written in the name of a research group and papers of substantial depth should generally not exceed **8,000 characters**.

Manuscripts should use SimSun font. The author&apos;s institution, name and postal code should appear below the title; the author&apos;s profile, including name, position and professional title, should be given in a footnote on the first page. References and notes should appear at the end. The main text should use small-four-point type, while the abstract should be no more than 300 characters and the abstract and keywords should use five-point type.

Papers must be sent to the United Front Work Department of the Shantou University Party Committee by **August 31, 2013**, with receipt confirmed. The university plans to forward the papers to the organizer for review by September 10.
