STU Library has opened trial access to EBSCO subject databases through **May 31, 2013**. The trial covers multidisciplinary research, business and economics, global infectious diseases, global public health and chemical hazards. On-campus users can access the resources through EBSCOhost.

### Trial resources

- **Academic Search Complete (ASC)**: Covers biology, engineering, social sciences, psychology, education, law, medicine, languages, humanities, public administration and the arts, with materials from 1887 onward. It provides indexes and abstracts for more than 13,000 journals, more than 9,000 full-text journals, and full-text books, reports and conference papers; many journals are peer-reviewed. Access: <http://search.ebscohost.com/login.aspx?profile=ctrial&defaultdb=a9h>.
- **Business Source Complete (BSC)**: Covers marketing, management, management information systems, production and operations management, accounting, finance and economics. Content includes journals, books, company profiles, country and industry reports, market research, case studies and SWOT analyses. Access: <http://search.ebscohost.com/login.aspx?profile=ctrial&defaultdb=bth>.
- **GIDEON, the Global Infectious Disease and Epidemiology Online Network**: Supports infectious-disease clinical care and decision-making, covering tropical diseases, epidemiology, microbiology and toxicology, with information on diseases, countries, microorganisms, antimicrobial drugs and vaccines. Access: <http://search.ebscohost.com/login.aspx?profile=gideon>.
- **Global Health**: Published by CABI, this resource covers global public-health and biomedical materials from 1973 onward, including infectious diseases, epidemiology, clinical microbiology, nutrition, food safety, occupational disease, environmental health and health economics. Access: <http://search.ebscohost.com/login.aspx?profile=pm>.
- **Chemical Hazard Information Library (CHIL)**: Provides reference information on toxic and hazardous chemicals, drawing on government documents, gray literature and other primary sources from environmental, medical, toxicological and cancer-research bodies. Access: <http://search.ebscohost.com/login.aspx?profile=chil>.

The databases are for individual teaching, research and study use. Access permissions and trial arrangements are subject to the library notice and the EBSCOhost pages.
