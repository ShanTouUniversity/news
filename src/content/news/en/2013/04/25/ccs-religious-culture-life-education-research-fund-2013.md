STU's Center for Christian Studies in the College of Liberal Arts has launched the Special Research Funding Program on Christianity, Life Education and Religious Culture. The center is also establishing an academic team for Religious Culture and Life Education Research. The program aims to build a research platform, support young scholars, advance research on religious culture, promote whole-person life education and develop research on Chaoshan culture.

The program's exchange and launch meeting was held on **March 25, 2013**. He Guanghu of the School of Philosophy at Renmin University of China and Gao Shining of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences attended the meeting and heard faculty members' research interests. The center noted that interdisciplinarity is a major feature of religious-studies research and said the program encourages projects across disciplinary boundaries.

### Eligibility and scope

The program is open to all STU faculty and staff, including educational support staff, administrative staff and retired employees. There are no restrictions on degree, field or academic rank. Interdisciplinary research and team applications are encouraged, but a team should generally have no more than three members, including the principal investigator. An application involving more than three members must explain the need.

Projects may use methods from the natural sciences, social sciences or humanities for theoretical or empirical research on:

- Christianity;
- life education; and
- other religious cultures.

The program's Q&A document clarifies that projects need not focus only on Christianity. Proposals on other religious cultures, life education and Chaoshan culture are also welcome. STU students may join a research team, but the principal investigator must be an STU faculty or staff member. Foreign teachers may apply, as may employees of the Medical College, the Li Ka Shing Shantou University Foundation, the poverty-relief office, the hospice office and other project offices listed in the notice.

### Organization and review

The program is administered by STU's Center for Christian Studies and filed with the STU Research Office. The center will organize a review committee to assess applications and score them anonymously. Selected projects will join the CCS Religious Culture and Life Education Research academic team and the special funding program, and successful applicants will receive appointments.

The academic team has three levels: research fellow, associate research fellow and assistant research fellow. The center will also invite senior scholars at STU with relevant experience to serve as specially invited research fellows. The review committee will consist of eight to ten senior scholars in relevant fields from institutions including STU, The Chinese University of Hong Kong and the academic advisory committee of the Center for Christian Studies. Review and acceptance procedures will follow STU's research-fund rules.

### Duration and outputs

Each project lasts **18 months**, beginning when the list of approved projects is formally announced. A project requiring an extension may state the need at the time of approval or submit a separate extension application after approval. The maximum extension is six months, making the longest possible project period 24 months.

After nine months, each project must submit an interim project report, interim financial report and interim outputs. The managing center and review committee will use these materials to assess progress. A completion report and final outputs are required at the end of the project.

Each project must produce at least **one academic paper of 10,000 Chinese characters** and publish it in a formal academic journal, newspaper or other academic publication, or present it at an academic conference. If the paper has not yet appeared at completion, an acceptance notice may be submitted. A conference paper must be formally presented and included in the conference proceedings; the invitation and proceedings must also be submitted at completion. Published outputs must prominently acknowledge the “STU Special Research Program on Christianity, Life Education and Religious Culture.”

The review does not require publication in a core or first-tier journal. Formal public academic journals, magazines, newspapers, conference proceedings and overseas academic publications may all qualify under the program rules.

### Funding and application

Funding is capped at **RMB 30,000 per project**. The review committee will determine the final amount based on the proposed budget and the applicant's prior outputs. Funding is approved once and paid in two installments: 50% of the approved amount within one month after the successful-project list is announced, and the remaining 50% within one month after successful completion. Overspending will not be supplemented. For a project that does not pass acceptance, the remaining installment will be paid only after the outputs are revised and accepted. Projects may also seek matching support from schools, departments, institutes and other research centers. The center may provide additional publication support for high-quality academic papers.

Funds are earmarked mainly for books and reference materials, research travel, office consumables, photocopying and research-related expenses. Page charges may be reimbursed when supported by official invoices. Funds may not be used to purchase equipment such as computers, printers or cameras. Detailed reimbursement procedures will be issued after the approved-project list is announced.

Faculty and staff who wish to apply must complete the application form, attach an academic CV and send the materials by **June 15** to:

- Professor Li Linghan: `leelinghon@stu.edu.cn`
- Dr. Chen Chen: `chenchen@stu.edu.cn`

The consultation telephone number is `8290-3655`. The application form requests the identities, ranks, departments, contact details and research histories of the principal investigator and co-researchers, as well as project design and budget information. Individual CVs, application forms and other personal application materials are not reproduced here.

### Intellectual property

Project outputs must follow academic standards, and authors are responsible for ensuring that they contain no plagiarism or other intellectual-property disputes. Authors bear responsibility for any such disputes. Intellectual property in project outputs belongs to STU, while authors retain attribution rights.
