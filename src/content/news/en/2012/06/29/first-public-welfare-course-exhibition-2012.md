It is reported that, to expand the influence of public-welfare courses among faculty and students and attract more participants to public service, the STU Public-Welfare Course Working Group will hold the university's first public-welfare course publicity exhibition on the arrival of the 2012 freshman intake. The event runs from September 2 to 16, open to faculty and students who have taken part in public-welfare courses.

Since public-welfare courses were launched in 2010 with support from the Li Ka Shing Foundation, their scale has grown steadily. By the 2012 summer semester, a total of 2,460 course places had been offered and 2,011 student enrollments had been recorded, with service activities spanning more than 50 villages, towns, communities, primary and secondary schools and social welfare institutions across Chaoshan and its surroundings. Activities include volunteer teaching, free medical consultation and repair services, medical poverty relief, rural health promotion, care for the vulnerable, environmental protection, women's development and community correction.

The exhibition features three activities: a poster design competition for which entries may cover course content and features, activity highlights, and participants' reflections, with works submitted in JPG format (about 200 dpi, 80 cm wide by 180 cm tall) to the designated email address for the working group to produce roll-up banners displayed at Golden Triangle; a photography competition themed on service scenes and touching moments captured during the courses; and a documentary competition themed on public-welfare courses, with entries burned onto CDs and delivered to the Academic Affairs Office's teaching section. All entries must be submitted by August 24, and outstanding works will receive certificates and cash awards.
