The 131st lecture in STU’s Toward Modern Mathematics series was held at 4:00 p.m. on December 24, 2012, in Classroom G-302. Qian Tao, a mathematics professor at the University of Macau, delivered a lecture titled “Adaptive Fourier Decomposition and Its Applications.”

Qian received a Ph.D. from Peking University in 1984, worked in the Chinese Academy of Sciences system, and conducted postdoctoral research and later university teaching in Australia under Alan McIntosh from 1987 to 2000. Qian’s research interests include harmonic analysis, single- and several-complex-variable analysis, Clifford analysis, and signal analysis. Qian has published more than 100 journal papers.

The lecture addressed the concept of single-component functions, adaptive single components, and adaptive Fourier decomposition. The theory draws on harmonic analysis, complex analysis, boundary-value problems, and signal analysis, with applications in theoretical mathematics and practical sciences.
