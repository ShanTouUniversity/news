STU Academic Affairs Office issued Document No. 8 of 2012, the *STU Examination-Room Conduct Rules*. The rules took effect in December 2012 and replaced the previous rules issued as STU Education Document No. 03 of 2011. They apply to undergraduate quizzes, midterm examinations, final examinations, and other examinations.

### Entry and examination conduct

- Students must arrive 10 minutes early, follow the proctor’s seating instructions, and bring their own student card for inspection, placing it in the upper-left corner of the desk. Students without the card, or whose identity does not match the card and name, may not take the examination.
- Students arriving no more than 30 minutes late may enter only with the chief proctor’s approval, and the examination time will not be extended. Arrivals more than 30 minutes late, unexcused absences, and absences without an approved deferred-examination request count as unauthorized absences and result in a zero course grade.
- Students may submit their papers and leave only after the first 30 minutes. A student who leaves before submitting the paper is treated as having submitted it.
- Unless expressly permitted for the course, textbooks, notes, reference materials, communication tools, and electronic devices must be placed in the location designated by the proctor. Students may not borrow another person’s belongings during the examination.

Students must complete the examination independently and honestly and follow proctoring instructions. Whispering, looking at another paper, copying, bringing unauthorized notes, passing notes, exchanging papers, taking an examination for another person, arranging for another person to take an examination, or using communication or electronic devices constitutes misconduct or cheating under the rules. Students must submit papers at the designated time, leave them on the desk, and depart only after the proctor has checked them. Taking an examination paper out of the room results in a zero grade for the course and may lead to disciplinary action. After submission, students must leave the room and avoid loud conversation inside or near the venue.

### Deferred examinations and order

Students unable to take an examination for a valid reason must apply for a deferred examination at their school office before the examination; the school will consolidate applications and submit them to Academic Affairs for approval. A medical deferral requires a certificate from the university hospital. Students unable to apply in advance because of force majeure should notify the academic administrator promptly and complete the application within three working days after the examination.

Students who disrupt examination-room order receive a zero course grade and disciplinary action. Academic Affairs asked teaching units to ensure that faculty and students understand and follow the rules.
