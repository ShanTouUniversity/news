STU Law School will hold the second Hong Kong Public Governance Experience salon on December 9, 2012, under the theme “Balancing Power Through Law: Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication, and Division in the Legal System and Rule of Law.” Barrister Tam Wai-chu, a Hong Kong deputy to the National People’s Congress and a member of the Hong Kong Basic Law Committee, will be the guest, with Chen Wong Sui, an honorary fellow of The Chinese University of Hong Kong, serving as guest moderator.

The salon will examine three decades of Hong Kong public policy and rule-of-law development from 1982 to 2012. Its “addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division” framework will address the expansion and improvement of legal institutions, what has been reduced or lost, the ways law and governance have been extended and integrated, and the processes of resolving or producing divisions. The dialogue will also consider the roles of legal and judicial institutions in governance, legislative principles, policy trade-offs, and the government’s position as both policymaker and subject of oversight.

Topics will include judicial review of government action, access to justice when litigation is expensive, the social costs of a rule-of-law system, the outcomes of legal reform, and the challenges Hong Kong and China face amid globalization and closer international integration.

Tam has participated in Basic Law-related work and public affairs in Hong Kong and received the Grand Bauhinia Medal. Chen has extensive experience in social service and public institutions. She is chair of the Hong Kong Deposit Protection Board and participates in public work related to education funding, law reform, and charity law.

The salon will use a dialogue format, drawing on the guests’ research and public-service experience and inviting responses from Law School faculty and students. The event is open to the university community, with registration handled by the Law School.
