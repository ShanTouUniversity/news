The STU School of Science’s Science Salon lecture series will hold a talk at 4:10 p.m. on Monday, December 17, 2012, in the School of Science Lecture Hall (Science 352). Dr. Xu Junliang, a postdoctoral researcher at The Ohio State University, will speak on “Molecular Dynamics Imaging Using Intense Laser Pulses.”

The talk will introduce laser-induced electron diffraction (LIED), a method proposed in recent years, and research using mid-infrared laser data for spatiotemporal imaging of gas-phase molecules. It will cover analysis of experimental data for N₂ and O₂: the retrieved N–N bond length for N₂ agrees with the known value to within 5%, while the retrieved O₂ bond length, about 1.12 Å, is substantially shorter than the known O–O bond length of 1.21 Å and closer to the bond length of O₂⁺.

The research team interprets the result as bond relaxation in O₂ after tunneling ionization. O₂⁺ has a vibrational period of about 17 femtoseconds, while the electron-return time for a 2,000-nanometer laser is about 5–6 femtoseconds. This may allow the two oxygen atoms to relax from their initial separation of 1.21 Å to the 1.12 Å separation of O₂⁺. The work establishes a foundation for a new approach to spatiotemporal imaging of gas-phase molecules.

Xu Junliang graduated from the University of Science and Technology of China in 2006, continued his studies in the Department of Physics at Kansas State University, and received his doctorate in 2011. He is currently conducting postdoctoral research at The Ohio State University. According to the speaker profile, he has published eight high-level research papers, including one in *Nature*, one in *Physical Review Letters*, one review in *Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics*, and three in *Physical Review A*.
