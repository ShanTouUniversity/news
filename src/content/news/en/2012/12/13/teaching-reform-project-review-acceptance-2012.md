STU Academic Affairs Office has begun reviews and final acceptance for undergraduate teaching-reform projects established in 2009 and 2011. Under the relevant project and completion notices, the office will conduct interim reviews of 47 projects established in 2011 and final acceptance of two delayed projects from 2009.

Project leaders must complete the appropriate form based on project progress and submit supporting materials, including published textbooks, copies of publicly published papers, multimedia courseware, research reports, and other outputs. The *STU Teaching-Reform Project Research Status Form* is for interim-review projects, while the *STU Teaching-Reform Project Acceptance Form* is for delayed completion projects. The attached project list covers engineering, science, law, liberal arts, art, journalism, physical education, general education, and educational administration.

### Review and acceptance arrangements

The Academic Affairs Office will organize review experts to assess the interim-review and final-acceptance materials and circulate the results. Funded projects that pass the interim review will receive their second installment of research funding. Projects that have completed their research tasks may apply directly for final acceptance. Leaders unable to undergo review on schedule must provide a written explanation; if a project leader has left the university, project members may organize the review.

| Project type | Number | Required form |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Interim review of projects established in 2011 | 47 | Teaching-Reform Project Research Status Form |
| Final acceptance of delayed projects from 2009 | 2 | Teaching-Reform Project Acceptance Form |

The forms request information on completed research and plans, papers and monographs, computer-assisted teaching, research reports, other outputs, awards, and research-fund expenditure. Leaders must submit electronic materials matching the paper materials. For book outputs, a scan of the cover and copyright page is sufficient; published papers may be downloaded from journal websites.
