The second installment of the Shantou University Library’s featured-literature series focuses on Teochew associations in Singapore. It introduces the historical background of Teochew community and regional associations and their roles in mutual aid, charity, education, and the preservation of Chinese culture.

The featured materials trace the establishment of Teochew associations in Singapore to the early nineteenth century. Early organizations helped migrants strengthen community ties, address livelihood and social needs, receive and settle new arrivals, support older and vulnerable people, establish schools, and mediate disputes. The materials also introduce early Teochew organizations including Yueh Hai Ching Temple, Wan Shih Shun, Ngee Ann Kongsi, and the Chui Huay Lim Club.

The materials note that the Teochew Poit Ip Huay Kuan was established in 1929 to connect people from the eight Teochew districts, promote solidarity and mutual aid, support charitable work, and advance education and culture. The commemorative publications of the Chaoyang, Jieyang, Chenghai, Chao’an, and Nanyang Puning associations present the development of Singapore’s Teochew associations from the perspectives of local history and community activities.

The featured publications are held in the Special Collections of the new building of the STU Library. Consult the OA system for the complete bibliographic descriptions and image materials.
