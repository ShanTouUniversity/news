The Provincial Key Laboratory of Infectious Diseases and Molecular Immunopathology at Shantou University Medical College held a seminar on molecular medicine and personalized healthcare from December 27 to 29, 2012. The event took place in the third-floor lecture hall of the Medical College Science and Technology Building in Shantou’s Jinping District. Experts from medicine, pharmacy, and life sciences discussed the connection between basic research and clinical practice.

### Event information

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Dates | December 27–29, 2012 |
| Venue | Third-floor lecture hall, Medical College Science and Technology Building, STU; No. 22 Xinling Road, Jinping District, Shantou |

| Main themes | Molecular medicine, personalized healthcare, cancer translational medicine, infection and immunity, and molecular pathology |

The December 28 program opened with the ceremony and presentations on the needs and practice of cancer translational medicine, advances in molecular medicine and personalized healthcare, nuclear-receptor drugs and personalized treatment, the relationship between basic science and clinical practice in translational research, and advances in the genetics of coronary heart disease. Further morning sessions examined BFGF antibodies and tumor-cell growth and angiogenesis, protective drugs for radiotherapy, CD4+ T-cell differentiation and vaccination, osteopontin and hepatocellular-carcinoma metastasis, and immunomodulating therapies for influenza-virus infection.

Afternoon sessions covered new pathways for the vertical transmission of HBV and HIV through germ cells, developmental toxicity associated with combined chemical exposure in electronic-waste-polluted areas, molecular imaging and anticancer-drug development, DNA damage response in esophageal squamous-cell carcinoma, the clinical translation of tumor molecular markers, molecular detection of influenza-virus drug-resistance mutations and the spread of resistant strains, cancerous immunoglobulins and CA215, and the molecular pathology of hyperuricemia and related metabolic diseases.

Speakers included Chinese Academy of Engineering academician Zhan Qimin, STU Medical College Dean Gu Jiang, Xiamen University School of Pharmaceutical Sciences Dean Zhang Xiaokun, National Institutes of Health Molecular Pathology Laboratory Director Zhuang Zhengping, and Barts and the London School of Medicine and Dentistry professor Ye Shu, along with experts from Jinan University, Sun Yat-sen University, Fudan University, and the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences.
