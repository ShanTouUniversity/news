The 2012 China University Professional Volunteer-Service Development Forum, themed “Service Learning, Grow Yourself,” will be held at STU from December 12 to 14, 2012. The forum will focus on service-learning education and professional volunteer service in higher education, promoting cooperation among universities in mainland China, Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan and providing practical reference for internationalization and reform in higher education.

The forum is organized by STU and the China Youth University for Political Sciences and hosted by the Medical Poverty-Relief Office of the STU Medical College. The Ministry of Education’s Department of International Cooperation and Exchange, including its Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan Affairs Office, will support the forum, and the Li Ka Shing Foundation will provide sponsorship. The notice said that representatives from 17 mainland universities, four Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan universities, and five NGOs had been invited.

Service learning connects volunteer service with the learning process, emphasizing knowledge, skills, and personal growth through social practice. Discussions will address a possible university service-learning exchange alliance, service-learning course development, and university public-service practices, with the aim of supporting continued exchange of experience.

### Topics and exchange

The opening ceremony and keynote presentations on December 13 morning will address the transition from volunteer service to public-service courses, university social responsibility, and student self-development. In the afternoon, a keynote on the possibility of volunteer mechanisms from the perspective of social-system reform will be followed by a roundtable on service learning and personal growth involving university public-service leaders.

Additional presentations will examine service learning and social responsibility in Hong Kong higher education, challenges and responses in service-learning courses, and campus volunteer service. The public-service exchange sessions on December 14 morning will use university roundtables to discuss sustainable mechanisms and practical experience. The forum will conclude with a summary and a visit to the public-service exhibition presented by universities and NGOs.

Speakers will include representatives from universities, research institutions, and public-service organizations, including Cai Yinghui of the STU Academic Affairs Office; Shi Bainian of China Youth University for Political Sciences; Ma Xuejia of Lingnan University; Huang Kuangzhong of Beijing Normal University–Hong Kong Baptist University United International College; Shan Jianglin of Nanchang University Fuzhou Medical College; and Yang Zhongli of Chinese Culture University.

The organizers will provide conference fees, materials, accommodation, and meals during the event. Participants will cover their own transportation. Participating organizations may also bring posters, roll-up displays, or publicity materials about service learning, public-service practice, and innovative education for the exchange exhibition.
