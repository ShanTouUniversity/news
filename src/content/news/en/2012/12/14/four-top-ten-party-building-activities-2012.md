The STU Party Committee has issued Document No. 28 of 2012, distributing an implementation opinion on studying and applying the spirit of the 18th National Congress of the Communist Party of China and further developing four “Top Ten” initiatives. The opinion was approved at the 10th meeting of the university Party Committee Standing Committee in 2012 and applies to the Medical College Party Committee, all Party general branches on the main campus, and directly affiliated Party branches.

The document calls for linking study of the 18th National Congress with the university’s central work and development of primary-level Party organizations, improving the standard of Party-building work, and building service-oriented university Party organizations. The four initiatives are:

| Initiative | Main focus |
| --- | --- |
| Top Ten Leadership Teams | Theoretical study, stronger leadership structures, and improved capacity for scientific development and university governance |
| Top Ten Model Party Committees for Personnel Selection and Appointment | Merit-based appointments and democratic, open, competitive, and selective personnel procedures |
| Top Ten Party Branches | Primary-level organization building, Party-member education and management, service, and standardized branch work |
| Top Ten Party-Building Innovation Projects | Practical, feasible, and effective innovation projects developed by Party branches |

### Main work

Party organizations and Party members among faculty and students will study the 18th National Congress report and the Party Constitution through a “two-study” program and discuss university development through special democratic-life and organizational-life meetings. The document also calls for improving the system under which Party delegates serve their terms, establishing Party-delegate offices, and having university leaders conduct research at the primary level and strengthen contact with and service to faculty, staff, and students.

For personnel work, the university will follow its trial rules for appointing management cadres and explore a “selection, probation, appointment, and placement” model. It will strengthen appointment procedures and implement democratic centralism, collective leadership, and democratic oversight.

For primary-level Party building, the university will clarify the affiliations of Party organizations and the organizational relationships of Party members, improve education, management, and services for mobile Party members, including student Party members with deferred employment, and expand training for young faculty, disciplinary leaders, academic leaders, and outstanding returnees. It will provide special Party classes for new students and strengthen development of applicants for Party membership. Each Party branch must create at least one practical Party-building innovation project after seeking views from Party members, faculty, and students.

The university will also develop demonstration bases and exemplary sites for primary-level Party building and establish annual Party-building research projects. These projects will address key challenges, current issues, and new conditions in university Party-building work and provide support for organizational development and decision-making.

### Implementation

The university will establish a leading group for the four “Top Ten” initiatives, chaired by the Party Committee secretary with the deputy secretary as deputy chair. Members will include relevant university leaders, the Medical College Party Committee, main-campus Party general branches, directly affiliated branches, and Party functional departments. Its office will be based in the Party Organization and United Front Department, with the Discipline Inspection Office, Party and Administration Office, and Party Publicity Department participating in coordination, guidance, and supervision.
