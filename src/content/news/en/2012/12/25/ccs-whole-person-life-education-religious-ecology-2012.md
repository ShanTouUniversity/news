The eighth event in Shantou University’s 2012 CCS Whole-Person Life Education public-lecture series will be held on December 28. Duan Qi, a researcher at the Institute of World Religions of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, will speak on “The Balance Disturbed and Restored: Christianity and Religious Ecology in Contemporary China.”

The lecture will examine the interaction and tensions between Christianity and traditional Chinese culture, the development of Christianity in Chinese society since the reform and opening-up period, relationships between Christianity and folk religions as well as folk Buddhism and Taoism, and possible changes in China’s religious ecology in the future.

Duan is among the earlier scholars in China to study the contextualization of Christianity in China. Duan’s publications and translations in Christian studies include *The Course of Chinese Christianity’s Contextualization*, *Western Society and the Church*, *Changes in American Religion: American Religion through Chinese Eyes*, and *The Vatican’s Difficult Choice in Troubled Times (1922–1945)*. Duan has also contributed as an author or translator to works including *Christian Studies* and *A History of Christianity*.
