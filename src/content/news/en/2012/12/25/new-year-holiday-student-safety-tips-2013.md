With the 2013 New Year holiday approaching, STU Student Affairs Office has issued safety guidance reminding students to protect themselves, their property, and their residences while traveling or remaining on campus.

### Safety while traveling

Students traveling during the holiday should follow safety guidance issued for their destinations, choose regulated tour operators, store cash and valuables securely, lock doors and windows, and tell family members, friends, or teachers where they are going. Because holiday travel brings heavier traffic, students should obey traffic rules, take care when using public transportation, refuse unlicensed or overloaded vehicles, and never drive without a license. Students visiting the countryside or relatives are encouraged to travel in groups, avoid displaying valuables in public, and guard against fraud, theft, and robbery.

The university also advises students to distinguish reliable information from false information and to remain alert to scam messages, fraudulent telephone calls, and online-shopping fraud. Students should stay calm during emergencies and seek advice from classmates or teachers when necessary.

### Residence and campus-activity safety

Students remaining on campus should follow university schedules and safety-management rules, avoid accommodating visitors from outside the university, and not allow strangers into dormitories. Dry winter conditions increase fire risks. Open flames and high-power appliances such as immersion heaters are prohibited in student dormitories. Students should not fish or swim in the university reservoir or other hazardous waters. Campus recreational activities must be conducted safely and must not disturb others. Candles and fireworks are prohibited during celebrations; students must not use fire in fire-restricted areas or carry open flames into the hills or use them for barbecues.

Students should also avoid climbing walls, railings, and fruit trees. In particular, they should not step on the sunshades on the second floor of the student residential area because of the risk of falling.
