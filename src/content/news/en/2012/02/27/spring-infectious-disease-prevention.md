On February 27, 2012, the Shantou University Campus Hospital issued a prevention guide for common spring infectious diseases, reminding faculty and students to take precautions against chickenpox, mumps, influenza, and measles.

According to the hospital, chickenpox is an acute infectious disease caused by the varicella-zoster virus with high transmissibility, spreading primarily through respiratory droplets and direct or indirect contact. Patients are contagious from several days before rash onset until all lesions have crusted over.

Mumps patients are contagious from 6 to 7 days before parotid swelling to 9 days after swelling appears, spread mainly through droplets. Influenza patients are the primary source of infection, with transmission more likely in crowded, poorly ventilated environments. Measles patients also spread the disease primarily through droplets.

The hospital recommended that students and staff maintain personal hygiene, ensure proper ventilation indoors, wash hands frequently, and avoid crowded places. Those experiencing fever or rash should seek medical attention promptly and report to their respective colleges or academic advisors.
