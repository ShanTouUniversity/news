STU’s Center for International Studies in the College of Liberal Arts will host an international symposium on “New Directions in Global Studies in the 21st Century” from November 30 to December 1. Scholars from the United Kingdom, China, the United States, and Japan will use interdisciplinary perspectives to examine people, information, finance, politics, technology, the environment, and cultural flows in globalization.

The symposium will exchange theories, research questions, and teaching experience in Global Studies, explore the possibility of establishing a Global Studies major and minor at STU, and discuss collaboration with scholars from the North American and Asia Associations of Global Studies. Venues include ACC Room 5 and CILL facilities at STU.

### Main program

| Time | Program |
| --- | --- |
| November 29, 5:30–7 p.m. | Welcome event for participants in ACC Room 5 |
| November 29, 7:30–9 p.m. | Public lecture “What Is Cultural Studies? What Does It Do? What Is It For?” in E-Lecture Hall 201 by John Storey of the University of Sunderland, UK |
| November 30, 9:30 a.m.–6 p.m. | Opening remarks and thematic talks on cultural studies and globalization, pedagogy of the nation-state and globalization, globalization and the gender division of labor, Shanghai’s 21st-century urbanization, and Web 2.0 and digital and information literacy |
| December 1, 9 a.m.–noon | CILL roundtable on developing an STU Global Studies program and a Global Studies network, followed by closing remarks |

The invited speakers include John Storey of the University of Sunderland, Jerry Harris of DeVry University in the United States, He Peiqun of Fudan University, Anna Greenspan of NYU Shanghai, and Robert Paterson of International Christian University in Japan and the Asia Association of Global Studies. Presentations, question-and-answer sessions, and roundtable discussions will support future teaching and research collaboration in Global Studies.
