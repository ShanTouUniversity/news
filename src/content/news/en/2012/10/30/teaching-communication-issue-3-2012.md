Shantou University’s Academic Affairs Office has published *Teaching Communication* 2012 Issue 3, the 13th issue overall. The issue reviews recent work in undergraduate education reform, public-service courses, innovation and entrepreneurship training and teaching administration.

### Undergraduate quality development

From October 14 to 22, EPAS accreditation expert Professor Michel Poté visited STU for interviews and discussions with the schools on restructuring the undergraduate teaching-quality system. The university appointed Professor Poté as an adviser for development of its undergraduate teaching-quality control system. The Academic Affairs Office, Institute of Higher Education and school teaching administrators exchanged views on quality-management processes, the new framework and its applicability.

The issue also reports the results of the spring 2012 classroom teaching-quality evaluation. The evaluation covered 473 instructors, 751 course offerings and 1,002 class sections, with 4,459 students taking part. The average score across the university’s general theory-course indicators was 90.84, while the average for general laboratory-course indicators was 93.50.

During the summer, STU completed a comprehensive renovation of Classrooms E201 and E202 and Lecture Classrooms E201 and E202. The first two classrooms became four multimedia seminar rooms of about 40 seats each, numbered E201-1, E201-2, E202-1 and E202-2. The lecture classrooms became two U-shaped multimedia rooms with 97 seats each. The renovated rooms passed inspection in September and entered service. Further public-classroom renovations were planned for 2013–2014.

### Public-service courses and innovation

The issue reviews STU’s participation in the first China Public-Welfare Charity Project Exchange and Exhibition and the first large public-service-course exhibition held on September 4 and 5. Since their launch in 2010, STU’s public-service courses have developed a service-learning model covering volunteer teaching, free medical services, volunteer repairs, medical assistance, rural health promotion, environmental protection and community services. The issue reports that the university had established 19 such courses and offered 2,460 course places, with service activities reaching more than 60 locations and organizations in and around Chaoshan. The first exhibition featured 16 courses and introduced their content and student experience to the 2012 freshman class.

The Academic Affairs Office also organized the 2012 Student Innovation and Entrepreneurship Training Program. The program comprised innovation training, entrepreneurship training and entrepreneurial practice. The main campus approved 72 projects, and all 72 were recommended for national-level status on August 31 under the quota allocated by the provincial education authorities. The projects were expected to complete their close-out work by the end of 2013.

At the first Guangdong Student Innovation Annual Conference, six STU innovation teams took part in project displays and academic exchange. Three projects were shown on display boards and three were presented in academic-paper sessions. The project *A Historical Study of Leprosy Villages in Chaoshan* was named the Most Popular Project. In addition, an STU School of Science student team won first prize in the undergraduate division of the sixth Guangdong Student Science and Technology Festival Biochemistry Experiment Skills Competition for its project on extracting and testing crude polysaccharides from gracilaria and making a nutritious jelly. Student names and identification numbers are not reproduced here.

### Teaching exchange and education news

The issue reports an undergraduate teaching meeting held by the Business School on October 10. The meeting analyzed course-evaluation results for 2011–2012, arranged guidance for the 2013 graduating class’s theses and discussed classroom teaching, teaching evaluation, faculty training and the development of a student-competency indicator system.

Its Education News and Distinguished Lectures sections also included a media report on Xiamen University’s proposed tree-climbing elective and Bill Gates’s 2007 speech at Tsinghua University on software development, educational opportunity and innovation cooperation with China, as reference material for teaching exchange.

*Teaching Communication* includes sections on teaching meetings, teaching research, teaching information, teaching plans, teaching exchange and education news. It invites faculty and staff to submit undergraduate teaching developments, outcomes, teaching experience and education theory or methods.
