The STU School of Science&apos;s Science Salon will hold an academic lecture on October 19, 2012. Ma Changqi, a researcher at the Institute of Suzhou Nanotech and Nano-Bionics of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, will speak on “Organic Chemistry and the Use of Solar Energy.”

The lecture will introduce the research background, development and current challenges of organic solar cells and outline industry investment in the field. Solar cells based on organic semiconductor materials offer advantages including accessible materials, simple processing and relatively low cost, giving them application potential in renewable-energy use.

Ma graduated from the Department of Chemistry at Beijing Normal University in 1998 and received a doctorate from the CAS Institute of Physical and Chemical Technology in 2003. He subsequently conducted postdoctoral and collaborative research at Heriot-Watt University in the United Kingdom and Ulm University in Germany. From 2007, he led a research group on dendritic conjugated molecules and organic thin-film photovoltaics at Ulm University&apos;s Institute of Organic Chemistry II and Advanced Functional Materials, where he led projects funded by the German Research Foundation and the German Federal Ministry of Education and Research. His long-term work focuses on the design, synthesis, characterization and application of organic optoelectronic functional materials, particularly highly conjugated oligomeric and polymeric materials for organic solar cells, organic transistors and organic electroluminescence. He has published more than 30 academic papers, which have been cited more than 700 times. He became a researcher at the CAS institute in June 2011.
