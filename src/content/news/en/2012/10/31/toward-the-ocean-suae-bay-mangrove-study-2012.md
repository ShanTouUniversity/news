Shantou University’s *Toward the Ocean*, *Exploring Marine Life* and *Introduction to Marine Biological Resources* courses will organize a field study of the Suae Bay mangrove ecosystem in Shantou on November 3, 2012. The activity will primarily use bicycles and is intended to help students understand coastal-wetland conditions and pay attention to mangrove conservation.

### Schedule

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Theme | Study of the current condition of the Suae Bay mangrove ecosystem |
| Time | Meet at 7:30 a.m. on Saturday, November 3; departure ceremony at 7:40 a.m. |
| Meeting point | STU motto monument |
| Faculty advisers | Sun Zewei, Chen Yichun, Zhang Liting, Wang Junying and Yang Mulan |
| Field site | Suae Bay Mangrove Reserve, in the Suae Bay wetland south of Nanbin Road, Shantou |

Students bringing their own bicycles will ride in formation from the meeting point and regroup at the entrance of Chaoshan Gymnasium, to the left of People’s Square. Students who need to rent bicycles will take Bus Route 17 to the terminal and meet the group at the gymnasium. Those renting bicycles should bring a student or identity card and small cash; a tandem bicycle costs RMB 20, or RMB 10 per person.

Participants should bring drinking water, lunch and protective supplies. During the ride, they should follow the leaders, avoid racing and remain with the group. Participants should protect the environment by taking their waste with them, avoiding unauthorized entry into the reserve and refraining from picking mangrove branches or leaves. They should also avoid loud noise that could disturb migratory birds. The activity will be canceled in heavy rain or other extreme weather, with a revised date to be announced later.

### Mangrove conservation

The notice’s background note describes the reserve as home to one of the larger, northernmost and relatively intact natural secondary Kandelia mangrove areas identified in Guangdong, as well as an important wetland for migratory birds. It also notes that reclamation, aquaculture, logging and pollution have reduced China’s mangrove area, while urban development along Shantou’s coast has placed additional pressure on the local ecosystem. The course team hopes the field study will encourage greater attention to coastal wetlands and their conservation.
