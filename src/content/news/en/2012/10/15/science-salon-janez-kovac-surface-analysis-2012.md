The STU School of Science&apos;s Science Salon will hold an academic lecture on October 18, 2012. Dr. Janez Kovac, head of the Laboratory for Surface and Thin Film Analyses at the Jozef Stefan Institute in Ljubljana, Slovenia, will speak on “Applications of X-Ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS), Auger Electron Spectroscopy (AES) and Time-of-Flight Secondary Ion Mass Spectrometry (TOF-SIMS) for Characterization of Surfaces and Thin Films.”

The lecture will present typical applications of XPS, AES and TOF-SIMS in the analysis of surfaces, thin films, multilayers, interfaces and nanostructures. With surface sensitivity at the nanometer scale, the techniques can be used to study surface and near-surface chemistry, depth profiles and morphology changes on graphite after ion-beam bombardment, including the formation of regular nanoscale ripples.

Kovac received his PhD in Electronic Vacuum Technologies from the University of Maribor, Slovenia, in 2000. In 1995, he participated in building some of the world&apos;s first X-ray photoelectron microscopes and later a transmission X-ray microscope for synchrotron light. He now heads the Jozef Stefan Institute laboratory. His research focuses on phenomena in thin films and multilayer structures, reactions at solid-surface interfaces and new surface and thin-film analytical techniques. He has published 120 papers in SCI-indexed journals.
