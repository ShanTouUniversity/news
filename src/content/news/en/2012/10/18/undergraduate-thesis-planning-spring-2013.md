To prepare early for undergraduate theses and graduation designs in the 2012–13 spring semester, Shantou University Academic Affairs Office has announced a preliminary schedule. Schools, departments, supervisors and students are required to follow the revised *STU Regulations on Undergraduate Thesis and Graduation Design Work* to support the quality of the projects.

Teaching units must begin the thesis and design process by week 6 of the semester, complete eligibility reviews by week 7, and finish topic development and approval by week 13. Students must select topics through the credit-based system by week 14. Supervisors must complete, sign and issue the assignment forms by week 16, and students should prepare for proposal presentations at the beginning of the following semester.

| Work stage | Deadline |
| --- | --- |
| Begin thesis and graduation-design work | By week 6 of the semester |
| Review student eligibility | By week 7 |
| Develop and approve topics | By week 13 |
| Students select topics through the credit-based system | By week 14 |
| Complete, sign and issue assignment forms | By week 16 |
| Prepare proposal presentation | Beginning of the following semester |

Students are generally eligible to take part when two conditions are met: the sum of credits already earned and credits being taken in the current semester is at least 85% of the credits required for graduation under the major&apos;s curriculum plan; and the student has completed all foundational and core courses in the major. Each major will conduct the eligibility review.

Thesis and graduation-design topics should match the educational objectives of the major, provide appropriate comprehensive training, have a suitable level of difficulty and workload, and offer theoretical or practical academic value. Supervisors normally propose topics based on individual students&apos; circumstances. Topics are discussed collectively by the department, approved by the department head and then published for student selection. A student-proposed topic requires supervisor approval and department review.

Once a topic has been reviewed and approved, it may not be changed arbitrarily. Students who genuinely need to change a topic should apply as early as possible; in general, topic changes are not allowed after week 4 of the spring semester. A change limited to wording or refinement resulting from deeper research does not require change approval, but the evolution of the topic must be explained in the preface or introduction of the thesis.

Projects from an outside organization or requiring outside laboratory facilities should be arranged locally or nearby where possible. The school or department must appoint a staff member to maintain contact and follow the progress of students working off campus. A thesis or design is generally assigned to one student. When a complex, high-workload project requires multiple students, their topics, content and directions must be differentiated so that each student has an independent task.

Supervisors must hold at least an intermediate professional title and have research experience. Schools and departments must also assign a reasonable number of students to each supervisor. The thesis and design form templates have been updated; units must use the new templates, which are also available on the Academic Affairs website.
