Shantou University&apos;s Women&apos;s Studies Center in the School of Liberal Arts will host a public lecture titled “Interpreting and Revising Village Regulations from a Gender-Equality Perspective” on October 20, 2012. Professor Li Huiying, a sociology professor and doctoral supervisor at the Party School of the CPC Central Committee, will speak. The STU Law School Department of Public Administration is a co-organizer.

Using a 2009 project jointly conducted by a gender-equality policy advocacy research group at the Party School of the CPC Central Committee and the Dengfeng municipal government, the lecture will examine common gender inequalities in rural village regulations in China, introduce practical efforts and common problems in revising them, and explain evaluation indicators and operating methods through case studies. It will also discuss possible ways to change public patriarchy and implement village committees&apos; responsibilities for gender equality under the Organic Law of Villagers&apos; Committees.

Li&apos;s research focuses on gender and public policy, gender sociology and women&apos;s participation in politics. She edited and published an early Chinese training manual on gender and public policy and has participated in gender-policy advocacy and related training programs.
