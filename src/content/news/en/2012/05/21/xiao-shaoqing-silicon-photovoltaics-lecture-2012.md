It is reported that the Science School’s Science Salon at STU will host Xiao Shaoqing, a researcher at Nanyang Technological University in Singapore, at 10:10 on May 23 in the Science School lecture hall, Room Li-352. His presentation is titled “Inductively Coupled Plasma Technology for Si-Based Photovoltaic Applications.”

The presentation will examine inductively coupled plasma (ICP) technology in amorphous and microcrystalline silicon thin-film solar cells, silicon-based heterojunction with intrinsic thin layer (HIT) solar cells, and silicon-nitride film fabrication. Research using remote ICP produced low-defect, device-grade silicon films, and microcrystalline silicon thin-film cells with conversion efficiency of 5%. HIT cells fabricated with the technology reached 16.7% conversion efficiency.

The talk will also introduce a plasma atomization deposition (PAD) system based on ICP for synthesizing silicon-nitride films. Applied to industrial crystalline-silicon diffusion solar cells, the method produced an 18.43% conversion efficiency in a five-inch p-type crystalline-silicon cell, approaching the performance of comparable cells using conventional processes. It replaces industrial silane and ammonia with a silicon target, hydrogen, and nitrogen, offering potential cost and environmental benefits.

Xiao earned both his undergraduate and doctoral degrees from Shanghai Jiao Tong University. He later conducted research on silicon solar cells and low-temperature plasma at Nanyang Technological University and has published multiple papers in applied-physics journals. Students and staff interested in the topic are welcome.
