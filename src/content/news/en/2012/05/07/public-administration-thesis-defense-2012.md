It is reported that thesis defenses for the 2008 undergraduate cohort of the Public Administration program in STU’s Law School will begin at 14:00 on Wednesday, May 16, the 12th week of the semester. Seventy-seven students have applied to defend their theses. Li Ping will chair the defense committee, with Yan Zhigang, Qin Guozhu, Hou Baojiang, and Li Jiacai as members and Wang Run as secretary.

The defenses will be organized into five groups:

| Group | Defense faculty | Students | Room |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| 1 | Li Ping, Fan Yaodeng, Chen Jiazhen | 15 | G-402 |
| 2 | Yan Zhigang, Sa Zhihui, Chen Xueyi | 15 | G-403 |
| 3 | Qin Guozhu, Tan Kanglin, Xu Ping | 17 | G-404 |
| 4 | Hou Baojiang, Chen Jian, Zhao Peihua | 14 | G-406 |
| 5 | Li Jiacai, Xu Lanlan, Yan Shenghua | 16 | G-407 |

Students must submit three copies of their thesis to the defense group. Supervisors must collect the theses, appraisal forms, and design documents and provide comments by May 11. Group chairs must distribute the theses for review and assign the primary examiner and speaking order by May 13. During the defense, each student will have up to 10 minutes to introduce the topic and significance, sources, main arguments, and innovations, followed by 15 minutes of preparation and answers to two or three questions from the primary examiner. A student’s supervisor may not serve as that student’s primary examiner, though other faculty members may ask additional questions.

Each group will recommend outstanding theses at a rate of approximately 15 percent for final approval by the defense committee. The six-part scoring system rates 90–100 as excellent, 80–89 as good, 70–79 as medium, 60–69 as passing, and 59 or below as failing. Serious plagiarism results in an automatic failing grade. All graduating students must attend, may not request leave or arrive late or leave early, and must participate throughout the defense. Students are encouraged to use PowerPoint for their presentations.
