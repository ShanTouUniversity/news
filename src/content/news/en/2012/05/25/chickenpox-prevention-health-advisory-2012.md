It is reported that STU’s Resource Management Office has published information on preventing chickenpox, an acute respiratory infectious disease caused by the varicella-zoster virus. The disease is mainly transmitted through droplets and contact and is highly contagious, with increased transmission risks in spring and in group settings such as schools.

The incubation period is generally 10 to 21 days. An infected person may be contagious from one to two days before symptoms begin until all skin lesions have dried and crusted. Common early symptoms include a mild fever, discomfort, and reduced appetite, followed by red spots and fluid-filled blisters that may spread from the torso to the rest of the body. Most cases are mild, but adults and people with chronic illness or weakened immunity face greater risks of serious complications.

The notice advises immediate isolation of infected people until all lesions have crusted. Care should include keeping the skin clean, avoiding scratching blisters, resting, drinking sufficient fluids, and eating easily digestible foods. Aspirin should not be used to reduce fever during the illness because of the associated complication risk described in the notice.

For prevention, susceptible people should avoid contact with infected individuals. The notice also describes the use of live attenuated varicella vaccine under the vaccination arrangements in effect at that time. Specific medical-care and vaccination questions should be directed to qualified medical professionals.
