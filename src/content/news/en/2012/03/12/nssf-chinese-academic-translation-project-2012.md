It is reported that applications for the 2012 Chinese Academic Translation Project of the National Social Science Fund (NSSF) have opened. The project adopts a rolling application system with two centralized reviews, in June and November. Shantou University's Research Office has briefed relevant units on funding scope, eligibility, and material submission.

## Funding Targets and Amounts

According to the Research Office, the project mainly funds the translation of outstanding published works by Chinese scholars and their publication with authoritative foreign publishers. Works written in foreign languages that are at least 60% complete but unpublished, as well as works still under research that have reached publishing intentions with authoritative foreign publishers, are also eligible.

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Funding Level | About 10,000 yuan per 100,000 Chinese characters; total length generally no more than 300,000 characters |
| Maximum Total Funding | No more than 500,000 yuan |

Funded translations are provisionally limited to eight languages: English, French, Spanish, Russian, German, Japanese, Korean, and Arabic. Eligible works include outstanding outcomes of NSSF projects, contemporary Chinese achievements in philosophy and social sciences, and outstanding achievements since the 20th century. Key funded fields cover research on Marxism and the theoretical system of socialism with Chinese characteristics, China's development path and experience, contemporary Chinese economy, politics, culture, law, and society, as well as traditional Chinese culture, philosophy, history, literature, art, religion, and folklore.

## Eligibility

The project is open to: researchers with high professional standing and bilingual writing ability in their academic field; domestic research institutions with close academic exchanges with foreign counterparts; and domestic publishing institutions with international co-publishing experience. Leaders of ongoing NSSF projects may not apply.

Researchers and research institutions may apply independently or jointly with foreign researchers or institutions; for joint applications, the domestic applicant must be designated as project leader. Domestic publishing institutions must apply independently and designate a project leader.

## Notes

According to the notice, project outcomes may take the form of a single academic monograph or a book series, and must bear the acknowledgment of NSSF funding upon publication. Outcomes must be published in foreign language, or bilingually, by authoritative foreign publishers alone or jointly with Chinese publishers, and enter mainstream foreign distribution channels. Translations should be faithful to the original while conforming to the language habits of foreign readers, and overly long texts may be appropriately condensed. Works already published abroad or funded by the "China Book International" program or the "Translation and Publication of Chinese Cultural Works" project cannot apply.

## Submission

Applicants must properly settle copyright matters before applying; publishing institutions must sign cooperation agreements with foreign academic publishers beforehand. Required materials include the application form (seven copies), the original work, translated manuscript, or sample chapters (six copies), copies of copyright-clearance documents and publishing agreements (two copies each), and CDs containing all materials (two copies). Campus applicants should submit materials to the Research Office for formal review.

Interested applicants may contact Huang Yumei or Wang Liju at the Research Office by phone at 82902563.
