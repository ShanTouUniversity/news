It is reported that the College of Science at Shantou University will host Dr. Lampros Nikolopoulos of Dublin City University, Ireland, as part of its "Science Salon" academic lecture series. He will deliver a talk titled "Two-electron angular patterns in sequential double ionization of neon" at 10:15 a.m. on Thursday, July 3, 2014, in the College of Science conference room (Sci-352).

## Research Content

In recent years, the two-electron angular correlations in sequential two-photon double ionization of noble gases by linearly polarized fields have attracted considerable theoretical and experimental attention. In his talk, Dr. Nikolopoulos will present a time-dependent density matrix approach to calculate how the alignment of the intermediate Ne⁺ states depends on pulse properties such as duration and intensity.

The research found that considerable dynamic ion alignment appears when the field duration is comparable to the precession time of about 41 fs for the spin-orbit doublet Ne⁺(2P1/2)–Ne⁺(2P3/2), and that the magnitude of this alignment grows as the field becomes stronger. This means pulse duration affects the angular patterns of ejected electrons in double ionization events — a change particularly evident for the Ne²⁺(3P) residual ion.

## About the Speaker

Dr. Nikolopoulos's research focuses on theoretical atomic and optical physics, with an emphasis on ab-initio computation of strong laser interactions with atoms and molecules. He earned his PhD in physics from the University of Crete, Greece, and has worked at the Max Planck Institute for Quantum Optics (MPQ), the University of Aarhus in Denmark, and Queen's University Belfast in the UK. Since 2009 he has been a lecturer at the School of Physical Sciences, Dublin City University, Ireland. A distinctive feature of his work is the development of high-performance computational algorithms for complex atomic and molecular structure calculations and the direct solution of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation for complex systems in strong laser fields. More recently he has focused on developing codes using general-purpose graphics processing units (GPGPU), collaborating with theory and experimental groups worldwide.
