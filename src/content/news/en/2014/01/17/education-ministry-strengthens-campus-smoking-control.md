The Ministry of Education has issued requirements on smoking control in schools, calling on educational institutions to create healthier campuses through education, supervision and long-term institutional measures.

## Smoking banned in university buildings

Under the requirements, smoking is prohibited in all buildings at higher-education institutions, and smoking rooms should not be established. Universities should display no-smoking signs and a campus supervision telephone number in prominent locations. Where appropriate, a limited number of outdoor smoking areas may be designated, provided that they meet fire-safety requirements and are away from areas where students and staff gather or regularly pass. Clear directional signs and health warnings should also be installed.

Universities with the necessary conditions may use smoke alarms, video equipment and other measures to monitor smoking, including in relatively isolated offices and laboratories. They are also encouraged to help students and staff who smoke to quit.

## Education and oversight

Education authorities and schools are expected to provide tobacco-control education through classes, lectures and Party or student activities, as well as around occasions such as World No Tobacco Day and student orientation. Local authorities should promote smoke-free-campus initiatives and establish inspection mechanisms. Schools should improve their rules, appoint tobacco-control supervisors, and incorporate related responsibilities into staff assessments and student evaluations.

The requirements call for smoking to be prohibited at all times and in all places on the premises of primary schools, secondary vocational schools and kindergartens. Schools should also improve no-smoking signage, tobacco-sales controls and guidance for visitors.
