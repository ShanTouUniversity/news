It is reported that applications for the 2014 National Social Science Fund of China (NSSFC) art projects have officially begun. The Research Office of Shantou University has recently forwarded the application arrangements to relevant campus units, requiring applicants to submit their materials through the online system within the prescribed deadlines.

## Project Categories and Eligibility

| Category | Requirements |
| -------- | ------------ |
| Key projects | Associate senior professional title or above, or a doctoral degree; must have led a completed provincial-level or ministerial-level research project in the same field |
| General projects | Associate senior title or above, or a doctoral degree; must have published three related papers in core journals in the relevant field |
| Youth projects | Applicants and team members must be no older than 35 (born after March 31, 1979); those without an associate senior title or doctorate require recommendations from two experts holding full senior titles |
| Ministry of Culture cultural and artistic science research projects | In principle, open to topics proposed by people in the cultural system and to urgent studies closely tied to national and local cultural development needs |

## Campus Application Timeline

| Milestone | Deadline |
| --------- | -------- |
| Submission of materials for formal review | Before March 17 |
| Submission of final draft | Before March 25 |

According to the notice, a project leader may apply for only one NSSFC art project per year, and leaders of ongoing national-level projects may not apply for new ones. Full-time graduate students are not eligible. For topic selection, applicants may either follow the 2014 Topic Guide or submit self-selected topics based on their research interests; both are treated equally in review procedures and funding levels.

Applicants must register on the designated system using their ID number and complete the application form. The "loose-leaf" argumentation section is limited to 4,000 characters and must not reveal the names of applicants or team members or their institutions. The Research Office warned that falsified applications will result in a three-year ban upon verification, and that contacting or bribing review experts during evaluation will lead to public criticism and project withdrawal if discovered.
