It is reported that Shantou University will hold its third Global Studies International Symposium from October 17 to 18, 2014, in Conference Room 3 of the Academic Exchange Center. Organized around the theme “Global Perspectives,” the symposium is convened by the Center for Global Studies in the College of Liberal Arts and will bring scholars and educators together to discuss approaches to global studies and ways of developing global perspectives.

## Context

Global Studies examines phenomena and issues with worldwide effects. It calls for rethinking the world beyond local, national or single-interest viewpoints, while highlighting the connections and interdependence among societies, regions and events. The symposium will address what global perspectives are, why they matter, and how faculty and students can develop them. It will also consider the educational and research approaches needed to cultivate global citizens as Global Studies programs continue to develop.

## Programme

### Morning Session, October 17

The morning session will run from 9:30 a.m. to noon. It will open with remarks from a representative of Shantou University. Patrick Mendis will then discuss “The New Type of Great Power Relations: ‘China Dream’ or ‘American Dream’,” followed by Changgang Guo’s presentation, “Protests in the 21st Century: A Global Perspective.”

### Afternoon Session, October 17

The afternoon session will run from 2:30 to 5:10 p.m. Huw Lewis-Jones will present “Antarctica: Heroic Past, Global Future.” Liwen Li will discuss developing interdisciplinary research and global perspectives at Beijing Foreign Studies University. Kang Liu will examine Global Studies and China Studies, with a focus on paradigm shifts in social research.

### Open Lecture, October 17

From 7:30 to 9:00 p.m., Huw Lewis-Jones will give an open lecture titled “From Everest to Antarctica,” also presented as “Challenges at the Peaks and Poles.”

### Morning Session, October 18

The October 18 morning session will run from 9:00 to 11:30 a.m. and will feature a roundtable on “Global Perspectives: What, Why and How.” The symposium will conclude with remarks from Terry Bodenhorn, Dean of the College of Liberal Arts.

The symposium is open to faculty members and students interested in Global Studies and global perspectives.
