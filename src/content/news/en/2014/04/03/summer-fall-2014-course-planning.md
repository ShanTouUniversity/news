It is reported that the Academic Affairs Office of Shantou University issued arrangements to all teaching units on April 3: course planning for the summer and fall semesters of academic year 2014 will proceed simultaneously, and each unit must promptly draw up course offerings (including public-welfare courses) for both terms while reporting textbook selection information for every course.

## Positioning of the Summer Term

As a formal term within the university's three-semester system, the summer term lasts four teaching weeks, and each unit's total teaching hours should be roughly 15%–20% of those in a spring or fall semester. Courses generally suited to the summer term include:

- Practical training components that require continuous sessions;
- Required public courses with heavy loads in spring/fall, such as ideological and political theory courses;
- Core (general education) or major courses that cannot meet student demand in spring and fall;
- Required courses with large numbers of students needing retakes.

Based on 45 hours of study per week — about one credit per student per week — students' summer workload should not exceed six credits. Public-welfare courses offered intensively carry 48 hours and one credit each. The fall term spans 16 weeks; students may take at most 25 credits (excluding public-welfare courses), and practice or internship courses requiring concentrated time should be scheduled in the summer term instead.

## Key Dates

| Item | Deadline |
| ---- | ---- |
| Written applications/filings for general education, new courses, bilingual (English) teaching and part-time lecturers | April 25 (Friday, week 9) |
| Course plan submission in the credit system | May 4 (Sunday, week 11) |
| Textbook information entered into the credit system and linked to courses | Before May 9 |
| Submission of summer/fall public-welfare course plans to the Academic Affairs Office | May 16 (Friday, week 12) |

Course selection for the summer and fall 2014 semesters will run simultaneously. The Office also asked programs to finalize curricula for incoming Class of 2014 students so that fall offerings reflect their needs, and to plan dual-degree and minor courses accordingly.

## Teaching Qualifications and Textbook Management

The Office stressed that course offerings must follow the revised Measures for Undergraduate Course Setup and Offering (STU Fa [2006] No. 108), with strict compliance regarding instructors' teaching qualifications. Teaching personnel outside the university's authorized staffing or employment system are treated as external part-time lecturers and may teach only after approval by the Academic Affairs Office; bilingual (all-English) courses and brand-new courses likewise require separate applications.

On textbooks, and in line with Ministry of Education directives, all course textbooks are ordered centrally by the Academic Affairs Office. When selecting foreign textbooks, units must respect intellectual property rights and may not copy or use unauthorized foreign materials. Units may obtain the CD-ROM compilation of national textbook catalogs for fall 2014 from the Office as a reference.
