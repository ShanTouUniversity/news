Shantou University recently issued Document No. 32 of 2014, setting out its graduate employment priorities for the year. The document reported a 97.15% initial employment rate and a 99.43% overall employment rate for 2013. Against the backdrop of Guangdong’s large graduate population and structural mismatches between supply and demand, the university called for higher employment quality and stronger services to help graduates find work.

## Main Priorities

The university will expand the employment market through campus recruitment fairs, employer information sessions and job-matching events, while strengthening cooperation with human-resources departments, industry associations, chambers of commerce and employers. Its units will also develop employment internship bases, draw on alumni and professional organizations, and organize career talks and employment workshops. Students will receive support for innovation and entrepreneurship competitions, training projects and business incubation.

Career education will encourage graduates to work at the grassroots level, in rural schools and in areas such as Tibet and Xinjiang where the country has pressing needs. The university will also publicize policies on grassroots employment, independent entrepreneurship and military service. Its employment information platform will be improved with online recruitment and video interviews, while job information will be updated through the employment website, email and graduate-employment text-message services.

For graduates facing employment difficulties, the university plans to establish a dynamic database and provide career advice, psychological support and job referrals. It will maintain named records and continuing services for graduates who remain unemployed after leaving campus. A broader guidance system combining career-development courses, online assessments and individual consultations will be developed, alongside more professional career-guidance teams.

The document also calls for graduate employment-quality surveys and an annual report system. Information about graduates’ career development and changing social needs will feed back into decisions on academic programs and talent-development models.

## 2014 Schedule

| Period | Main work |
| --- | --- |
| January–June | Provide routine career guidance, job-search subsidies for financially disadvantaged undergraduate graduates, an alumni-employer recruitment fair and the annual employment-services program; collect and report employment data |
| April–May | Sign employment-work target responsibility agreements, hold career-guidance activities, assist students without confirmed employment intentions, and prepare the 2014 graduate employment plan and 2015 graduate source database |
| June | Issue eligible employment awards, process employment registration certificates, complete graduation education, organize files and report pre-departure employment data |
| July–September | Resolve outstanding graduate issues, track graduates still seeking work, send files and compile and publish initial employment rates by major |
| August–December | Process registration certificates for graduates whose files are held in custody, archive 2014 freshmen files, and organize 2015 recruitment and career-development activities |
| November | Collect and report information on graduates who secured jobs after June |
| October–December | Hold a Changjiang Industries recruitment session and a 2015 graduate job-matching event, and assist with 2015 graduate qualification reviews |
| December | Compile graduate employment statistics as required by education authorities |
