Shantou University Library featured *Tracing the Origins of Overseas Chaoshan Chinese* in the ninth installment of its Chaoshan literature recommendation series. The collection brings together Pan Xingnong’s writings on the development of Chaoshan communities overseas, including the history of migration, Chaoshan communities in Singapore, cultural work among overseas Chinese in Malaya and the Young Men’s Inspirational Society. It also includes a chronology of the author’s life.

The book documents the development of Chaoshan communities in Singapore and Malaya over the past century through accounts of organizations such as the Ngee Ann Kongsi and the Singapore Teochew Eight Districts Association, as well as biographies of community leaders. The library said the work offers useful material for studying overseas Chinese history, especially the history of overseas Chaoshan communities, and for compiling local association histories.

Pan Xingnong (1904–1987) was born in Chao’an, Guangdong. He moved to Singapore for business in 1921, worked with organizations including the Ngee Ann Kongsi and the Singapore Teochew Eight Districts Association, and founded Nanyang Press, where he edited and published Chinese-language periodicals.
