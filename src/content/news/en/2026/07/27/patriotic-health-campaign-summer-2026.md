It is reported that, with high summer temperatures and frequent rain, mosquitoes have entered an active breeding season, posing a risk of transmitting mosquito-borne diseases such as dengue fever and chikungunya. To eliminate mosquito breeding sites at the source, cut off the transmission chain of the "two fevers," and safeguard the health of faculty and students staying on campus during the vacation, the Resources Management Office has issued a notice urging all on-campus faculty and students to continue the patriotic health campaign.

## Clean Living Spaces Regularly

Faculty and students staying on campus are required to conduct a thorough indoor cleaning once a week, tidying up dormitories, offices and study rooms, clearing away waste, clutter and household garbage in a timely manner, and sweeping floors, areas under desks, cabinet corners and balconies to keep the rooms clean and orderly.

## Eliminate Standing Water Hazards

During the thorough cleaning, priority should be given to "turning over basins and containers," promptly emptying water from plant trays, buckets, basins and other containers. Idle basins and buckets should be stored upside down to prevent standing water from forming mosquito breeding environments. Drainage outlets in bathrooms and balconies should be checked regularly to ensure smooth drainage.

## Practice Daily Protective Measures

Maintain window screens daily to reduce mosquitoes entering rooms. Actively cooperate with property management in the disinfection and pest control of public areas on campus, and take the initiative to participate in cleaning around green belts and corridors, working together to reduce the mosquito density on campus.

The university calls on all on-campus faculty and students to raise their awareness of hygiene, implement routine environmental cleaning, and build a strong defense line against mosquito-borne diseases in a clean and tidy living and learning environment.
