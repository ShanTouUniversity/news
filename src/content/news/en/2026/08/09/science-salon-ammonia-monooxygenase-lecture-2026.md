On the afternoon of August 9, Yang Xiaoyun, research assistant professor at the Southern University of Science and Technology (SUSTech), delivered a lecture titled "Molecular Basis of Structural and Functional Differences between Archaeal and Bacterial Ammonia Monooxygenases" at the College of Science "Science Salon" lecture series, held in Conference Room C302 of the High-Level Laboratory Building and streamed live via Tencent Meeting (Meeting ID 928-646-088).

## Lecture Highlights

Yang explained that ammonia-oxidizing microorganisms are key drivers of the global nitrogen cycle. They sustain the primary productivity of marine and terrestrial ecosystems and play a significant role in greenhouse gas emissions and ecological environment governance. Ammonia monooxygenase (AMO) is the rate-limiting enzyme with which ammonia-oxidizing microorganisms initiate nitrification and obtain energy. However, long-standing technical bottlenecks - the difficulty of pure culturing, the instability of active AMO membrane proteins, and the lack of effective genetic manipulation systems - have left the three-dimensional structure of AMO, its catalytic mechanism, and the ecological niche differentiation it mediates as core puzzles in the field for decades.

In the lecture, Yang presented her team's findings: using a self-established genetic manipulation system and membrane protein sample preparation methods together with cryo-electron microscopy, they resolved high-resolution three-dimensional structures of the functional and inactive states of bacterial and archaeal AMO in their native membrane environment, constructing for the first time a complete AMO structural system covering both archaea and bacteria. By further integrating structural biology, biochemistry, spectroscopy, and quantum mechanics, they revealed the differential mechanisms of AMO in substrate capture strategies and copper catalytic function, providing direct structural evidence at the enzymatic molecular level for the ecological niche differentiation of ammonia-oxidizing microorganisms, and laying a solid theoretical foundation for future engineering of these microorganisms and precise regulation of the nitrogen cycle.

## About the Speaker

Yang Xiaoyun is a research assistant professor at SUSTech and a recipient of the Shenzhen Pengcheng Peacock Special Appointment Plan. She received her PhD from the University of Science and Technology of China and focuses on the structure, function, and engineering of key enzymes of important environmental microorganisms by integrating biochemistry, molecular biology, and structural biology, with current research centered on the key mechanisms by which oxidizing microorganisms drive the carbon and nitrogen cycles. Her work as first or corresponding author has been published in journals including Nature Communications, Nucleic Acids Research, Communications Biology, SCIENCE CHINA Life Sciences, International Journal of Biological Sciences, and Structure, totaling more than 20 papers. She has led and participated in multiple national and provincial natural science foundation projects and Shenzhen science and technology innovation projects, and currently serves as a senior expert of the Shenzhen Analytical Testing Association, a standing member of the Shenzhen Key Laboratory of Archaea, and a reviewer for journals including Frontiers in Microbiology and Nucleic Acids Research.
