On August 8, the Residential College Headquarters issued a notice asking each residential college to make overall plans and build its own future learning center (future-featured learning area) on its existing premises. The initiative aims to deepen the university's dual-college collaborative education reform and digital transformation of education, build a campus-wide distributed future learning space system, integrate smart education scenarios into the one-stop student community, and bring learning resources down to students through ubiquitous learning.

## Positioning and Principles

Each residential college's future learning center will serve as a grassroots site of the university's distributed future learning network and will be fully integrated into the college's overall plan for general education, community education, academic atmosphere building, and interdisciplinary exchange. The centers are designed as community smart learning bases that students can use as soon as they leave their dormitories, open around the clock, AI-enabled, and integrated into daily life, closing the "last mile" of after-class course extension, self-study, peer tutoring, and innovation practice.

Construction follows four principles: integrated integration, embedding each center into the college's overall plan in coordination with mentor studios, activity rooms, and cultural spaces; distributed collaboration, connecting all sites to the university-level smart learning platform for shared resources and data; distinctive differentiation, building features tailored to each college's affiliated disciplines and student needs rather than copying a uniform template; and people-oriented, AI-enabled design, equipping centers with smart learning devices and AI services to create "teacher-student-machine" three-way collaborative learning scenarios.

## Core Functions

All centers must cover four general basic functions: 24-hour self-study, with soundproof study pods, quiet study areas, and an online reservation system, connected to the university-level AI learning platform for intelligent Q&A, error collection, and personalized learning path recommendations; group collaboration and discussion, with modular movable furniture, interactive screens, wireless projection, and telepresence equipment to support group assignments, project-based learning, reading clubs, and salons, with AI automatically recording meeting minutes and discussion points; peer tutoring, with designated tutoring stations and an academic support area where senior peer mentors provide course Q&A, subject tutoring, and pre-exam sharing sessions under the SLC mutual learning system; and general education and cultural development, with general reading bookshelves, a red-themed reading corner, and a discipline case resource corner, hosting regular general education lectures, reading sharing sessions, and humanities salons.

Differentiated expansion functions are built by each college according to its partner disciplines. Examples given in the notice include: science and engineering colleges building an AI innovation and virtual simulation area with computing terminals and VR/AR equipment; humanities and language colleges building an immersive language and creative practice area with AI voice assessment terminals and multilingual conversational agents; economics, management and art colleges building a case study and creative incubation area for business simulation, design creation, and work review; and comprehensive colleges building interdisciplinary research spaces supporting cross-disciplinary joint courses and research topics.

Each center will be open to all students of its college around the clock and available for cross-college booking, and will host faculty for small-class tutoring, extended course teaching, and AI teaching practice.

## Plan Submission

Each college's plan must cover the current site conditions, the overall integration scheme, space zoning and functions, smart and AI configuration, operation and management mechanisms, and a phased implementation plan. Construction proceeds in three phases: pilot renovation in 2026-2027, full optimization in 2027-2028, and quality improvement in 2028-2029.

Colleges must submit the "Residential College Distributed Future Learning Center (Area) Construction Plan (2026-2029)" in paper form (stamped with the college seal) and electronic form to the Residential College Management Office before September 20, 2026. Paper copies go to Room 223, Administration Center, Sangpushan Campus (Headquarters General Office), and electronic copies to o_fdy@stu.edu.cn.

## Implementation Requirements

The Headquarters requires each college to strengthen top-level coordination, with the college dean leading joint discussions with faculty, academic mentors, counselors, and student leaders, while avoiding formalistic construction that values hardware over use; to implement dual-college collaboration by bringing in course resources, discipline mentors, and laboratory resources; and to ensure long-term operation by including center activities and space usage in the annual assessment of academic atmosphere building and collaborative education, with a student feedback iteration mechanism. The Headquarters will select exemplary centers with clear design and outstanding educational outcomes as university-level demonstration sites and organize campus-wide exchange visits.
