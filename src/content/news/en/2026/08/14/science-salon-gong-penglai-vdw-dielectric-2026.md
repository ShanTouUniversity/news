## Abstract

As the feature size of two-dimensional semiconductor devices continues to shrink, conventional oxide dielectric layers, with surface dangling bonds and defect scattering, have increasingly become a bottleneck limiting carrier mobility and device energy efficiency. Van der Waals dielectric materials, with their naturally flat surfaces and no dangling bonds, can form high-quality interfaces with two-dimensional semiconductors and represent an important direction for continuing transistor scaling in the post-Moore era. Among them, "inorganic molecular crystals" that combine the flexibility of molecular crystals with the stability of inorganic materials show great application potential.

The lecture will systematically present the team's recent progress in experimental preparation and theoretical design of inorganic molecular crystal van der Waals dielectrics. Experimentally, in collaboration with experimental teams, they successfully fabricated high-quality 4-inch wafer-scale antimony trioxide (Sb2O3) van der Waals thin films using a standard thermal evaporation deposition process, and built molybdenum disulfide field-effect transistors based on this dielectric layer featuring ultrahigh on/off ratio, extremely low leakage current and near-theoretical-limit subthreshold swing. Theoretically, first-principles calculations revealed the unique "electronic decoupling" and "surface ionic effect" of few-layer Sb2O3 — its band gap shows strong thickness robustness, while the out-of-plane static dielectric constant increases significantly with layer number; calculations confirm that a 4-layer film can achieve an equivalent oxide thickness of less than 1 nanometer while meeting IRDS leakage current standards. Furthermore, to address the long-standing trade-off between high dielectric constant and ultra-wide band gap in single dielectric materials, the team proposed a rational design strategy for hybrid molecular crystals and, through high-throughput structural screening, discovered a novel composite with both dynamic and thermodynamic stability, achieving a synergistic breakthrough in high dielectric constant and wide band gap, providing new ideas for next-generation high-performance van der Waals dielectric materials and microelectronic devices.

## About the Speaker

Prof. Gong Penglai is a professor and master's supervisor at the School of Physics Science and Technology, Hebei University, engaged in computational condensed matter physics based on density functional theory (DFT), focusing on two-dimensional semiconductor devices and advanced dielectric material physics. By deeply integrating high-throughput computing and machine learning, he aims to reveal microscopic mechanisms and achieve function-oriented precise material design. He has published 25 papers as first or corresponding author in authoritative journals including Nature Communications, National Science Review, Physical Review B, Physical Review Research, Applied Physics Letters, Advanced Materials and ACS Nano. His papers have been cited more than 1,800 times in the past five years.
